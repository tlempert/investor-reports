Published figures span $11.1B to $53B — a spread wider than the whole company's market value.

The headline "$1.3–2.5 trillion" is wider than its own inputs support

Working the requirement across every combination of basic/diluted share count and the memo's own two net-asset ranges gives $1.41T to $2.26T. Neither published endpoint reproduces: the low is too low and the high too high, so the band overstates uncertainty at both ends. The direction of the claim survives — $89.80 does require a SpaceX valuation well above the $1.45T most commonly cited — but the specific band should not be quoted.

Boost subscriber counts don't roll forward

Q1 2026 closed at 7.53M; Q2 reported a decrease of ~118K and a close of 7.38M. Those cannot all be true — 7.53 − 0.118 = 7.412M, a 32,000 gap. This is an inconsistency between two sources (a trade-press Q1 report and the company's Q2 release), not an error introduced by the council. The direction — Boost is shrinking after adding 212K a year earlier — is unaffected.

Read the report with these caveats

1 · The report contradicts itself on the SpaceX reconciliation

The memo's Thesis in One Sentence and Load-Bearing Factors still assert that a constant 2.207% stake reconciles the four conflicting figures as one holding at four SpaceX valuations. That claim was withdrawn in review as a divisor tautology — V/s ≡ implied EV holds for any two numbers, so it cannot fail and proves nothing. It rests on a single media-tier source and assumes a stock split the dossier expressly forbids assuming. The correction block at the top of the report supersedes the body; the body was not rewritten. Do not quote the 2.207% figure.

2 · "Eleven of twelve cells" is nine of twelve

Stated twice in the memo, once in the Final Decision. A true statement about one grid was transposed onto another. The error runs toward the verdict the memo had already reached.

3 · The automated valuation anchors are meaningless here and were discarded

The pipeline produced a Graham floor of −$237.14 and DCF values of −$99.07 / −$98.57. These are correct outputs of a growth-DCF applied to a trailing year containing a $17.63B non-cash impairment, from a company that has since sold its principal assets. They describe a company that no longer exists. Discarding them was verified correct in review.

4 · Two subsidiaries are in Chapter 11 — the parent is a debtor in neither

DISH DBS and DISH Wireless filed a prepackaged Chapter 11 on 30 June 2026; Hughes Satellite Systems filed separately on 2–3 August 2026 after failing to repay a $1.5B maturity. Headlines reading "EchoStar went bankrupt" are wrong, but so is treating the parent's assets as cleanly ring-fenced: a §363 sale of the estate's alter-ego and veil-piercing claims against EchoStar and its insiders is in play, and its outcome is not established in either direction.

What happened to this company

EchoStar spent 2025 building a national 5G network, then abandoned it under FCC pressure and sold nearly all its spectrum. Chairman Charlie Ergen called it a forced pivot — "we would win the battle, lose the war." What remains is a holding company: cash, an unmarked stake in SpaceX, and three shrinking legacy businesses.

Governance

Charlie Ergen holds approximately 90.3% of the votes on 50.9% of the economics. There is no dividend, and a $5B buyback authorisation is blocked by bond indentures. Roughly $20B is destined for "EchoStar Capital," a division whose mandate is disclosed nowhere in the file. The synthesis concluded a minority holder is a passenger — not because of the litigation, but because no outsider can determine which legal entity owns 74% of the Pay-TV business, where $8.9B of debt went in six months, or what the $20B is for.

How this verdict was produced

Twelve independent expert analyses, one synthesis, and three adversarial red-team passes that found 19 fatal findings between them (6, then 8, then 5). The verdict held at PASS/0% through all three; the arithmetic beneath it was rebuilt twice and still ships with the named defects above.

  • Draft 1 — TOO UNCERTAIN, 0%. Broken: a $21.8B enterprise-value error omitted cash from its own bridge, inverting the memo's central refutation.
  • Draft 2 — PASS, 0%. Broken: the rewrite was judged a transcription of the red team's own required-changes list rather than an independent re-derivation. That was the editor's fault — the feedback prescribed specific numbers instead of naming defects, manufacturing agreement.
  • Draft 3 — PASS, 0%. Commissioned with defects only and no prescribed answers. It produced two findings that beat the red team: the $23B-vs-$20.25B gap is contractual, not tax (killing its own bullish point on primary evidence), and the DISH intercompany loan runs DBS→DISH Network, so cash flows to the bankruptcy estate.

The gate was stopped at three passes rather than run to zero findings: severity was declining, each rewrite spawned fresh errors, and every remaining correction pushes toward the verdict already reached. That was a judgment call, and it is disclosed rather than hidden.
