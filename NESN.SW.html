Munger's Verdict

MUNGER SYNTHESIS — Nestlé S.A. (NESN.SW), CHF, 2026-10-06

Price CHF 75.08 (pipeline quote, not cross-checked against a CHF print; it fits the NSRGY ADR at USD 89.73 only on an FX rate the dossier lacks). Every figure below is in CHF unless marked.

1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags. 4 MODERATE flags from Buffett, the Biologist, the Historian and the Psychologist. 1 MINOR from the Anthropologist. The row is the Quality band, 10x–18x. The Historian calls a Survivor Trajectory at 60% confidence and puts casualty at 25%, so the 14x cap does not apply. The flags agree on the shape of the moat. Coffee, Nespresso consumables and Purina hold up. Infant nutrition (a recall in nearly 60 countries) and waters (Perrier) are trust franchises that are now under repair. Food & Snacks has the narrower brand advantage [MEDIA per Find My Moat]. Even so, its only sourced volume figure is strong: Q1-26 organic growth (OG) of 4.2% with real internal growth (RIG) of 2.1%, against group RIG of 1.2% [SEARCH]. Private label is a threat the register marks Speculative, not a measured loss.

The pricing evidence points both ways.

- The limit (dated): in 2024, Fortune reported nine-month sales down 2.4%, and the chairman said prices went "too far". - The power (undated): a separate report gives 8.4% pricing with RIG of −0.6%. Its year is not in the excerpt, and it does not fit FY2024. Pricing of 8.4% would mean about 7.8% organic growth in a year when CHF revenue fell 1.7% (CHF 93.35B → 91.72B) and gross margin hit its four-year peak of 46.9%. So I read it as the inflation-era round. Losing 0.6 points of volume for 8.4 points of price is pricing power as much as a limit on it.

This moat bends under strain without breaking.

2. The Bull-Bear Balance

The bull cases, one line each: - Bezos: volumes, input-cost relief and exits restore cash. - Buffett: coffee, Purina and Nespresso keep volume while savings rebuild margins. - Burry: positive volumes plus restructuring restore cash without hurting brands. - Cook: faster inventory turns and cheaper inputs rebuild margin. - Jobs: Nespresso, coffee and Purina keep preference while pruning sharpens focus. - The Psychologist: trust is restored and volume turns into earnings. - Sherlock: free cash flow (FCF) returns to CHF 10.7B+, worth about CHF 80 at 5.70%. - The Futurist: four positive-RIG quarters and coffee at 7.5% produce a 7–8% total return. - The Biologist: coffee and emerging markets re-accelerate, making CHF 4.42 underlying EPS the base. - The Historian: the P&G-style prune works and the UTOP margin returns toward 17%+. - The Anthropologist: emerging-market households (RIG 2.8%) compound volume. - Lynch: underlying EPS reaches CHF 5.00 and the shares CHF 90, plus CHF 9–10 of dividends, in three years.

Lynch's case. Lynch argues that the bad numbers are mostly accounting. - The write-down is non-cash. The H1-26 write-down of CHF 1.3B is non-cash, about CHF 0.51 a share. - The P/E depends on which EPS you use. TTM EPS of CHF 2.89 gives 26.0x. FY2025 basic EPS of CHF 3.51 gives 21.4x. Underlying EPS of CHF 4.42 gives 17.0x. - The payout depends on the same choice. It is 107% on TTM EPS, 88% on basic EPS and 70% on underlying EPS. - The recall is healing. Nutrition went from −3.9% in Q1-26 [SEARCH] to +1.7% in Q2-26 [MEDIA]. - Volume is growing. RIG has been positive for four quarters, with Q2 at 1.8%.

On these points he is mostly right, though the Q2 Nutrition figure comes from the media, not the company. The bears who quote 26x are quoting a write-down.

Three things stop me following him. - The cash fell on a cash basis. Company FCF fell 14.2% to CHF 9,154m from CHF 10,666m. The UTOP margin fell 110 bps to 16.1% before any write-down. - "Underlying" leaves out costs that keep coming back. Restructuring was CHF 0.8B in H1-26 against CHF 0.4B in H1-25, and about 16,000 job cuts are announced. - His fair value needs more growth than the dossier allows. His own table gives CHF 76 at 5.70%, but only with g = 1%, which is 0.53 points above the 0.47% CHF cap. CHF-reported revenue went CHF 94.78B → 93.35B → 91.72B → 89.89B, about −1.7% a year, because currency and disposals ate organic growth of 3–4%.

His bull case is a decent stalwart payoff, and he says so himself: HOLD, not pound the table. I agree with his numbers. I do not agree that they leave a margin of safety.

The bear case. Burry's arithmetic makes it. - The dividend takes almost all the cash. Dividend cash of CHF 7.97B (CHF 3.10 × 2,572M shares) takes 87% of company FCF and leaves CHF 1.18B. - The pipeline overstated FCF by more than that surplus. Its FCF of CHF 10.99B is CHF 1.84B too high. - Leverage is real. Net debt was CHF 56.71B at 30 June 2026, and net debt/Adjusted EBITDA was 2.85x at FY2025. Net debt rose after the April dividend. - The dividend is still being raised. It went up 1.6% to CHF 3.10 while basic EPS fell 16.3%.

That is the Historian's Kraft Heinz watch item: a payout running ahead of earnings. It is not yet a fracture.

The team is new, and the recall was handled badly. The prior CEO was dismissed "with immediate effect" over an undisclosed relationship, and Chairman Bulcke is stepping down in favour of Pablo Isla. CEO Navratil (at Nestlé since 2001) and CFO Manz therefore have under a year's record, so promises against delivery cannot yet be scored. That is why the Psychologist rates them "Owners — provisional" and the Historian credits only a "new leader from the core". On the recall, Austrian authorities described a "silent" recall over Christmas. The cause was traced to a single supplier batch of arachidonic acid oil, which is a supplier-control failure in the category where trust is the product (Cook). Working capital is clean, with receivables CHF 9.42B → 8.75B and DIO 99 → 96 days. SBC is CHF 0.13B, and the gap between UTOP of CHF 14.389B and trading operating profit of CHF 12.675B is disclosed rather than hidden.

I weight the bull argument at 45% and the bear argument at 55%. The business arguments split roughly evenly. The price arguments lean bear, because no expert finds a cushion over the CHF hurdle.

The Private Buyer test. A strategic or PE buyer would pay about the current price, not much more. - No buyer exists at this size. EV is CHF 249.8B, and nobody can write that cheque. - Pieces sell at big multiples. U.S. confectionery "could be worth as much as $3 billion" on CHF 900m of sales [MEDIA per WSJ], but that is a 3%-of-U.S.-sales sliver and an analyst guess. - A whole-company control price can't be pinned down. On the EBITDA proxy of CHF 16.3B, which mixes a company profit line with a yfinance D&A line, control multiples of 13x / 15x / 17x (JUDGMENT; there are no transaction comps in the dossier) give about CHF 60 / 73 / 86 a share after net debt.

So the test can't conclude anything. The answer is the multiple I chose, and the range runs from below my ceiling to above the price. All it shows is that no outside buyer's price contradicts the zone. A minority buyer earns the 4.74% FCF yield either way.

3. Decision Logic

Ceiling (hidden value). There is no hidden value to raise the ceiling. - No cash incinerator could be sized. Bezos found none. Blue Bottle's roughly 140 stores lose money, but the losses are unsized and the sale is half-closed. - No core costs are being hidden. Burry finds no cost dumping. The CHF 1.84B gap is the pipeline's error, not Nestlé's. - The ceiling stays where it is. No hidden value passed the audit.

The stress test. The dossier's Adjusted column, anchored to CHF 10.99B, labels the zero crossing at about −25% revenue. Its own −20% row (CHF 0.02B) puts the crossing at about −20%.

Burry's rebase keeps that column's slope of CHF 0.61 of FCF lost per franc of sales (JUDGMENT, from one year's cost stickiness). I don't use it. The slope is above the 45.6% gross margin, so each lost franc of sales would cost more cash than the gross profit it carried.

I bound the slope by gross margin instead: CHF 0.456 per franc, assuming every operating cost is fixed and there is no tax shield. That is the worst case. The Futurist's 30–35% decremental (JUDGMENT, gross margin less variable selling cost) is the central case. On company FCF of CHF 9.154B, against a CHF 89.89B revenue base and CHF 1.18B left after the dividend:

At −20% revenue, FCF would be CHF 1.0–3.8B against a CHF 7.97B dividend. A 20% fall over five years means −4.36% a year, which leaves management time to respond. The business survives that. The dividend, at its current size, still does not. A single bad year of about −3% to −4% uncovers it.

Floor (moat and quality). Every floor witness confirms a good business, not a franchise. - Buffett: pricing power "with limits". - Jobs: "Sugared Water", not Insanely Great. - The Biologist: a healthy symbiont "with eroding pricing leverage". - The Anthropologist: an enduring institution, split by division.

The Quality row it is. Only 1 of 4 franchise-premium tests pass. 1. Tribunal: passes, with 0 SEVERE flags. 2. Pricing power: fails on every route. - Brand route: gross margin was 45.4% / 46.1% / 46.9% / 45.8% in FY2022–FY2025, below 50%. Operating margin was 16.5% / 16.8% / 17.0% / 15.6%, below 25%. The company's own FY2025 figures are 45.6% gross and 14.2% trading margin. - ROIC route: ROIC was 10.9–13.2% (11.5% in FY2025), with goodwill of CHF 27.6B in the denominator. - The other measures don't help. ROIC is 27.5% in key_metrics and ROE 26.2% in CARRY, both on undefined definitions. ROE runs on book value of CHF 12.76 a share (P/B 5.89x). 3. Durable demand: UNPROVEN. There is no retention or repeat-purchase data. 4. Price-led growth: UNPROVEN. There is no three-year price/volume split. The sourced splits cover only Q1-26, Q2-26 and H1-26, and the 8.4%/−0.6% episode is undated.

Which hurdle I use. The CHF cost of equity is 4.70% (CHF 10-year 0.47% plus Damodaran ERP 4.23%, country risk premium 0.00%, beta 1). On top of that sits the dossier's unsized +1.0 point for unsourced revenue geography, which takes it to 5.70%. - Why not the USD rates: the US 10-year (5.27%) and the USD cost of equity (9.50%) are the wrong currency. - Why the 5.70% end for the ceiling. The dossier calls the extra point "a placeholder cap, not evidence". My three facts are reasons for uncertainty, not for direction: - Beta is absent. That is a reason to bracket, not evidence that beta is above 1. - Emerging markets carry the volume growth (RIG 2.8% against 0.6%), but revenue geography is unsourced. - Leverage of 2.85x would act through a levered beta, which is the missing input.

The price implies a 5.23% hurdle. At that rate, with growth at the cap, the governing ceiling equals CHF 75.08. So this verdict is a JUDGMENT: until the premium is sourced, I pay only a price that clears the whole placeholder. At CHF 75.08 the stock clears it only if the true premium is 0.53 points or less. I do not know which side of that line it falls, and that is exactly the margin of safety I am asking to be paid for. - Growth: capped at 0.47%. The block's own g is 0.0% (ROE 26.2% × 0% retention). The 12%-retention reading gives about 3.1%, which is above the cap and rests on a ROE defined three ways, so I set it aside.

Is FY2025 FCF the right base? I tested it against the dossier's own series and the owner-earnings line. - The FCF series: CHF 6.55B (FY2022, an inventory-build year), CHF 9.74B (FY2023), CHF 10.666B (FY2024, company) and CHF 9.154B (FY2025, company). - The four-year average is CHF 9.03B, so FY2025 is not a trough on that reading. - FY2025 does carry peak coffee and cocoa costs, which the CEO says are reversing. The 2023–2025 average of CHF 9.85B gives a ceiling of CHF 73.6, still below the price. - The input-cost peak is charged once, in the base. The hurdle premium is for geography and beta, not input costs. - Owner earnings: the line is CHF 12.52B (OCF less D&A less SBC). That is a 6.5% "owner yield" on the CHF 193.1B market cap, and CHF 93.5 at 5.70% with capped growth. I don't use it. It treats D&A of CHF 3.633B as maintenance capex, but capex is not itemised, and the OCF-to-FCF gap is CHF 6.75B (CHF 15.9B less CHF 9.154B). That gap is nearly twice D&A, and the dossier can't say what is in it: growth capex, leases or interest.
