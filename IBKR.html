Munger's Verdict

IBKR (Interactive Brokers Group): Munger Synthesis

Silicon Council, 2026-10-03. Price $88.30. Hurdle 9.74% (US 10-year 5.28% [LIVE: ^TNX] + Damodaran total ERP 4.46% [DATA: Damodaran, January 2026], beta 1).

This is a very good business sold at a price that leaves the buyer nothing to spare. The arithmetic is simple once three defects in the pipeline are removed. The rest of this memo removes them, then does the arithmetic.

0. Basis: three defects handled before any valuation

(a) Share count. I value a Class A share on Class A earnings. The pipeline's 453.08M count and its implied ~1.70B count (276% apart) are not a contradiction. They are two different securities. [SEC] There are ~445.4M Class A shares (445,612,825 Class A-held IBG LLC interests, 26.3%), and IBG Holdings LLC holds 1,250,737,416 interests (73.7%), out of 1,696,350,241 total membership interests. At $88.30 that is $39.3B for Class A alone and $149.8B for all interests [CALC]. The $88.30 quote is the price of a Class A share, so my per-share figure is [SEC] net income available to common of $984M divided by 443.86M diluted weighted Class A shares, which gives diluted EPS of $2.22. The quarterly Class A figure ($0.69 for Q2 2026 [SEC]) is on the same basis, and §3 starts the valuation from it. I do not use total net income per interest ($4,357M / 1,696M = $2.57 [CALC]). The dossier suggests, but marks [UNVERIFIED], that Holdings' members pay tax on their share personally while the Class A slice is taxed inside the corporation (FY2025 effective rate 8.7%, $414M / $4,771M [SEC]). A tax receivable agreement pays Holdings for tax benefits [SEC]. Whatever the mechanism, the 10-K's net income to common defines the Class A claim. Per-interest earnings exceed Class A EPS by about 16%; put the other way, a Class A share earns about 14% less than an interest. The 10-K risk factors list dependence on IBG LLC distributions, the TRA payments to Holdings, and future-sale dilution of up to ~1250.7M additional shares on exchange [SEC]. When those exchangeable interests are converted, Class A share count and Class A income rise together, so Class A EPS still holds up under that overhang (165,613,780 shares have already been exchanged since 2011, fair value $2.2B [SEC]). The two bases give different multiples, 39.8x on Class A and 34.4x on all interests ($149.8B / $4.357B), but both lead to the same conclusion. The 4:1 split in June 2025 explains the FORENSIC series' 308% jump (108M to 441M) and the "368% dilution". The real figure is weighted Class A shares +5.0% over 2023-25 (419.86M, 432.45M, 440.93M [SEC]), consistent with exchanges (the dossier does not split exchanges from issuance). About 2.9M shares are potentially dilutive. There is no buyback.

(b) The pipeline's $15.7B "FCF" is not owner earnings. [CALC, pipeline] FY2025 FCF was $15.74B (TTM OCF $15.91B, FCF $15.83B), which is 3.6x total net income and 16.0x common net income. Quarterly OCF swung $4.48B, $1.61B, $3.58B, $6.22B while quarterly pre-tax income stayed at $1.1-1.5B. The rejection does not need a mechanism; the arithmetic is enough. - Pipeline FCF over 2023-25 sums to $28.92B ($4.50B + $8.68B + $15.74B). Total net income over the same years sums to $10.58B ($2,812M + $3,407M + $4,357M [SEC]). - Owner equity can only grow through retained earnings (plus issuance). The ~$18.3B of cash beyond cumulative earnings must therefore be matched by someone else's claim (a liability), not by owners' equity. FY2025 segment assets grew from $150.1B to $203.2B on the liability side of that ledger. - The firm holds only $7.71B of cash and short-term investments of its own.

Cash that cannot be found in owner balances is not owner cash, whatever the mechanism. The usual explanation is customer-segregated cash moving through a broker with $930.3B of customer equity and $182B of client cash. The dossier marks that mechanism [UNVERIFIED], and nothing above depends on it. One caution: the unexplained $26.79B of long-term financial assets is close in size to the cumulative FCF. Equity arithmetic still caps owners' share at retained earnings. Borrowings are $0.01B, so consolidated net cash is $7.70B. That is ~5% of the $149.8B all-interests cap, and Class A's 26.3% share of it ($2.0B) is likewise ~5% of the $39.3B Class A cap. The cushion is real but small.

Capex is $0.08B TTM and SBC ($0.12B) is already expensed, so GAAP net income to common is the upper bound of Class A owner earnings. Two claims rank ahead of the Class A holder, and the dossier can size neither: - regulatory capital that a broker-dealer growing balances 30%+ a year must retain under Rule 15c3-1 (retention is 87%; Cook, Burry, Jobs and the Psychologist all call full distributability optimistic); - TRA cash paid to Holdings, which is not in EPS.

Unsized is not the same as zero. Both deductions push Class A owner cash below GAAP EPS, never above it. The 10.4% owner yield, the $226.49 / $228.50 DCFs and the $50.04 Graham floor all inherit Side A, and none of them is used.

(c) ROIC of 1695% / 942% / 0% is a broken metric. No invested-capital denominator is shown, and the $203.2B of FY2025 segment assets [SEC] is mostly customer money. The only usable return figure is ROE of 24.0% [CALC], which is on a common scope, and the book value it uses ($13.10/share) has an undocumented basis. P/B of 6.74x divided by ROE is just P/E again, so it is not an independent check. The printed sustainable growth rate, g = 24.0% x 87% = 20.9%, inherits both problems and a stale payout, so I do not use it as a forecast. For revenue I use the [SEC] net figures ($4,340M / $5,185M / $6,205M, +19.7% in FY2025), not the pipeline's gross $10.2B and +9.7%.

Only partial: true for API professionals, not for retail.

The business fails the test, so there is no franchise premium.

Typical colonisation time for a regulatory-gated shift in market plumbing is long (years, not quarters), and the relief is temporary and conditional. Net view. Nothing in the record shows an invader taking IBKR hosts at scale today: accounts +35% is the observable fact. The real invasive-species question is price-setting power over the next decade, not survival.
