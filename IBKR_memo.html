The bull case and the counterargument

The case for a widening moat: accounts grew from 2.562M to 4.399M over two years with 3,182 employees1; advertising was about $96 per net new account against about $1,600 of revenue per account4; EPS rose from $1.42 to $2.221. All twelve council bull cases are growth cases; none says $88.30 is cheap on current earnings.

The counterargument is economic. Revenue is 0.82% of customer equity4, and commissions rose because volumes rose, not prices. A simplified illustration, not a forecast: a 20% fall in net revenue against a mostly fixed cost base cuts pre-tax income about 26% to $3.53B and EPS to about $1.644.

The evidence qualifies both sides. Client cash growth slowed from 35% to 27%2, a rival's transaction revenue grew 44% against IBKR commissions at 30%12, and tokenized-venue relief was granted on 17 September 202612. The council puts the professional franchise's half-life near 12 years, the interest annuity's within five to eight13.
