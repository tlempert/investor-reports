Constellation Brands (STZ) as an investment

A #1 beer franchise on a perpetual license, priced as if its owner earnings shrink forever — and a BUY that turns on one two-year assumption about a Mexican brewery.

My stance is a thin BUY, Moderate conviction: the #1 U.S. beer franchise by dollar sales trades at ~10.2x forward owner earnings while the price implies those earnings shrink slightly, forever15. The central risk is the pond, not the fish — a shrinking category and a licensed, not owned, trademark, under new Mexican capacity that still needs demand to fill it23. The paradox: the business gained volume share in 49 of 50 states through the downturn while its two flagship brands kept losing depletions4. Decision: buy 2% now, inside a $97.13–$119.67 buy zone — the price sits just below a zero-growth ceiling of $11.16 owner EPS at 9%, less a two-year, $4.33/share capex charge678.

What you would own

Beer (~91% of sales) is Modelo, Corona, Pacifico and Victoria, sold under an exclusive, perpetual U.S. license from a 2013 antitrust divestiture, guided at 37–38% operating margin29. Wine & Spirits, the rest, is a smaller tail being pruned toward "exclusively higher-end," guided at 5–6% margin10. A drinker switches for free; what you own is shelf pull through a concentrated wholesaler system — one distributor is ~25% of sales, one company carries ~70% of wine-and-spirits volume11.

Cash flow and shareholder economics

Owner yield is owner EPS ($11.16, guided cash flow less depreciation and stock comp) over price: 9.8%5. It differs from a plain FCF yield because owner earnings backs out comp paid in dilutive shares, which cash flow adds back as non-cash12. Burdened FCF is $9.00/share on the guide (7.9% yield), $10.02 trailing12. My interpretation: that gap is the whole dispute between the council's buyers and passers.

The bull case and the counterargument

Strongest bull case: the #1 dollar-share brand gained volume share in 49 of 50 states through a downturn, pricing offset a shipment decline, and Pacifico/Victoria depletions grew above 15–16%414. Counterargument: it's economic — the category is shrinking, and ~$800M of FY2027 capex, twice depreciation, funds capacity a flat guide hasn't earned the right to fill15. Example, with its disclaimer: charging that spend two years costs ~$4.33/share; forever, it drops the ceiling to ~$100–111, below today's price824 — an illustration, not a company stress test. The license also names no termination terms, required production site, or non-alcoholic scope2: if Mexican production turns out to be a license term, today's tariff and water exposure becomes structural, not a coin flip.

How I would make the decision

One sentence: at $113.63 you pay for owner earnings to shrink slightly forever, and the council believes flat-to-up is more likely — a buy, just a thin one56.

Conditions that would support buying

  • Modelo Especial/Corona Extra depletions stabilize toward flat for two quarters
  • FY2027 operating cash flow tracks its guided range
  • FY2028 capex guidance shows Veracruz spending falling toward depreciation

Conditions that would support waiting

  • The stock re-rates toward the ceiling without the above confirming
  • Owner EPS falls below ~$10.62, dropping the ceiling under the price24
  • The effective cost of equity rises above ~9.5%

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Veracruz capex never falls back toward depreciation, dropping the ceiling to $100–11124
  • The license is confirmed to require Mexican production or exclude non-alcoholic lines
  • The category's decline proves structural, and share gains stop offsetting it
