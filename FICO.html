1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 4/5 SEVERE flags raised by: Buffett, Biologist, Historian, Psychologist. 1 MODERATE flags by: Anthropologist. 0 MINOR/NONE.

Outside the tribunal, Bezos, Burry, Cook and Jobs also flag SEVERE; Sherlock, the Futurist and Lynch flag MODERATE. Eight of twelve call it severe. That is not twelve opinions on whether FICO is a good business; every one of them says it is. It is twelve readings of one fact: the profit sits in a regulated price, and the regulator has changed sides. Scores earned $1,026.2M of segment income on $1,168.6M of revenue in FY2025, an 87.8% margin (88.4% in FY2024). That is 80.6% of segment income and 111% of consolidated operating income of $924.9M, after $349.1M of unallocated corporate ($181.5M), SBC ($156.7M) and restructuring ($10.9M) costs [SEC; CALC]. 2. What is actually known

The reported business is superb. Revenue grew from $1,317M to $1,991M over FY2021–FY2025. Gross margin rose 74.7% → 78.1% → 79.4% → 79.7% → 82.2% and operating margin 38.4% → 39.4% → 42.5% → 42.7% → 46.5% (FY2021–FY2025). R&D fell from 12.9% to 9.5% of revenue and SG&A from 30.4% to 25.8%. FCF went $0.50B, $0.46B, $0.61B, $0.74B, and $0.96B TTM. ROIC was 43.7% in 2022, 47.6%, 52.8%, 61.9%, and 78.9% TTM [SEC XBRL; CALC]. In fiscal Q3 FY2026 revenue was $674.2M (+25.7%), Scores $459M (+41%, 68.1% of the quarter) and net income $237.17M. B2B scores rose 49%, "primarily attributable to a higher mortgage origination scores unit price" [SEC 8-K 2026-07-29]. The FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guide was raised from $40.45 to $42.43. Scores was 58.7% of FY2025 revenue and Software 41.3%. 92% of revenue comes from banking, and Scores volume depends on mortgage, card and auto originations.

That sentence is the whole case. The growth is price. Equifax and Hawley say the royalty went from $0.60 to $10.00 in five years, and from $4.95 to $10.00 for 2026, a step worth about $500M across the industry [MEDIA, interested parties]. The price/volume split and the mortgage share of Scores are not disclosed. FY2025 Scores growth of 27% also includes an insurance-score licence renewal of undisclosed size. Against that price sit four facts:

  • FHFA lender choice (July 2025, in the 10-K).
  • VantageScore 4.0 opened to all approved lenders on 2026-09-09, after the 2026-04-22 limited rollout. VantageScore itself, an interested party, claims it was the sole score on more than 9% of GSE mortgages from 2026-05-01 to 2026-08-31.
  • The 2026-09-28 unified LLPA grid, with "no effective date set". Rocket named VantageScore 4.0 its preferred model, and the stock opened down more than 20% the next day [MEDIA, law-firm notices].
  • FICO's own Direct License performance model at $4.95, "a 50% reduction in average per-score fees into tri-merge resellers". It was announced on 2025-10-01, so it predates the September events and is not a reaction to them.

Add TransUnion bundling VantageScore free through 2026, a DOJ probe, the Sherman Act class action (1:20-CV-02114), and Pulte's "monopoly who has ripped off Americans for decades". The 10-K says GSE use "is subject to ongoing validation and approval". The rent is contested on every side at once.

The balance sheet reads the habitat as static. Borrowings rose from $3.06B to $5.58B between 2025-09-30 and 2026-06-30, against $0.25B of cash. Net debt is 5.6x TTM FCF, 7.2x FY2025 FCF and 31.6% of market cap. FY2025 buybacks were $1.41B: 0.8M shares at about $1,760 each, 66x EPS of $26.54, and 216% of net income and 191% of FCF. FY2026 to date is $1.96B for 1.705M shares at about $1,149 (32x the April GAAP guide of $35.60). Marked at $781.07, the $3.37B spent is worth $1.95B, a $1.41B difference equal to 8.4% of market cap [SEC; MEDIA per Intellectia.AI; CALC]. The stockholders' deficit widened from $(962.7)M to $(1,745.8)M. On 2026-04-28 Lansing said "correct" when asked whether he was not worried at all, and on 2026-05-05 he questioned whether "Vantage gets any share at all". VantageScore's >9% claim followed within four months.

Two dossier corrections matter. First, the $402.5M operating income used across the council to derive "$87.8M of quarterly below-the-line cost, $351M annualised" reconciles to a 10-Q segment set ($432,476K + $62,594K) that sits under a source titled "revenue up 39% … in Q2 2026". 4. Decision Logic: Ceiling and Floor

Ceiling (hidden value). Bezos finds no cash incinerator. Software earns $247.7M at segment level, and a Scores-only shutdown raises the P/E to 37–43x rather than lowering it. Burry finds no cost dumping. His stress test is the one the dossier lacks: the raw dossier has no STRESS TEST block. A 20% Scores decline spread over five years, at a 100% decremental margin, leaves FCF of about $549M against $739M; the business survives. Burry's zero-FCF point, a 78% decline, is measured on the FY2025 Scores base ($1,168.6M, about $911M lost) at FY2025 interest. He says himself that it is "not a forecast with updated interest expense".

Restated on the base I value, it reads differently. Run-rate pre-tax income is $314.66M × 4 = $1,258.6M; Q3 is the first quarter to carry the $5.58B borrowed by 2026-06-30. Run-rate Scores revenue is $459M × 4 = $1.836B. At an 88% decremental margin, pre-tax income reaches zero after about $1,430M of lost Scores revenue, which is 78% of the run-rate base. The percentage is the same as Burry's by coincidence; the dollars are not. A 10% fall in total revenue cuts earnings about 25%.

Each 10% of run-rate Scores revenue is $184M of revenue and about $162M of operating income. At the 20.4% tax rate the base carries (§5), that is about $5.95 a share. The dossier's $6.1 uses 18.8%. Nothing is hidden, and four tribunal flags are red, so the ceiling is not raised.

Floor (moat and quality). Buffett confirms pricing power in the past and denies anti-fragility. Jobs rules "Sugared Water", the Biologist "Parasite (Vulnerable)", the Anthropologist "Cultural Utility (Functional)", and the Psychologist "Promoters". By the floor logic that is a row below Quality, and the tribunal's count fixes it at Moat Uncertainty.

Franchise premium tests.

  1. 0 SEVERE flags: fail (4 SEVERE).
  2. Pricing power in the economics, margin route: pass. Operating margins were 42.5%, 42.7% and 46.5% over FY2023–FY2025 [SEC XBRL; CALC], all above 35%.
  3. Durable demand: fail. On the recurring route, ARR is 37.5% of revenue and Scores is per-score transactional. No Scores retention series exists for the repeat route.
  4. Customers cannot switch: unproven. Neither a migration-time figure nor a price/volume split exists. VantageScore's >9% sole-score share in four months argues that switching is practical.

One of four. The band cannot govern.

The base. Status-quo owner EPS is Q3 pre-tax income of $314.66M × 4 = $1,258.6M, taxed at 20.4% (DERIVED: the average of the filed FY2023–FY2025 rates of 22.4%, 20.1% and 18.8%) and divided over 21.6M shares: $46.36. Why the run-rate rather than TTM:

  • TTM ($37.50) mixes regimes. It contains a quarter before the 2026 price step, and that quarter carried less debt.
  • TTM less the new interest (the Biologist's ~$35) removes interest a second time. Q3 pre-tax already carries the June debt.
  • The mechanisms below are defined against today's $10 price. Applied to a base that holds only part of the step, they would count its reversal twice.

I vary the base down to $44.92, the H2 average implied by the company's $652M Q4 guide.

Bounding the mortgage pool. The mortgage share of Scores (m) is undisclosed, and Lynch is right that it decides the bear. The dossier does constrain it.

6. TOO UNCERTAIN tripwires

  1. Three or more SEVERE flags: true.
  2. A load-bearing regulatory variable: true. It is the effective date and terms of the LLPA grid and bi-merge.
  3. Two or more PASS votes citing a variable that can't be calculated: not met. The test is the reason each PASS gives:
  4. Bezos and the Biologist: owner yield against the hurdle.
  5. Cook: needs post-policy evidence.
  6. Jobs: no proof of customer love.
  7. The Psychologist: management's calibration.
  8. Buffett: "the dossier cannot quantify that endpoint". He is the only one who cites an unquantifiable endpoint.
  9. Writing "depends on the unknowable": not met, narrowly. "The bet at $781 is on the regime" describes today's price, and the no-buy decision at $781 holds across the whole range in §5. The buy zone's ceiling is set to hold across m = 40–75%. Only the floor depends on m, and I print its span.
  10. Not met: the ceiling is 12% below my own central value.

Two tripwires are true, so the default is TOO UNCERTAIN, and I have to argue out of it. The argument rests on a bound I can compute, not on a slope.

  • The regime can only reach the mortgage pool. Card, auto and the rest of Scores already run under lender choice, and FICO is "used in most U.S. credit decisions" [SEC].
  • The pool is capped. It is 58% of Scores if the reported 2x price step explains all of the $133.5M year-on-year gain. Above 75% it needs a realized mortgage gain of 63% or less against a doubled list price.
  • At that cap, the worst mechanism set stays profitable. Price halved, bi-merge and 25% VantageScore together remove $1,033M of revenue. Pre-tax income reaches zero only at $1,430M, so EPS is about $12.9 at run-rate debt cost.
  • Zeroing earnings takes the whole mortgage pool at m ≥ 75%. That means FICO off every GSE loan. The multiyear dual-delivery transition Experian describes [MEDIA] points the other way.

I do not count "90% of lenders" as support. It is a current reach figure from FICO's critics [MEDIA per Hawley; Pulte-FHFA article], not a forecast. The Biologist's 10-year view is "a dual-score market, a lower and regulated per-score price, and a Software business growing in the low single digits plus Platform". That is the end-state my central case models, not a floor under it. The equity's loss is therefore bounded short of zero. The ceiling ($500) clears the hurdle on the central mechanisms anywhere in that range of m.

What the bound does not do is make the floor certain. The bear is worth $173–383 across m, and a buyer at $500 who gets the bear at m = 58% loses about 45%. That risk is why the position is capped at 2%. The case is WAIT, not the Too Hard pile, because the loss can be priced. If the next filing shows m above 75%, or a realized mortgage price already near $4.95, the bound fails and so does the escape.

Host population health. The dossier has no mortgage-origination volume series and does not disclose the mortgage share of Scores revenue (an explicit unknown). I therefore cannot say whether the host is healthy or whether FICO is simply charging more of a flat or shrinking volume. Banking-industry contraction is a named risk [SEC 10-K].

Consolidation: it cuts against FICO. The three bureaus form a concentrated host layer. Two of them, Equifax and TransUnion, co-own VantageScore [MEDIA per VantageScore release, an interested party]. That means two of the three channels that deliver FICO's product also own the main substitute. This is a host that carries the parasite and also breeds the antibody. TransUnion offers VantageScore 4.0 at no additional charge through 2026 to mortgage customers who buy a FICO score [MEDIA per NMP, threat 6]. Equifax issued the public attack on FICO's pricing. Rent extraction rate. Cannot be computed as a share of host economics. The dossier has no lender revenue per loan. HISTORIAN OF DISRUPTION: FICO (analysis date 2026-10-20)

Source note. The dossier says "Analogues: none in the dossier." Every historical analogue below is from my own historical knowledge, tagged [HIST], with figures rounded and hedged. None of it is dossier data, and none is SEC-filed for FICO. All FICO figures carry the dossier's tags.

Hurdle used throughout: LOCAL COST OF EQUITY 9.67-10.67% (USD 10-year 5.14% [LIVE] + total ERP 4.46% [DATA: Damodaran] + 0.07% revenue-weighted CRP = 9.67%; up to +1.0% [JUDGMENT: leverage, net debt $5.33B = 31.6% of market cap]). I use 10.17% as the midpoint. Perpetual growth is capped at g <= 5.14% [LIVE]. I add no regime premium to the discount rate; I put it in the cash flows.

0. What kind of disruption is this?

This is not a Kodak-style technology disruption. FICO's product still works, and about 90% of lenders still use it [MEDIA per Hawley]. The threat is regulator-enabled substitution of a gatekeeper standard, aimed at the price of the standard. The historical question is therefore "what happened to toll-takers when the referee opened the road?" The question is not "what happened to firms whose product became obsolete?"

Two things can happen to such a firm. The standard survives and the rent does not. Or the rent was the whole business and the firm dies. The dossier's evidence is mostly about the rent: - Q3 FY2026 B2B scores revenue +49%, "primarily attributable to a higher mortgage origination scores unit price" [SEC 8-K 2026-07-29 Ex.99.1]. - The royalty went $0.60 to $10.00 in five years, and $4.95 to $10.00 in 2026 [MEDIA per Equifax/Hawley]. - Scores are 80.6% of FY2025 segment operating income [CALC], at an 87.8% segment margin [SEC].

The earnings at risk are price-led, not volume-led, and that is why the analogue set below is built around pricing rent.

1. DEAD ANALOGUES (the casualties)

1a. NYC taxi medallions (peak ~2013-14, collapse through ~2018). [HIST] - The moat was regulatory scarcity: a fixed number of licences, so a monopoly on street hail. - Roughly $1M+ at the peak, then down roughly 80-90% once Uber and Lyft arrived and regulators declined to defend scarcity. Treat these as approximate. - The decline took about 4-5 years, and prices fell faster than volumes did. - Management and owners said the licence was the safest asset in the city. The rent had been capitalised into the asset price, as FICO's rent was into a $1,530 stock (2026-01-28 [MEDIA]). - Match to FICO: a moat that was a regulator-granted position (the dossier's own MOAT TYPES list "regulatory_capture"). Once FHFA moved to lender choice in July 2025 [SEC 10-K], the position stopped being protected. The 2026-09-28 unified-grid action removes the pricing disadvantage of the competing score [MEDIA]. Mismatch: FICO carries real network effects and is embedded in credit models, which a taxi medallion never was.

1b. R.H. Donnelley / Dex (Yellow Pages), peak ~2007, Chapter 11 in 2009. [HIST] - This is the leverage lesson. A franchise with 90%+ margins and a decade of annual price increases to captive customers was bought with debt (Dex acquisition ~2006 at roughly $10B, hedged), on the belief that the toll was permanent. - Once usage migrated, the leverage turned a decline into a bankruptcy in about two years. - Match to FICO: price increases to a customer base that resents them (Hawley, Equifax, Pulte calling FICO a "monopoly who has ripped off Americans" [MEDIA]), plus borrowings of $5.58B at 2026-06-30 vs $3.06B at 2025-09-30 [CALC from SEC/BALANCE SHEET]. Mismatch: FICO's debt is ~5.6x TTM FCF (net debt $5.33B / $0.96B [CALC]), nowhere near the Dex multiple of EBITDA, and FICO's business is still growing.

1c. Kodak (film share ~two-thirds to 90% in the US through the 1990s; bankruptcy 2012). [HIST] - Kodak invented the digital camera in 1975 and shelved it to protect film margins. - The decline took about 12 years, slow at first and then sudden. - Match to FICO: optimising extraction over adaptation. R&D fell from 12.9% to 9.5% of revenue and operating margin rose from 38.4% to 46.5%, FY2021 to FY2025 [CALC from SEC XBRL]. Mismatch: FICO has no Kodak-like technology gap. VantageScore 4.0 is not a technically superior product; it is a cheaper, regulator-permitted one.

Common casualty pattern: the price rises ran ahead of the customer's ability to switch. When a referee created a switching option, the price fell before the volume did. In all three cases the stock priced in the rent as permanent.

2. SURVIVOR ANALOGUES (the adapters)

2a. Visa/Mastercard after the DOJ anti-steering settlement (2010) and Durbin (2011). [HIST] - A regulator capped part of the rent (US debit interchange) and opened routing choice. - The networks lost take-rate in one product line, but the standard survived because it was the thing every merchant and issuer needed. - They shifted the franchise toward volume (cash-to-card migration), cross-border and value-added services. - Lesson for FICO: survivors conceded price while the standard was still universal, and they did it early. FICO: Cultural Anthropologist Analysis

Analysis date 2026-10-20. Price $781.07, market cap $16.87B [CALC pipeline]. Data vintage: last reported quarter ended 2026-06-30. Every FHFA event of September 2026 falls after it, so none of it is in any TTM figure.

0. The one-paragraph reading

FICO has two cultures bolted together. The first is the consumer culture: "what's your FICO?" is a 300-850 number that has organised American credit life since 1989 [SEC]. The second is the lender culture, where FICO is a mandated toll. The first culture is genuine but pays nothing. The second pays everything (Scores was 58.7% of FY2025 revenue and 68.1% of fiscal Q3 FY2026 revenue; 80.6% of segment operating income [SEC/CALC]) and it is the one the regulator is now dismantling. I see a brand that is a cultural verb with a payer that is a prisoner, and the prisoners have started to leave.

1. Cultural role

Ritual or utility or status symbol? To the borrower the score is a ritual and identity marker: you check it, you protect it, you are told it by a lender. The dossier's product line confirms the ambition: FICO Score 10, 10 T, Score 10 BNPL, UltraFICO, Score XD, myFICO direct-to-consumer [SEC]. But there is no engagement data in the dossier (no myFICO users, no NPS, no churn except DBNRR), so I can state the cultural weight only qualitatively. [INSUFFICIENT DATA for any quantified claim about consumer attachment.]

Cultural verb test. "FICO score" is used generically for a credit score. That is a real asset, but it is a consumer asset. The payer is the lender, and FICO "in most cases does not sell scores directly to lenders" [SEC]; revenue flows through the three bureaus. The cultural verb therefore does not sit on the invoice. A brand that is a verb among people who do not choose it, and a line item among people who do, is a weak moat the moment the chooser is given a free choice.

Embedded in a cultural moment? Yes, the largest one: buying a home. The GSE mortgage is the single most culturally loaded financial transaction in the dossier, and it is exactly where the lender-choice rule (July 2025 [SEC 10-K Item 1A]) and the unified LLPA grid (2026-09-28 [MEDIA]) land. Being embedded in the home-buying moment helped FICO when the mandate was single-score.
