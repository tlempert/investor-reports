Dassault Systèmes as an investment

An excellent engineering-software franchise whose current price leaves no margin for disappointment.

I would WAIT, with moderate conviction: Dassault is durable, but its central value of €21.16 barely exceeds the €20.98 price, while the €19.04 ceiling demands a discount for measurement risk34. The central risk is that slowing growth meets multiple compression before the platform mix improves. The paradox is a deeply embedded franchise exposed at its reseller, developer and lower-market edges. The council voted 1 BUY, 1 WAIT, 3 HOLD, 6 PASS and 1 SELL; trigger prices ranged from €12.80 to €20.98, with most near €18–1913. My decision is therefore a 0% position today, reconsidered inside the €17.30–€19.04 buy zone or after stronger organic evidence4.

What you would own

You would own CATIA and SOLIDWORKS design tools, DELMIA manufacturing software, the 3DEXPERIENCE data platform and Medidata life-sciences software, with ArisGlobal pending5. These businesses are not separately reported with revenue or profit. The moat is experienced as the cost and operational risk of moving years of engineering data, processes and integrations: 390,000 customers use the platform, and 83% of software revenue recurs5.

The bull case and the counterargument

The strongest case is that workflow lock-in lets 3DEXPERIENCE and cloud expand within the installed base: in Q1 2026 they grew 7% and 8%, against 3% for total software1. The council weighted the bull case 45% and the bear case 55%10.

The counterargument is economic: the faster products’ euro base is undisclosed, while headline growth remains slow. If 3DEXPERIENCE represented 30% of software, 7% growth there alongside 3% total growth would imply roughly 1.3% growth elsewhere; even at a 50% mix, the blend would be only about 4%7. This is an illustration, not a disclosed product mix.

Primary evidence supports both sides: FY2025 3DEXPERIENCE growth was 10% and cloud growth was 8%, versus Q1 2026 rates of 7% and 8%; the mix shift endured, but absolute platform growth slowed7. The moat could weaken through cloud-native alternatives, developer backlash over a reported 10% royalty or $100-per-unit fee, or a reseller remedy that becomes precedent outside Turkey6.

How I would make the decision

I would buy the franchise only when either price supplies the missing protection or operating evidence strengthens the growth case.

Conditions that would support buying

  • Product revenue bases are disclosed and platform growth is broad-based.
  • Organic guidance improves without acquisition contribution.
  • Receivables stabilize and management explains the collection step.
  • Partner economics improve without weakening customer lock-in.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Guidance is merely reaffirmed.
  • Platform products keep outgrowing the whole but continue slowing.
  • The Turkish matter remains confined and unresolved.
  • ArisGlobal closes without evidence of returns.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • A major industrial customer defects.
  • Platform growth converges with total software growth.
  • A large jurisdiction forces the reseller model open.
  • Cash conversion deteriorates across a full reporting cycle.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Dassault spent €0.34B–€0.64B annually on buybacks while stock compensation ran €0.17B–€0.19B, yet the share count rose 0.5%1212. The council therefore required repurchases to reduce per-share claims, not merely exceed stock compensation in euros. Consistency matters: unverifiable implied repurchase prices cannot establish allocation skill.
