Microsoft as an investment

A toll bridge everyone agrees is wonderful, priced for a decade of growth a five-year lens cannot confirm.

I would WAIT, with moderate conviction. Returns on capital stayed above 26% through the heaviest capex cycle in the company's history1. The risk is what the price assumes about AI infrastructure paying for itself: switching costs and recurring cloud revenue are the strongest evidence here, while the $72.5B a year behind them has no audited return anywhere in the dossier1. Decision: 0% position at $516.17, against a $432.79 central value, a $308 ceiling and a $257 floor1.

What you would own

Three segments: Productivity & Business Processes (Microsoft 365, Office, LinkedIn), 60% operating margin on $140.0B revenue; Intelligent Cloud (Azure, Dynamics), 41% margin on $137.8B; More Personal Computing (Windows, Xbox, Search), 27% margin on $54.1B2. The moat as a reader would feel it: moving 1,000 users off Microsoft 365 costs $1–5M and takes 12–24 months3.

Cash flow and shareholder economics

Owner earnings (operating cash flow less maintenance capex and stock compensation) were $127.08B TTM, a 3.3% yield at $516.17; free cash flow was $66.99B, a 1.75% yield, depressed by $115.95B of TTM capex4. Owner earnings treat the AI buildout as growth spending; free cash flow charges all of it. The dividend is $3.92/share, 0.76% yield, about one-thirteenth of the 9.64% cost of equity5.

The latest quarter

Q4 FY2026: revenue $90.0B (+18%), operating income $40.6B (+18%), GAAP net income $35.8B (+31%), GAAP diluted EPS $4.81 (+32%); Azure passed $100B revenue at +41%, Copilot passed 30 million paid seats6. My interpretation: strong on every reported line, but silent on whether the seats or the backlog convert to durable per-customer economics.

The bull case and the counterargument

The strongest bull case: Microsoft 365 commercial cloud revenue grew 17% on 6% seat growth, evidence of price/mix strength, while commercial RPO grew 84% to $678B — 2.04x revenue — showing multi-year committed spend, not month-to-month renewal7. Losing OpenAI model exclusivity also bought extended IP rights to 2032 and revenue share through 2030, independent of OpenAI's own progress8.

The counterargument is economic: growth capex of about $72.5B a year must add roughly $7.0B of after-tax earnings just to earn the 9.64% cost of equity, and $20.2B to hold the current 27.9% return on capital9. The price makes the same point directly: solving owner EPS $17.11 × (1 − g/27.9%) ÷ (9.64% − g) = $516.17 gives required perpetual growth of about 7.18%, roughly two points above the 5.18% Treasury used as a rough growth ceiling10. Disclaimer: this restates the price; it is not independent proof of overvaluation.

The backlog may be less diversified than it looks: a media estimate puts roughly $281B of a differently-dated $625B Azure RPO figure with OpenAI; if so the rest grew only about 8%11. That is a bounding case, not a finding, but it means the metric most of the council cites may be mostly one counterparty. The moat could weaken from the EU's ten-year bundling remedy, a widened FTC probe, and the DMA gatekeeper designation for Azure, all within the same year1.

How I would make the decision

Own the enterprise franchise, decline the AI-capex bet at this price, revisit when the multiple — not the dollar price — says otherwise.

Conditions that would support buying

  • The trailing owner-EPS multiple falls to 18x or below (about $308 today, rising with owner EPS).
  • A disclosed figure shows OpenAI below roughly 25% of commercial RPO, with the rest still growing.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Capex keeps outpacing revenue growth with no new disclosure of returns on it.
  • Recurring-revenue share stays undisclosed above 65%, short of the 75% franchise threshold.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Commercial RPO growth reverses, showing the backlog was mostly one counterparty's spend running off.
  • Antitrust remedies materially narrow Azure or Microsoft 365 bundling beyond what is already priced.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Using SEC-filed totals, buybacks implied roughly $419/share (FY2025), $375 (FY2024) and $267 (FY2023)15. Share count fell only from 7,464M to 7,427M — buybacks mostly offset stock-compensation dilution, not opportunistic float-shrinking. Disciplined, but not itself a valuation signal.

Price discipline without false precision

The buy zone is $257 to $308, 15x–18x trailing owner EPS of $17.11. The floor is where even a skeptic should act; the ceiling is where the quality band and the margin-of-safety rule agree to cap the price. Both move as owner EPS is remeasured, and the band choice (quality vs. franchise) changes the ceiling by only about $1713.

Final investment view

Verdict: WAIT — 0% position. A high-quality compounder with a real moat, but the record did not pass every test — Jobs's simplicity test and the fourth franchise criterion both failed17. The price asks growth to outlast a five-year view; at $257–$308 it would not need to.
