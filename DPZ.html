Munger's Verdict

MUNGER SYNTHESIS — DPZ (Domino's Pizza, Inc.) — $297.62 — 2026-10-04

1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags. 2 MODERATE flags, from Buffett and the Psychologist. 3 MINOR, from the Biologist, the Historian and the Anthropologist. Buffett's MODERATE flag is about pricing power, not the existence of a moat: orders grew "meaningfully" in Q2 while U.S. same-store sales rose 0.1% and international fell 0.1% [SEC]. If that order count is a same-store count, ticket or mix went negative. The dossier does not say whether it is same-store or system-wide; a system-wide count that includes ~3.6% unit growth would be compatible with flat ticket. The Psychologist's flag comes from a thin evidentiary record: no transcript Q&A, no order-count percentage and no cohort data. Management chose order count as its headline metric ("the most important driver of long-term success") and reported same-store sales alongside it. Neither says the moat is breaking. The Historian calls this a Survivor Trajectory at 60% confidence, so the casualty cap (a 14x ceiling) does not apply. The Biologist rates the system a Symbiont (healthy) with 92% ten-year survival, and the Anthropologist calls it a Cultural Utility. Under the rules this is the normal Quality band. Leverage is charged once, in the cost of equity, as set out in §4–§5.

2. What the council actually disagrees about

The tally is 6 PASS (Bezos, Buffett, Burry, Cook, Jobs, Psychologist), 5 WAIT (Sherlock, Futurist, Biologist, Historian, Anthropologist) and 1 HOLD (Lynch). It reads as bearish. It is not. Every one of the six PASS reports names a re-entry price, and every one of them is a verdict on price. Buffett says "a good franchise at a price that requires growth." Burry "would pass at the current price rather than claim the structural break has already occurred." Cook grades operations B. Under our vocabulary, PASS means the business fails the quality bar at any price. None of these six says that. On substance, all twelve are saying the same thing, Lynch included ("a decent business at a fair price"): a good business at a full price.

The Jev independence audit shows how little the trigger cluster proves. All 12 experts used a scenario grid or DCF, and 8 of 12 used one key metric: earnings yield against the hurdle. Five of the PASS triggers (Bezos $240–255, Buffett, Burry and Jobs $240–245, Cook $235–245) come from one calculation: the corrected pipeline DCF of ~$303 less about 20%. That is one witness, not five. The rest of the room does not add independent witnesses either: - Sherlock's, the Biologist's and the Historian's Gordon grids discount the same TTM owner earnings at the same ~10% cost of equity. That is the same DCF with a single growth stage. - Lynch's and the Futurist's return grids reduce to the same identity: yield plus growth, compared with r.

The council has run one valuation many times, with different g paths. Those paths do not agree. The Futurist's weighted ~6.7% a year sits three points below the hurdle and supports $235–245, while Lynch's ~9.1% supports $265–290. A genuinely independent cross-check would be asset-based or transaction-based. The dossier has none: Section M (peers) is empty, and the Pizza Hut sale carries no multiple. So the memo rests on one yield-plus-growth calculation and says so. At $297.62 the owner yield is 6.5% against a 9.7–10.2% local cost of equity, so the price needs ~3.3% perpetual growth at 10%. The question is whether ~3.0–3.2% (§4) or more is the right g.

3. Bull–Bear Balance and the Private Buyer test

Bull cases, as each expert wrote them: - Bezos: more orders lift supply-chain volume and franchisee profit. - Buffett: order growth becomes sales growth without more leverage. - Burry: openings stay profitable. - Cook: volume expands supply-chain margins. - Jobs: orders become durable franchisee profit. - Psychologist: growth beats the 4.3% DCF input. - Sherlock: comps back to +3.0% compound owner earnings well above the 3.3% the price implies. - Futurist: ~3.6% unit growth plus a 1.6–1.9% share shrink gives 12%+. - Biologist: a share gainer trading 19% below the H1 repurchase price. - Historian: a repeat of the 2009–2010 turnaround. - Anthropologist: 14%+ per-share compounding. - Lynch: comps at 3% and a 20x re-rating give ~$457 by 2029.

Lynch, addressed rather than dismissed. He is right on four points: - Every segment is profitable. U.S. $575.3M, Supply Chain $320.1M, International $288.5M [SEC], or 48.6%, 27.0% and 24.4% of segment income [CALC]. Excluding the $559.5M of advertising passed through at cost, the total segment margin is 27.0%. The revenue base is $677.1M of U.S. royalties and fees, $338.7M international, $2,989.5M supply chain and $375.2M from company-owned stores. - Share is rising against failing rivals. 23.3% vs 22.5% of U.S. QSR pizza [SEC, preliminary Circana]. Pizza Hut's systemwide sales fell ~8% and it sold for $2.7B [MEDIA]. - DPE is a narrative problem more than a cash problem. It is 24% of international stores but 1.4% of revenue [SEC], ~$69M [CALC]. - The buyback criticism is mostly hindsight. The H1 paper loss is ~$43M, 0.4% of market cap.

His arithmetic undercuts his own HOLD, though. His weighted 9.1% a year sits below the 9.7–10.2% hurdle, and he concedes the call turns on one point of EPS growth. A 1% starter position below the hurdle is a bet on the bull-case weight, not a margin of safety. His "clear BUY at or below ~$265" sits just inside my $273 ceiling. The bears add three facts he cannot net away: - U.S. net openings were 45 in H1 against 172 in FY2025 [SEC]. - Franchisee economics for FY2025–26 have no primary source. The last dated figure is ~$150K of EBITDA per store in 2023 on a ~$1.4M AUV [MEDIA]. - Shareholder returns ran at roughly 115% of H1 FCF (Sherlock's estimate), on $4.72B of net debt.

I weight the bull argument at 40% and the bear argument at 60%. This is a weight on arguments, not on scenarios. The bears are not claiming the business is broken. They are claiming the price already pays for its recovery, and on the evidence that is the stronger argument.

Private buyer. A PE buyer gets nothing from financial engineering. The structure already carries ~4.9x net debt to income from operations, and EV is $14.57B, ~15.3x FY2025 income from operations of $954.0M. Unlevered FCF of ~$0.79B is a 5.4% yield on EV (Lynch). A strategic buyer for the world's largest pizza company is implausible, and Pizza Hut's $2.7B sale comes with no multiple. The informative buyers are two recorded ones: - Berkshire, which disclosed its stake in the Q3 2024 13F at an estimated $400–450 cost [MEDIA, unverified]. Whether it still holds in 2026 is not established. That cost is ~24–27x FY2024 diluted EPS of $16.69, the EPS of its entry year, and ~24x today's ~$17.65. Today's price is 25.6–33.9% below it. - Management, which repurchased at ~$365.9 in H1 2026: ~$398.8 in Q1 and ~$351.9 in Q2 [CALC from SEC], or ~22.6x and ~19.9x trailing earnings. Those purchases came after a FY2025 with +3.0% U.S. comps. Comps at the time of Berkshire's entry are not in the dossier.

So these buyers paid ~20–27x. My buy zone is far below both. The 2026 comp stall is the reason. Their losses show what a quality multiple costs without a margin of safety. They do not show that $297.62 is cheap.

4. Valuation — and what the DCF defect does

The defect. The pipeline's $160.32 (owner earnings) and $164.55 (FCF) discount cash flows that are already after $181.1M of net interest, then subtract the $4.72B of net debt again. That counts the debt twice. Adding back ~$142.7 a share on 33.08M shares gives $303.0 and $307.2. I accept the correction: an equity cash flow discounted at a cost of equity is already an equity value. I do not use $160 as a bear anchor, and I do not use $303 as fair value either. The corrected figure carries the pipeline's 4.3% growth input. Against that, the 2026 evidence shows: - Q2 global retail sales +3.0% and Q1 +3.4% [SEC]; - Q2 income from operations +2.6% ex-FX [SEC]; - U.S. comps averaging ~+0.5% across H1; - H1 FCF down 5.5% ($313.6M vs $331.7M).

The $303 is a staged figure: 4.3% growth for the explicit period, then a 3% terminal rate. In single-stage terms it equals perpetual growth of ~3.4% at 10%: g = (10% × $303 − $19.35) / ($303 + $19.35). It is not "the value at 4.3%." A perpetual 4.3% would be worth $354 at 10%, and the Historian is right that this overstates the case. I therefore drop the perpetual 4.3% column from my grid. The corrected $303 is the pipeline's own answer at ~3.4% perpetual growth, which is just above the market's implied ~3.3%. The Graham Floor of $87.45 is internally consistent but too punitive here, because it capitalises after-tax operating income and subtracts debt at par. I replace it with 10x owner EPS.

My central value is $290. It is equity-basis Gordon arithmetic, value = owner EPS × (1+g) / (r − g), on $19.35 of owner EPS ($0.64B TTM owner earnings / 33.08M shares):

The g bridge. Royalty and supply-chain revenue follow system retail sales, so the system retail sales growth rate sets g. Global retail sales ex-FX grew +3.0% in Q2 and +3.4% in Q1 [SEC], an H1 average of ~3.2%. That figure already combines unit growth (~3.6% annualised: +776 stores in FY2025, +389 in H1) with comps that slightly offset it (U.S. ~+0.5% across H1, international negative). Income from operations agrees: Q2 +2.6% ex-FX [SEC], Q1 ~+4.2% ex-gain and ex-FX [MEDIA], an H1 average of ~3.4%.

I set g at the H1 average of 3.2%, with the Q2 low of 3.0% as the conservative case. Two effects are left out on purpose: - Upside left out. With interest held flat, levered earnings grow ~1.2x faster than operating income ($954.0M / $772.9M pre-tax). - Downside left out. U.S. openings slowed to 45 in H1 from 172. U.S. stores are ~32% of the base.

I treat the two as offsetting and as prudence for a perpetual rate. Buybacks are not added on top, because the ~100% payout is already the yield. At 3.2% and 10.0% the grid gives $294. Less ~$4 of refinancing cost (below), the central is $290.

The owner-earnings base, varied. TTM owner earnings ($0.64B) sit below FY2025 ($0.66B, or $19.95 a share). The 8-K attributes the H1 FCF decline to "changes in operating assets and liabilities and the timing and amount of payments for advertising activities," which describes timing, not lost earning power. On the FY2025 base the central becomes $303 − $4 = ~$299, about the price, and the ceiling (below) becomes ~$281. I keep TTM as the base because it is the latest reported figure and the timing explanation is unverified. Moving to FY2025 shifts value by ~3%, inside the margin of safety.

The 2027 maturity, quantified. Six experts (Burry, Sherlock, the Biologist, the Historian, the Anthropologist and Lynch) wrote that the coupon on the maturing 2027 tranche "is not in the dossier." The raw 10-K lets us infer it: - The 2017 Ten-Year Notes ($1.0B at 4.118%, July 2017) and the 2018 9.25-Year Notes ($400.0M at 4.328%, April 2018) both reach their anticipated repayment dates in 2027. - Their $1.4B original face fits the $1.34B scheduled for 2027. The matching is my inference. - Rolling ~$1.34B from a blended ~4.18% to the September 2025 coupons (4.930% and 5.217%, ~5.07% average) costs ~$12M pre-tax. At the FY2025 tax rate of 21.9% ($168.6M / $770.3M), that is ~$9M after tax, or $0.28 a share: 1.5% of owner EPS and ~$4 of value. - At 5.5% the cost is ~$6 of value. At 6.0% it is ~$0.58 a share and ~$9 of value. I deduct the ~$4 base case.

The maturity is a few dollars a share of earnings drag, not a solvency event. The real leverage mechanism is a different one. Principal amortization on the notes is suspended only while Holdco leverage stays at or below 5.0x (older notes) or 5.5x (2025 Notes) of Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA. At FY2025 the company passed both tests. My rough estimate of gross debt to EBITDA is ~4.7x: $4.88B over ~$1.04B, which is $954.0M of income from operations plus ~$0.09B of depreciation. That implies roughly 7% of EBITDA headroom before the 5.0x test bites.

The indenture defines both "total debt" and "Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA," and the dossier gives neither value. My proxy leaves out the indenture's add-backs (SBC of ~$0.04B, at least). Those add-backs raise EBITDA, so the proxy probably overstates leverage and understates headroom. The true headroom is probably wider than 7%.

The consequence of a breach can be sized. Scheduled amortization would resume, at about the $49.3M a year the schedule shows for 2026. That is ~7.6% of TTM FCF and ~14% of FY2025 buybacks ($354.7M). The cash goes from buybacks to debt reduction, which is roughly value-neutral, and nothing goes toward default. Unrestricted cash is only $0.16B. The DSCR covenant is 1.75x, and interest cover is ~5.3x.

Stress test. The adjusted column is anchored to FY2025 FCF of $671.5M (TTM $0.65B). It takes FCF from $0.67B to $0.57B at −10% revenue, $0.47B at −20% and $0.37B at −30%. FCF turns negative only at about a 70% revenue decline. Burry's question (does the business survive −20%?) gets a yes: $0.47B of FCF against the $1.34B due is ~2.9x. The Futurist's 20% demand shock implies ~7x leverage and ~3.6x interest cover, and values the stock at ~$142–178. That is the tail which the leverage magnifies.

Implied multiples. Central $290 is 15.0x owner EPS and 16.4x GAAP EPS (~$17.65). The stock trades at 15.4x owner EPS and 16.86x P/E. There is no peer median, because Section M is empty. My central sits ~3% below the market. That is the gap between 3.2% and the market's implied 3.3% growth, plus the refinancing cost.

5. Decision logic: ceiling and floor

Ceiling, hidden value. Bezos finds no cash incinerator: every segment earns money, and the core P/E is the headline ~16.9x. Burry's cost-dumping check is clean. Total G&A was $281.9M against $281.4M. Supply Chain G&A fell $7.2M and International $7.9M while U.S. G&A rose $15.6M (+10.3%), which is reallocation, not concealment. Nobody (Buffett, the Futurist or anyone else) confirms a widening moat. I make no ceiling raise.

Floor, quality. Buffett finds pricing power unproven. Jobs rates the product "Sugared Water": focused, but with no evidence of love (no NPS or retention data). The Biologist confirms a healthy ecosystem. The Anthropologist sees durability as a functional utility, not as identity. This is a Quality-tier business, not a Franchise-tier one. On the Franchise Premium test: - (1) It passes: no SEVERE flag. - (2) The margin route fails (operating margin is 19.3%). The ROIC route at 66–78% passes for a capital-light franchisor with ~$0 goodwill. ROIC's denominator includes the debt, so the debt-funded stockholders' deficit does not impair it the way it impairs ROE. - (3) It passes only arguably: revenue is recurring per order, not per contract. - (4) It fails: the consumer switches in one tap, and the franchisee lock-in is worth only what that consumer pull is worth.

No franchise premium. Ceiling: $273, by one derivation. It is the Gordon value at the conservative end of both judgment inputs, less the refinancing cost: - r at the 10.2% top of the hurdle band. The dossier's 0–0.5% securitized-leverage premium sits inside that rate, and this is the only place I charge leverage. - g at the Q2 low of 3.0%.

That gives $277 − $4 = $273: 14.1x owner EPS and 15.5x GAAP EPS, inside the 15–18x Quality band, with no further cut. The margin of safety is 5.9% below the $290 central, and it comes from forecast prudence on g and r, not from a second leverage charge. Floor: $194, which is 10x owner EPS ($193.5) and matches the Futurist's no-growth EPV of $190–199.

6. Final Decision

Decision: WAIT. The business clears the quality bar. The price does not. Tripwire check for TOO UNCERTAIN: none of the five fires. There are no SEVERE flags and no regime variable, and no expert says the outcome is unknowable; the dominant variable, U.S. comps, is observable every quarter.

Moat Tribunal result: Strong-to-Uncertain, not Decaying. Sherlock, the Biologist, the Historian, the Anthropologist and Lynch rate it MINOR. Bezos, Buffett, Burry, Cook, Jobs, the Psychologist and the Futurist rate it MODERATE, for different reasons: - Bezos, Buffett, Burry and Cook: share is rising while sales per store stall, and franchisee returns are unverified. - Jobs: no evidence that customers love the product. - the Futurist: platform disintermediation. - the Psychologist: the evidence is too thin to judge.

Buy Zone: $194–$273

At or below $273, the owner yield (7.1%) plus 3.0% growth earns ~10.3%, which clears even the top of the hurdle band. Below $194, you get the business at its no-growth value and the growth for free. There is no band cut for leverage, because leverage is charged once, in r. I made no hidden-value raise.

Why it would be mispriced at $273: fear. A comp stall, a new CEO (Joe Jordan, effective October 1, 2026, not yet confirmed by a filing), a 2027 refinancing headline and a securities class action alleging overstated net-store-growth guidance [MEDIA, allegations only] are the stories that produce a temporary ~8% drop in a durable franchisor. At $297.62 there is no mispricing. The price is a fair one for ~3.3% growth, and it sits ~3% above my central value.

Moat half-life: ~15 years. The franchisee and supply-chain lock-in (26 dough centers, a ~99% franchised system, a 95%-promote-from-within operator base [MEDIA]) decays slowly. The consumer-facing edge (32.9% delivery share [SEC, preliminary and simulated Circana], >80% digital sales [MEDIA]) is what aggregators and autonomous delivery would erode. That is the shorter clock, roughly a decade.

Position Size: 0% at $297.62. At or below $273 the position is up to 3%, the Moderate-conviction cap. Three unresolved inputs justify Moderate rather than High conviction. Each moves value by more than the $17 margin of safety ($290 central less the $273 ceiling): - Whether order growth converts back into same-store sales. Half a point of g (3.0% to 3.5% at 10%) moves value by ~$23 ($285 to $308). - FY2025–26 franchisee EBITDA. It governs U.S. unit growth. U.S. stores are ~32% of the base, and their ~2.4% FY2025 unit growth contributes ~0.8 points of g. A stall costs roughly $20–25. - The hurdle band itself. With the 10-year at 5.28% [LIVE], r between 9.74% and 10.2% moves value by ~$19 at g = 3% ($277 to $296).

The 2027 coupon (~$4–9) and the owner-earnings base (~$9) are smaller than the margin of safety, so they are not part of the conviction basis.

What would change the call at today's price, without a price drop: two quarters of U.S. comps at +2% or better with orders still growing, U.S. net openings above 100 in H2, and the 2027 notes refinanced near 5%. Those would move g toward 3.5–4%, which the 10% row of the grid values at $308–335 before the ~$4 refinancing cost. What would turn it to SELL for holders is the Historian's tell: U.S. net store count going negative, or franchisee EBITDA below the 2022 trough of $139K.

DPZ — Cultural Anthropologist Analysis

Analysis date 2026-10-04, price $297.62. Dossier confirmed as Domino's Pizza, Inc. (DPZ). Two DKOB.F (Domino's Pizza Group PLC) transcripts were already excluded by the dossier, and I use none of their content.

1. CULTURAL ROLE: a utility with ritual edges

What Domino's is in a life. It is a convenience utility wrapped around a ritual food. Pizza itself is the ritual: Friday night, game day, the family order. Domino's is the lowest-friction way to buy it. The dossier shows where the strength sits.

  • Digital ordering exceeds 80% of U.S. sales, and Rewards members number "tens of millions" [MEDIA, "Business & Moat Analysis (2026)"]. That is a habit in the phone, not an identity.
  • The brand has recall (77% of Gen Z/millennials call the jingle catchy; 67% favor the brand refresh, 73% of Gen Z, 70% of millennials [MEDIA, Pizza Marketplace]). This is a survey of opinion about advertising, not of purchase behavior. It shows the brand is not disliked. It does not show devotion.
  • "Domino's" is not a cultural verb the way "Google it" is. The dossier says brand-as-verb data is not present. I will not invent it.

Is the cultural role growing? Category and brand tell different stories.

  • The U.S. QSR pizza category was $43.4B in 2025 vs $42.8B in 2024, +1.4% [CALC from SEC]. The FY2025 food basket rose +3.5% [SEC]. A category growing slower than its own input prices is shrinking in real volume. Pizza is a mature ritual, not an expanding one.
  • Domino's is taking share inside that flat pie: ~23.3% of U.S. QSR pizza in 2025 vs ~22.5% [SEC, citing preliminary and simulated Circana CREST data]. Delivery share is ~32.9% and carryout ~19.6% [SEC]. Pizza Hut's systemwide sales fell ~8% in fiscal 2025 and it was sold for $2.7B in June 2026 [MEDIA]. Papa John's and Pizza Hut "announced hundreds of store closures" [MEDIA, QSR Magazine].
  • U.S. retail sales were $9,952.9M (+4.8%) in FY2025 [SEC]. So Domino's grew about 3.4 points faster than the category [CALC]. The cultural role grows by displacement, not by pizza becoming more central.

Embeddedness in cultural moments. Delivery is the moment Domino's owns: the night you do not want to cook or go out. Carryout is the weaker moment (19.6% share vs 32.9% in delivery [SEC]), and the Q2 release says order growth came in both channels [SEC]. That is a positive sign of widening occasions. The U.S. same-store figure stayed at +0.1%, so the added orders came at lower average ticket or mix. That is arithmetic from the dossier, and the size of the effect is not disclosed.

2. TRIBAL DYNAMICS: Captive vs Devotee

The customer tribe. The dossier has no cohort, NPS, churn or retention data (Jobs section: "NPS, churn and retention figures are not in the dossier"). I can say what the structure implies.

  • Domino's customers are mostly occasion buyers, not members of a community. Fast-food pizza has many substitutes (Little Caesars, Papa John's, Pizza Hut, local pizzerias, Uber/DoorDash alternatives). Switching cost is a few taps. That points to neither captive nor devotee: customers are tenants who stay while the price, speed and coupon are right.
  • The Captive vs Devotee test therefore gives a mixed result. Customers are not prisoners (no lock-in, no monopoly). The loyalty program and app are a soft devotion, but nothing in the dossier shows tribal advocacy or resentment. A brand that earns share by being cheap and fast survives disruption only as long as it stays cheaper and faster.

The franchisee tribe is the real community, and it is the load-bearing one.

  • ~99% of stores are franchised [SEC]. The system take is a 5.5% royalty plus a 6.0% U.S. advertising contribution [MEDIA/SEC], plus a supply-chain margin of ~12% on food sold to franchisees [SEC].
  • Franchisee economics are old or third-party. U.S. average unit volume is ~$1.4M; EBITDA per store was $170K at the 2020-21 peak, $139K in 2022 and ~$150K in 2023 [MEDIA, Franchise Cost & Profit 2026 / FDD data]. That is ~10-12% of AUV [CALC]. No FY2025 or 2026 franchisee margin is in the dossier. The FY2025 release says "increased franchisee profits" with no figure.
  • Culture signal: 95% of owners are former store-level employees [MEDIA, Vestrule]. This is an internal promotion ladder, a genuine tribal glue. It is a media claim and I weight it accordingly.
  • The SBA loan default rate is 0.7% [MEDIA, FDD data]. That is mild evidence that franchisees are not in distress.
  • Franchise-store comps were 0.0% in Q2 and order growth was driven by third-party delivery [MEDIA, Franchise Cost & Profit 2026]. If the incremental order comes through a marketplace that charges 30-40% effective cost [MEDIA, threat register #2], the tribe is being asked to grow orders at lower margin. Food basket +2.2% in Q2 against comps of ~0% squeezes them further. This is the single most important tribal stress point, and the dossier gives only third-party evidence on it.

Gatekeepers. Delivery aggregators are the new gatekeepers. DoorDash leads more than half the U.S. market [MEDIA, threat #2]. Domino's "teams with DoorDash, ditches Uber Eats exclusivity" [MEDIA] and has agreements with Uber and DoorDash [SEC]. A brand that spent 25 years building its own delivery and app channel now borrows other people's customers. A Morgan Stanley estimate flags 20-25% of locations as exposed, with up to 2% sales erosion at mature stores [MEDIA, undated and apparently predating the agreements]. That is a cultural shift: discovery moves from "I want Domino's" to "I want dinner" and the app decides.

3. GENERATIONAL ANALYSIS: the "My Kids" Test

The dossier has no usage-by-cohort data. I cannot show decline or growth by generation, and I will not claim to.

What can be reasoned from the facts present:

  • Stack fit. Gen Z and Gen Alpha are mobile-first and marketplace-first. Domino's is >80% digital [MEDIA], which fits. But the marketplace habit means the child may open DoorDash first and meet Domino's as one tile. That works if Domino's wins the tile, which it does on speed and price. It weakens brand-as-destination.
  • Brand perception. The refresh scored 73% favorable with Gen Z vs 67% overall [MEDIA]. Gen Z is more favorable than average. That is a cheap and weak test, since it measures ad reception, but it points against an obvious generational rejection.
  • Values. The next cohort's stated priorities are transparency, authenticity and health. The threat register carries GLP-1 usage at about 18% of U.S. adults in spring 2026, up from ~14% in 2025 (FTI survey of 1,007 adults) [MEDIA]. A separate KFF poll gives ~12% [MEDIA]. The two surveys disagree by six points, which is the dossier's own warning on precision. Both point the same way: a rising share of adults eat less. Pizza is a high-calorie, high-carbohydrate product. The status is "speculative" in the register and I treat it as a slow headwind to category volume, not a thesis breaker. The category is already +1.4% in nominal terms [CALC].
  • Health transparency. Calorie-labeling rules are hard to apply to customizable pizza (up to 34 million combinations in a Federal Register comment [MEDIA]). A cohort that wants labels meets a product designed for customization. Minor.

Assessment. The next generation will probably keep ordering pizza. Whether it orders it from Domino's depends on marketplace economics and price, not on affection. I assign survival of the category: high. Survival of Domino's share: moderate-to-good, with the price paid in franchisee margin.

4. ORGANIZATIONAL CULTURE: preservation with operator discipline

  • How it talks about itself. "Hungry for MORE" ("MORE sales, MORE stores, MORE profits") [SEC]. The Q2 CEO quote leads with order count: "order growth is the most important driver of long-term success" [SEC, 8-K]. Management chose to headline orders in the quarter in which U.S. same-store sales were +0.1% and international was -0.1% [SEC]. That is a cultural tell. Culture here frames the story in the metric that looks best. It is not deceptive (both numbers are in the same release), but the emphasis follows the better number.
  • Calcified or entrepreneurial? Evidence leans operator-efficient rather than founder-entrepreneurial. Total G&A was $281.9M vs $281.4M (+0.2%) on revenue +5.0% [CALC from SEC]. Two segments cut G&A by 10.0% (Supply Chain) and 13.6% (International), while U.S. G&A rose 10.3% [CALC]. The cuts equal 18% and 28% of the respective segment-income gains [CALC]. Cost discipline is part of the earnings story, and the U.S. increase is a sign of investment or a rising cost of defending the home market.
  • Succession. Joe Jordan (then COO and President, Domino's U.S.) became CEO effective October 1, 2026 per the company release [SEC-company release, via search]. Weiner becomes Executive Chairman Designate, then Executive Chairman at the April 2027 annual meeting. No post-October-1 filing is in the dossier to confirm the change took effect, so I treat it as announced and probably done. An internal promotion of the U.S. operator, with the outgoing CEO staying close, is a preservation signal. It signals culture continuity, and it also means no outsider is questioning the model while U.S. comps stall.
  • Capital culture. FY2025 dividends ($237.3M) plus repurchases ($354.7M) were $592.0M, 98% of net income ($601.7M) and ~88% of FCF ($671.5M) [CALC, SEC]. The dividend rose to $1.99 a quarter ($7.96 a year), +14.4% on the FY2025 per-share rate [CALC]. This is a shareholder-return culture financed partly by securitized debt: stockholders' deficit, $4.88B borrowings [SEC]. It works while the royalty stream is stable and breaks if the stream wobbles. Culture here prefers returning cash over reinvesting at scale. Capex is ~$120M a year [SEC].
  • Buyback timing as a cultural signal. H1 2026 repurchases averaged ~$365.9 per share (Q1 ~$398.8, Q2 ~$351.9) [CALC from SEC]. The current price of $297.62 is 18.7% below the H1 average [CALC]. A holder at $400-$450 (Berkshire estimate [MEDIA, unverified]) is deeply under water, and we are told no one knows the exact price.
  • Litigation culture. A securities class action alleges DPZ overstated prospects regarding its largest master franchisee and long-term net-store-growth guidance [MEDIA, law-firm notices]. Filing date, class period and status are not given. These are allegations, not findings. I treat them only as a flag that the guidance culture is being questioned. The no-poach suits and the PULSE/joint-employer litigation are old and undated [MEDIA] and I place little weight on them.

  • The cultural fault line is the largest master franchisee. Domino's Pizza Enterprises (DPE) runs 3,524 stores in 12 markets, ~24% of international and ~16% of global stores [SEC], but it produced only 1.4% of DPZ revenue (~$69M [CALC]) [SEC].
