The defect: §2 and the Executive Summary state that valuing Tencent on owner earnings ($4.04/share) "clears today's price" and "WAIT would become BUY" (also "On owner earnings it is $59 (BUY)" in Load-Bearing Factor #1). So even on the owner-earnings base, the verdict stays WAIT, not BUY. The cash-base choice (owner earnings vs. SBC-adjusted FCF) therefore no longer decides the verdict by itself — every input pair that crosses the price includes the owner-earnings base, but only a minority of those pairs cross the actual BUY line (price ÷ 0.9). Read "Cash base" in the Load-Bearing Factors as the largest single swing factor in the central value, not as a factor whose adverse state alone flips the decision.

2. Why the council split, and who is right

The six Codex-run experts voted PASS/PASS/SELL/PASS/PASS/PASS. The six Claude-run experts voted HOLD/BUY/BUY/BUY/HOLD/BUY. I checked the three explanations the question suggested. Two of them are wrong.

  • Not the cost of equity. All twelve adopted the dossier's 13–15% local cost of equity (E006) and all twelve refused the printed anchors as fair value, because those anchors sit near the 10.32% base (E007, E065).
  • Not the regime chain as such. Every Claude-pool BUY names the same dual-sovereign risk in its KEY RISK line as the Codex PASSes do. Where they differ is in what they do about it. The Claude pool read "size as if the regime risk crystallizes" (E005) as a sizing instruction and bought 2–4%. The Codex pool read it as a pricing instruction and demanded the discount first.
  • The real split is the cash base, plus one category error. The Claude pool anchored on owner earnings of $4.04/share (E017: $36.49B). The Codex pool anchored on free cash flow of $3.14 (E145: $28.37B). That is a 29% difference in the numerator, and in my model it is the whole verdict. My base is FCF less SBC: $2.72 per share. On that base the central value is $40.19. Keeping everything else fixed:
  • FCF without the SBC deduction ($3.14) gives $46.15.
  • Owner earnings ($4.04) give $58.83, the only base that clears today's price, and WAIT would become BUY.

Burry settles which base is right. By the dossier's definitions, both measures start from the same operating cash flow. Owner earnings = operating cash flow − PP&E depreciation − SBC. FCF = operating cash flow − capex. So owner earnings less FCF ($36.49B − $28.37B = $8.12B) must equal capex minus PP&E depreciation minus SBC. With FY2025 capex of $11.8B (E113) and SBC of $3.83B (E026), that implies negative depreciation. The likeliest explanation (my judgment, not a filed fact) is that the pipeline's FCF deducts purchases of content and licences, and owner earnings deducts only PP&E depreciation. Either way, owner earnings exclude spending that recurs. The owner-earnings premium over FCF also appeared only in 2024–25 (E018, E019, E020: in 2022 owner earnings were below FCF), which is exactly when capex began to outrun depreciation. 2Q2026 capex was RMB52.8B, up 176% year on year (E098). That is a run-rate of ~$31.5B a year, against $11.8B in FY2025 (E113). GPU fleets must be replaced to stay competitive, so calling that spend "growth, therefore optional" is the error. On capex, FCF is the honest base, and the Codex pool is right on the number.

That comparison is between two capex treatments, not two SBC treatments. Owner earnings deduct SBC ($3.83B, E026); FCF does not, because operating cash flow adds SBC back as a non-cash charge. Switching to FCF must not quietly drop the dilution cost. So my base takes FCF's treatment of capex and owner earnings' treatment of SBC:

$28.37B − $3.83B = $24.54B, or $2.72 per share.

That is Burry's figure, a 4.9% cash yield at $55.65. I model no separate share-count dilution, because the SBC is already charged.

The Claude pool's second error is comparing segment growth with whole-company growth. Lynch and the Futurist set International Games at +32% (E123, E149) and Business Services at +22% (E124) against the 5.7–7.7% growth the market implies. But these segments are a minority of revenue, and in the latest quarter the reported FinTech & Business Services line grew only +9% (E094). The Futurist concedes the point. Lynch does not. There is also an internal inconsistency: the Claude pool's own triggers sit below today's price. Historian and Anthropologist are at $27–31, the Futurist at $29–39, the Biologist at $38–42, Sherlock at $38–40. Eleven of twelve experts put their entry between $27 and $42. The only exception is Lynch's $72–85, which is a price target and not an entry. The Claude pool agreed with the Codex pool on where the margin of safety is. It then bought a starter position above that level. The independence audit makes this sharper: 8 of 12 triggers use one basis (scenario grid), and 4 use zero-growth yield. This clustering is one witness, the hurdle, wearing eleven hats. It is not eleven confirmations.

The Codex pool made errors of its own, and I correct them: - Cook's 3% perpetual growth at 14% stacks a regime hurdle on top of a utility growth rate. That double-counts. - The Codex pool gave too little credit to the one full observed cycle. Margin went 33.9% → 18.9% → 29.9% in about two years (E151). Behaviourally, that sits on Alibaba's side of the fork, not Ant's. - Burry's SELL overreaches. His own text says the dossier "does not yet prove fraud, insolvency, or a near-term short catalyst." The stress test backs him on that: FCF stays at $17.56B after a 20% revenue fall (E069) and turns negative only near −55% (E071). A business in that condition is a WAIT, not a short.

4. Valuation

Treatment of risk: an explicit override of the dossier's discount rate. The dossier sets the local cost of equity at 13–15% (E006): a 10.32% base (E065, of which Damodaran's country premium is 0.91%, E063) plus a named 3–5pp JUDGMENT premium for three risks: 1. The US Section 1260H listing and delisting-adjacent risk. 2. PRC regulatory intervention. 3. RMB-to-USD capital controls.

I discount at 11.65% and price risks (1) and (2) as scenarios instead of in the rate. Risks (1) and (2) are discrete events, so they become explicit scenarios: D (Washington forced sale, 15% weight) and C (Beijing repeat, 20% weight). Risk (3) is a continuous friction, so it stays in the rate.

The rule for the residual. The dossier gives the 3–5pp premium for three named risks without splitting it. I split it equally. Risk (3) therefore gets one-third of the 4pp midpoint, 1.33pp, varied from 1.0pp to 1.67pp (one-third of 3pp and of 5pp). That gives 10.32% + 1.33pp = 11.65%, varied 11.32%–11.99%.

Disclosing the size of the sovereign charge. I solved for the single flat rate that, applied to scenario A's cash flows with no tail scenarios, reproduces my $40.19 central value. It is 13.6%. That is a total premium of 3.3pp over the 10.32% base. It sits inside the dossier's 3–5pp band, at its lower end, and is not below it.

5. Decision Logic — Ceiling and Floor

Ceiling. - No incinerator to switch off. Bezos could not run the shutdown test because segment operating profit is not disclosed. The AI build is an investment programme, not a loss-making unit that could be shut down to release profit. Operating profit is still growing faster than revenue: +12% against +11% (E088, E090). - Cost-dumping cannot be tested (Burry). Two gaps block it: attributable profit grew +0.7% against +9% on an adjusted basis (E091, E092), probably because of investee mark-to-market (E093), and FY2025 EBITDA of $50.2B exceeds net income by $16.6B (E111). - The business survives a 20% revenue drop (E069). - The only hidden value I credit is the listed portfolio, at a haircut. There is no ceiling raise beyond that.

Floor. - Buffett: no proof of pricing power beyond "a more favourable pricing environment" in cloud (E114). - Jobs: "Sugared Water". There is no NPS or churn data (E128). - Biologist: Keystone (Thriving). - Anthropologist: a Cultural Institution (Enduring), but a state-protected one. - Offsetting evidence: management is still building. It has spent more than RMB300B (~$45.1B) on R&D since 2018 (E140). It builds its own Hunyuan/Yuanbao models and holds a stake in Moonshot (E115). Its CSO says domestic chips will be used more in H2 (E141), a partial answer to GPU export limits. But GPU scarcity (E126) and hyperscaler-scale rivals (E116) cap the cloud. - ROIC has been flat to slightly down: 18.8% → 16.2% (E117). - SG&A grew 19% in FY2025, from $22.25B to $26.53B (E042, E041), faster than revenue growth of $98.5B → $112.1B (E009).

Result: the Quality band applies conditional on survival. Scenarios A and B sit at 18.4x and 14.5x SBC-adjusted FCF. The probability weighting pulls the central value down to 14.8x.

  • Fair-value limit: $36. This is $40.19 less a 10% margin of safety ($36.17).
  • Absurdly-cheap floor: $32. This has one basis only: the Graham 10x on SBC-adjusted FCF ($27.17) plus the listed portfolio at a 50% haircut ($4.52), which gives $31.68. The dossier's $38.68 no-growth floor (E021) ignores SBC and uses the 10.32% rate. So do the $65.31 and $83.82 DCF anchors (E023, E024), which also assume no tails.

6. Too-Uncertain Tripwires

  1. 3+ SEVERE flags: no.
  2. Load-bearing regime variable: yes.
  3. Two or more PASSes citing uncalculable variables: no. All five PASSes are price-based ("at the current price").
  4. Unknowability language: no. I have weighted the tails rather than declared them unknowable.
  5. A margin of safety so wide it signals distrust of my own estimate: no. It is 10%.

That is one tripwire, so TOO UNCERTAIN is not the default. I argue out of it on two points. - The Beijing track has a base rate: one full cycle with a two-year recovery (E151). - The Washington track is bounded. It threatens a US holder's access, not Weixin's cash, and the Hong Kong line survives it.

The dominant variable can be weighted. It cannot be assumed away, and that is why it sits in the scenario weights.

7. Final Decision

Decision: WAIT. This is a good business at a full price. The business deserves a real position. The price does not yet pay for the two sovereigns that stand behind it. At $55.65 the market pays the sovereign-free value: $55.88 for scenarios A and B at the 10.32% base. My probability-weighted value is $40.19. That carries a sovereign charge equivalent to a 13.6% flat rate on the survivor case, at the low end of the dossier's 13–15%.

TCEHY — Ecosystem Biologist Field Report

1. Species Classification

Tencent is best classified as a keystone species with parasitic tendencies in one habitat and symbiotic tendencies in another — the classification is not uniform across the ecosystem, and a biologist who forces a single label is lying to the food web.

  • Weixin/WeChat (the core habitat): This is keystone infrastructure. At ~1.4 billion users [MEDIA, see MOAT THREAT SEARCH] it combines messaging, payments, mini-programs, and social graph into what outside sources describe as functioning like national infrastructure. Remove it and the mini-program merchant population, the payments rail, and a large share of Chinese digital social life collapse simultaneously — that is the textbook definition of a keystone species, not a preference.
  • Gaming publishing (Tencent-owned studios + third-party publishing): Here the ecology tilts toward parasite-adjacent. The dossier's own comparator is precise: ByteDance reportedly offers developers a 90/10 revenue split versus Tencent's ~40% take — meaning Tencent extracts roughly 60% of the host's (developer's) economics [MEDIA per Naavik]. That is a materially higher extraction rate than a rival platform is now offering in the same niche. Developers who can leave for a lower-take host will; the ones who stay are the ones who can't (network effects, distribution lock-in) — which is exactly the "captive host" signature the persona brief asks to watch for.
  • Enterprise cloud (Tencent Cloud): This reads as genuine symbiosis. The state-owned bank running 80,000+ physical hosts, the 40% cost reduction and sub-30-minute RTO case studies [MEDIA per PR Newswire/Tencent Cloud] describe a host that is better off with the relationship than without it, and that is voluntarily deepening the dependency (migrating more workloads, not fewer).

Net: Tencent is not one species: it is a keystone habitat (Weixin) that hosts one symbiotic tenant population (enterprise cloud) and one higher-extraction tenant population (game developers) inside it. The moat classification in the dossier — network_effects, switching_costs, platform_ecosystem — is consistent with this: the switching-cost and platform-ecosystem elements are what let the higher extraction rate in gaming persist without immediate host flight.

2. Host Health

Host health is genuinely bifurcated, and the bifurcation matters more than any single blended number:

  • Domestic gaming/social host population (the ~70% "Individual" tier, per the dossier's estimated segmentation): This is the population most directly capped by state action — PRC minors' online-gaming restriction (~3 hrs/week, Fri/weekend/holiday only) has been in force since ~2021 [MEDIA per Reuters/Bloomberg], and the dossier's own Historian extract notes user growth is "increasingly reliant on older, already-saturated demographics" [MEDIA]. A host population that cannot expand at its edges (minors) is not a growing host population, even if current adult users remain intensely engaged.
  • Enterprise cloud host population (~10% Enterprise tier, estimated): Expanding — the case-study evidence (state-owned bank migration, 150+ subsystems in 6 hours) reads as a host population deepening its dependency, not shrinking it [MEDIA].
  • Consolidation effect: Enterprise-tier host consolidation (fewer, larger accounts like the 80,000-host bank deployment) generally increases Tencent's leverage — large accounts that have already re-architected around Tencent Cloud face high switching costs. Consolidation in the individual/gaming tier, by contrast, does not increase leverage in the same way because the constraint there is regulatory (a ceiling on hours/spend), not competitive.
  • Rent-extraction rate on the host base overall: Directly quantifiable only in gaming (the ~60% take, above) [MEDIA per Naavik]. No equivalent extraction-rate figure exists in the dossier for FinTech/payments or advertising — flag this as a blind spot rather than estimate it.
  • Could the host evolve to no longer need this species? Two live threats registered in the record argue yes, partially: (1) WeChat Stores' relaunch let merchants run e-commerce without third-party SaaS systems, which squeezed providers like Youzan/Weimeng — a case of Tencent's platform absorbing a dependent species rather than the reverse [enacted, per MOAT THREAT SEARCH]; (2) more interestingly for the "agents building direct channels" pattern named in the persona brief, a reported WeChat AI agent could schedule a merchant's own self-operated mini-program directly, "completely skipping" the discovery/diversion links that platforms like Meituan currently monetize [speculative, per MOAT THREAT SEARCH]. That second item cuts for Tencent in the near term (it is the platform doing the disintermediating, not the one being disintermediated) but is worth tracking as a capability that, generalized, could someday let end users route around any single intermediary — including WeChat's own commission-taking layers.

3. Invasive Species Threat

  • Same-rules competitors: ByteDance/Douyin is the concretely-named invasive species, and it is not playing by the same economic rules — it is undercutting Tencent's take-rate in game publishing (90/10 vs. Tencent's ~40/60) [MEDIA per Naavik] and competing directly for short-video/mini-game ad dollars and e-commerce mini-program traffic (Douyin, Xiaohongshu) [MEDIA, MOAT THREAT SEARCH]. ByteDance has also gone further than a market competitor and turned litigant, suing Tencent in a Beijing court over alleged monopolistic behavior [enacted per Reuters/TechCrunch] — an invasive species that is also lobbying the ecosystem's regulator.
  • Adjacent-ecosystem crossover: AI-native "Little Dragons" (Moonshot named specifically) sit adjacent rather than head-on today, and notably Tencent has taken an equity position in at least one of them [MEDIA] — a biologist would call this pre-emptive symbiosis with a potential invasive species rather than passive exposure. Tencent is also building its own Hunyuan/Yuanbao models rather than waiting to be disrupted [MEDIA per Buffett extract]. This is the correct evolutionary response to an invasive-species threat: absorb or co-opt it before it colonizes your niche.
  • Colonization speed: The historical record in this dossier suggests colonization-and-regulatory-response cycles in Chinese tech run on a roughly 2-3 year clock — the "common prosperity" wave that hit margins from 2021 shows a trough-to-recovery arc of about two years (33.9%→18.9%→29.4-29.9%, 2022-2025) [CALC]. That is a useful base rate: invasive-species and regulatory shocks in this ecosystem are not permanent extinction events on the evidence available, but they are not instant either.
  • A second, slower-moving invasive vector: GPU/compute export restrictions are throttling Tencent Cloud's absolute capacity relative to US hyperscalers (capex still trailing Microsoft/AWS/Google in absolute dollars despite +176% YoY growth) [MEDIA per DCD] — this doesn't invade Tencent's niche directly, but it caps how fast the symbiotic cloud-host relationship can scale, and a domestic rival with better chip access could invade that specific niche.

4. Ecosystem Resilience vs. Brittleness

This is where the "regime/political" problem-type classification in the dossier matters most, and the persona brief's instruction to size position "as if the regime risk crystallizes" is directly on point.

Points of failure, counted plainly: 1. Single dominant habitat: Weixin/WeChat concentration means a single-product shock (a ban, a forced architectural change, a sustained outage of trust) would cascade across gaming, ads, and payments simultaneously — this is the classic keystone-species fragility: enormous value when present, catastrophic and correlated loss if removed. 2. Single-jurisdiction regulatory exposure, domestically: PRC minors' gaming caps, antitrust-forced licensing unwinds (Tencent Music), and a live ByteDance antitrust suit are all draws from the same regulatory well. 3. A second, independent jurisdiction: the US DoD Section 1260H listing and NDAA Section 851 lobbyist exodus is a wholly separate failure point with a different owner (Washington, not Beijing) and its own timeline — the dossier is explicit that these two tracks should not be blended into one "China risk" line, and a biologist agrees: two independent predators is a materially different risk profile than one predator with two teeth. 4. DMA-style regulatory mimicry risk: the EU's Digital Markets Act obligations on gatekeepers (alternative app stores, mandated interoperability, real-time data portability) [enacted, MOAT THREAT SEARCH] is not yet aimed at Tencent specifically, but it is a template a PRC regulator or a future US action could borrow. 5. Portfolio-level concentration in a shrinking satellite ecosystem: the minority-investment portfolio fell from ~$100.3B to ~$81.6B fair value in a single quarter (31-Dec-2025 to 31-Mar-2026) [SEC] — a ~19% swing that now flows through consolidated GAAP earnings and adds a volatility vector unrelated to the operating ecosystem's actual health.

Diversifying or concentrating? Genuinely mixed. International Games (+32% YoY [SEC]) and Business Services/cloud (+22% YoY FY2025 [SEC]) are adding host niches outside the PRC-regulated domestic-gaming perimeter — that is diversification. But the core habitat (Weixin) itself is not diversifying geographically — the dossier is explicit that "the super-app model has not exported" and international growth comes from individual game titles succeeding on their own merits abroad, not from the ecosystem model transplanting. A biologist reads this as: the species is diversifying its range (new games, new cloud customers) without the habitat (WeChat) itself expanding its range — a meaningfully weaker form of diversification than it first appears.

Could one shock collapse the position? No single disclosed shock, on the evidence supplied, has yet done so — the 2021-23 crackdown cut margin by 15 points but did not extinguish the business, and the DoD listing has "not, in the supplied material, been tied to a disclosed operational or financial consequence to date." But the persona brief's mandate is to price the tail, not just the base case: a coordinated escalation (e.g., a PRC crackdown on the ad/gaming core coinciding with a US delisting-adjacent action on the ADR) is not in the historical base rate because it has not happened yet — it is a correlated-tail scenario the position sizing below already discounts for.

  • Capex is up 176% YoY (RMB52.8B, 2Q2026) [SEC] funding AI infrastructure, and cumulative R&D since 2018 exceeds RMB300B (~$45.1B) [MEDIA] — this is a species actively re-tooling, not one milking a static niche.

  • Buybacks continued through the capex ramp (FY2025 FCF still positive and growing, $28.37B [CALC]) — capital is being returned and reinvested simultaneously, not one at the expense of the other, on the annual data supplied.
  • The caveat: the gaming-publishing take-rate (~60% extraction vs. ByteDance's more generous split) [MEDIA per Naavik] is the one place the record shows Tencent defending a legacy extraction rate rather than adapting it. If that gap persists while a lower-take rival scales, this is the leading indicator to watch for whether Tencent is truly co-evolving with its developer-host population or simply mining a legacy position until the hosts have somewhere better to go.
  • Ownership signal worth reading ecologically: Prosus, the largest non-founder shareholder, has been reducing its stake (~29% in 2024 reporting → ~22.8% by April 2026, including a $14.6B accelerated offering) [MEDIA per Paul Weiss/Revenue Memo] — a large, informed long-term holder trimming is not itself proof of ecosystem decay (it can reflect Prosus's own capital needs), but it is not the behavior of a shareholder betting on accelerating co-evolution either, and the dossier confirms no 13F "smart money" has stepped in to replace that selling.

10-year evolutionary arc: if the current trajectory holds — continued AI co-option, continued international game diversification, continued cloud-host deepening — Weixin's keystone position in China is very likely durable for a decade; the closer historical analogue in the dossier (the prior 2021-23 crackdown) shows this species has already survived one full predator cycle and re-grown from an 18.9% margin trough back to 29.9%.
