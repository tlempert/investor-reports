The bull case and the counterargument

The bull case: recurring revenue keeps funding acquisitions, and new businesses reach healthy margins quickly — the latest cohort moved from -16% margin in Q1 to +16% by Q215, following the 2025 cohort, now near 20%16. The council weighted this 45% against a 55% counterargument17.

The counterargument is economic. Growth = organic + reinvestment × return on new capital. Base case: 2% organic (middle of the dossier's 1–3% net-of-churn range)18, 80% reinvestment, 11.6% return on new capital (inverted from 17.1% historical FCFA2S growth)19, giving 11.3% annual growth — short of the 12.4% a 20x exit requires. Disclaimer: assumes a constant return on reinvested capital; an approximation, not a forecast.

Bull evidence is company-sourced and forward-looking315. The counter: Constellation traded near 7x revenue and 25x EV/EBITDA amid enacted deal competition20, against 1–3% organic growth18. The moat could weaken from data-portability mandates enacted in roughly sixty countries21 and rising bids from other acquirers20; AI-driven margin compression is speculative and unquantified22.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Managers are paid cash bonuses used to buy shares, not stock; the share count has been flat at 21M for four years29, with no disclosed buyback program30. Retention is 91%, payout 9%, dividend yield 0.20%31. That passes a dilution test, but the sustainable-growth figure of 21.2% (ROE × retention)32 is stale, built on a trailing payout ratio.
