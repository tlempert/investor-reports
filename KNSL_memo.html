Kinsale Capital Group (KNSL) as an investment

A 24% return on equity, priced almost exactly for a 24% return on equity — "fair" is not "cheap."

I would not buy Kinsale today, though I admire the business. The risk isn't underwriting skill — among the best in its niche — but timing: the E&S pricing cycle turned down just as the stock carries a premium multiple. The paradox: casualty underwriting, investment income and buybacks still compound while the headline growth number and a widening expense ratio say otherwise. Restated: WAIT. Buy zone $251–$312 against a $346.50 central value1; 0% position at $356.852.

What you would own

Kinsale writes excess & surplus lines insurance in one segment, sold through independent wholesale brokers, not to policyholders directly3. Casualty — hard-to-place liability admitted insurers won't touch — is 70.7% of 2025 gross written premium; property, led by Commercial Property (18.9% of GWP), is being deliberately shed as pricing turns unfavorable3. There is no organizational switching cost: one-year policies renew through brokers, and ~60% of policyholders renew each year4. What you own is a process — in-house underwriting and claims, low expenses, a culture that shrinks volume rather than chases price — not a customer relationship.

The bull case and the counterargument

The strongest case: Kinsale holds a mid-70s combined ratio through a soft market while casualty, submissions and investment income keep growing — a 26–30% ROE, fully organic underwriter at ~14.5x trailing GAAP earnings is a gap the market isn't paying for11. Investment income grew 27.9% in 2025, duration extended 3.0 to 4.0 years — real, non-cyclical growth12.

The counterargument is arithmetic: at 4x book and 24–26% ROE, the price already equals ~14–16x earnings by identity — a high ROE at a high multiple is the price, not a mispricing. My interpretation: the pricing index fell to −5.9% in Q2 from −3.3% in Q1 — the whole book, 70.7% casualty, so softness is already partly present, not just a future risk of "spreading"13. Reserves are 91.2% IBNR, with adverse construction-liability development from 2016–2019 sitting inside the year's favorable development14. The expense ratio rose 20.6% to 21.7%; the non-commission component improved to 10.3% from 10.6%, so the rise sits in commissions — broker-driven, not volume deleverage6. The moat could weaken if AI underwriting reaches cost parity industry-wide, a trend the register documents without yet showing an effect on Kinsale's own ratio15.

How I would make the decision

Thesis: pay for this compounder only when the price no longer assumes the cycle holds.

Conditions that would support buying

  • Casualty pricing specifically stabilizes, not just property.
  • Reserve development stays favorable for two more quarters.
  • The company or a regulator answers the claims-handling allegation.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Price stays above the ceiling while the cycle question is open.
  • Submissions stay strong but written premium stays negative.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Reserve development turns adverse in aggregate, not one pocket.
  • The expense-ratio rise proves persistent commission inflation.
