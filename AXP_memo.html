The bull case and the counterargument

The council’s strongest bull case is that membership becomes habitual, younger customers replenish demand, and benefits spending eventually stabilizes. Filed fee per card rose from $92 to $117 while proprietary cards increased from 80.2M to 86.6M.6

How I would make the decision

My interpretation: I would buy durable membership economics when price allows for slower growth and the evidence supports earnings after credit losses.

Conditions that would support buying

  • Fee growth outpaces benefits costs, with total expenses no longer outrunning revenue.
  • Credit remains stable after reserve-release effects are disclosed.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Benefits inflation persists or underlying profitability remains obscured.
  • Price continues to require a longer growth runway than the governing model assumes.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Merchant pricing deteriorates faster than membership fees can compensate.
  • Alternative routing or partner losses structurally damage rewards economics.
