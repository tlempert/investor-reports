Cash flow and shareholder economics

The dossier’s TTM owner yield is 5.1%, beside 5.4% FCF yield4. Its owner earnings subtract PP&E depreciation—a maintenance-capex proxy—and stock-based compensation from operating cash flow; FCF subtracts actual capex. Neither deducts acquisitions.

The council normalizes owner earnings to $8.20B, or $6.01 per share, using the fiscal-year/TTM midpoint5. The quoted owner yield uses the higher TTM base, not that normalized base. The historical owner-earnings average is consequently not independent confirmation from distributable cash.

The bull case and the counterargument

The council’s strongest bull case combines restored shelf space, productivity, a simpler lineup and international growth. Walmart reportedly reduced Frito-Lay space, while snack prices were reportedly cut by up to 15%7. The activist agreement followed Elliott’s investment; the record does not establish that it forced the agreement.

Filed evidence shows every segment profitable, while Walmart accounts for approximately 14% of revenue3. My interpretation: scale could support recovery, but customer concentration limits the claim that the moat is widening.

The economic counterexample is the dossier’s simplified stress case: a 20% revenue decline leaves $3.10B FCF4, against $7.9B guided dividends6. This is a stress illustration, not a forecast or matched-period coverage test. Business survival would not guarantee dividend survival.

Private label, changing diets and regulation could weaken demand. The FTC pricing case was dismissed without prejudice; the separate private suit remains set aside in valuation7.

How I would make the decision

My interpretation: I would buy durable distribution only when the purchase price allows for uncertain pricing power and cash conversion.

Conditions that would support buying

  • North American organic revenue and food volume recover without further price concessions.
  • Core margins improve and cash generation funds shareholder returns.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Volume recovery remains dependent on promotional spending.
  • Borrowings bridge cash shortfalls without subsequent seasonal repayment.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Persistent organic contraction after pricing adjustments.
  • Acquisition impairments accompany continued cash-distribution shortfalls.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Filed shares declined from 1,382M to 1,369M, then 1,364.9M; annual SBC was $0.29B in the latest fiscal year8. Guided repurchases are $1.0B6. Net share reduction is not gross shares repurchased, so the dossier’s inferred purchase prices are unusable.

The council’s standard is growth that becomes owner cash without persistent debt support. Its consistency warning matters: acquisition-driven growth cannot be capitalized indefinitely while acquisition spending is omitted.
