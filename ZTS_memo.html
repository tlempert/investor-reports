Zoetis (ZTS) as an investment

A 21.6%-ROIC franchise priced at a third of its peer multiple — but the discount may already be spent.

I would wait, not buy, at $71.61 on 413.2M shares1. The central risk: U.S. companion-animal demand keeps shrinking while International and Livestock carry the company. The paradox: a 21.6%-ROIC toll road on veterinary prescriptions2, trading at 11.4x earnings against a 36.6x peer median3, is worth about $77 a share once growth is funded4 — only 7.1% above the price, short of the 10% margin a contested moat and an unsized legal tail require. Decision: WAIT — 0% position at $71.61; buy inside a $59–$69 zone, or at today's price once U.S. demand stabilizes5.

What you would own

Zoetis sells medicines, vaccines and diagnostics for companion animals (~70% of revenue) and livestock (~29%)6, across roughly 300 product lines, eight species and about 45 direct-market countries7. The moat runs through the prescribing veterinarian, not the brand: more than 5,500 patents, per-product FDA/EMA-grade approval, and 21 owned plants plus 90-plus contract manufacturers back the largest revenue base in the industry8. Switching happens one prescription at a time, with no contract or backlog to defend9 — so a well-capitalized rival copying the formula, not a generic, is the live threat.

Cash flow and shareholder economics

Operating cash flow ($2.84B) minus a maintenance-capex proxy ($0.33B, PP&E depreciation) minus stock compensation ($0.08B) equals owner earnings ($2.43B)11. That works out to $5.88 a share. The council uses this measure rather than the 24.1% free-cash-flow margin12 because free cash flow also deducts the ~$190M spent above depreciation to fund growth — a reinvestment charge, not a maintenance one.

The latest quarter

Q2 2026 consolidated revenue was flat, -1% organic; U.S. revenue fell to $1,266M from $1,356M (-7%), with U.S. gross margin down to 83.1% from 84.7%13. Net income of $691M fell 5% reported, yet diluted EPS rose 1%; adjusted diluted EPS rose 5%14. My interpretation: the buyback is doing real work per share even in the worst revenue quarter on record.

The bull case and the counterargument

Eleven of twelve council members make the same bull case: International (+6% organic) and Livestock (+11% organic) carry the company while U.S. demand recovers15. The counterargument is economic, not technological: growth must be paid for. Example — 2% perpetual growth at the company's 25.4% ROIC needs 7.9% of owner earnings reinvested16; crediting that growth as free overstates value by several dollars a share. Disclaimer: a stylized illustration, not a forecast. Evidence cuts both ways: U.S. gross margin expanded from 80.2% to 83.5% over 2023–2025 even as volume weakened17, while management's own stated causes — clinic traffic, Gen Z/Millennial price sensitivity, share loss to Elanco's Credelio Quattro and Zenrelia — are core-business, not cyclical18. The moat weakens fastest where Simparica Trio (~16% of revenue) and Apoquel (~12%) face direct rivals19, and in Librela, under safety scrutiny and litigation20.

How I would make the decision

Buy where the price already holds the margin of safety a contested moat requires, not where a re-rating must happen first.

Conditions that would support buying

  • U.S. segment revenue flat or up for two straight quarters, gross margin above 84%.
  • Price trading inside the $59–$69 zone.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • U.S. softness continues without a second guidance cut.
  • Librela and EU antitrust matters unresolved, no new adverse signal.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Simparica Trio or Apoquel revenue declines in the FY2026 10-K.
  • A label restriction on Librela.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Shares fell from 464M to 413.2M since 202225, but 2025's $3.235B of buybacks exceeded that year's $2.28B of free cash flow by $0.95B, funded by debt rising from $6.65B to $9.15B26. The standard: don't borrow to buy back stock at a rich multiple. Share-count-implied 2023–2024 buyback prices ran roughly $188–$201, above today's price, though biased upward by netting out share issuance27. Management is buying cheaply now; it did not always.

Price discipline without false precision

The $59–$69 zone is honest only to the width the ledger states: the floor is a fixed 10x-owner-earnings rule; the ceiling moves with the discount rate, growth-funding and weight judgments the council chose28. Treat $69 as a line to buy below, not a target.
