Brown-Forman as an investment

Pricing power intact, priced as if its category were not shrinking

I recommend waiting, with moderate conviction: a real brand franchise in a real category downturn, priced at $25.941 as if the recovery had already printed. The risk is not the brand — gross margin held at 60.5% against a 51.6% peer median2 even as flagship depletions fell 5%3 — it is whether last year's $402M jump in operating cash flow is a durable lower cost base or a release of an overfull warehouse4. Restated from the council's gated synthesis: worth about $22, buy at $17.40 or below, own nothing today56.

One segment built around Jack Daniel's Tennessee Whiskey: whiskey is 73.9% of net sales, whiskey plus RTD plus tequila 94.0%7 — a near-monoculture. The moat is habit and shelf presence, evidenced by a gross margin nine points above peer median [2] that survived a 2018 tariff with little lasting share loss8. Cash flow and shareholder economics

Owner yield — $1.65 owner EPS ÷ $25.94 = 6.3%13 — sits below the dossier's raw 7.5% TTM FCF yield14: owner EPS strips the $402M step-up back out, on the premise that a zero-growth whiskey business lays down barrels only to replace what it sells; raw FCF treats the step-up as recurring. My interpretation: likely between the two — inventory sits at 599 days against 430–481 days before the 2018–22 build15, suggesting a partial, one-time barrel release.

The latest quarter

Q1 FY2027: net sales −1% to $911M; operating income +4% organic (−3% reported); diluted EPS +6% to $0.38; gross margin +40bp to 60.2%, though management said that may be "the high point for the year"16. Guidance was reaffirmed, not raised. My interpretation: a beat that didn't move the outlook is not yet a turn.

The bull case and the counterargument

The strongest bull case is pricing power under stress: 60.5% gross margin held while emerging markets grew 14% to $974M and New Mix (tequila RTD) grew 41%, opening a new occasion instead of defending the old one17. The counterargument is economic: the category is shrinking under the brand — US spirits volume fell 5.1% in Q1 CY2026, premium tiers fell hardest (−8.8% to −9.3%), and Kentucky alone reportedly holds "over 13 million" barrels of oversupply, an industry "making nearly three times as much as it was currently selling"18.

Illustrative only: a 20% revenue decline against 60.5% gross margin and mostly-fixed costs cuts gross profit ~$475M and operating income toward $0.5B19. Evidence: margin and the 2018 tariff for the bull; −5% JDTW depletions [3] and the −3% to −5% guide20 for the bear. The moat weakens if margin breaks below ~58% while depletions stay negative.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Diluted shares fell 477.2M → 466.7M over four years, but $800M of buybacks bought only ~13M of them, at prices the record can't reconcile — one estimate implies ~$60–63, double today's price29. My interpretation: that is cannibalism, not discipline, favoring the dividend (3.56% yield, 34 straight years of increases30) as the more trustworthy return.
