Munger's Verdict

PEPSICO, INC. (PEP) — MUNGER SYNTHESIS

Analysis date 2026-10-05. Price $125.89 (2026-10-02 close), 0.6% above the 52-week low of $125.16 and 26.6% below the $171.48 high. Market cap ~$171.8B on 1,364.9M shares. Q3 results land on Oct 8, 2026, three days from now, so every "latest quarter" figure below is about to be replaced. Hurdle throughout is the dossier's local cost of equity of 9.74% (US 10-year 5.28% + Damodaran total ERP 4.46%, beta 1). The RXO and Enova items in the raw dossier are not about PepsiCo and are ignored. Provenance: Burry, Cook and the Psychologist ran on a substitute model pool. I judge their reports on their evidence, and none of my conclusions rests on one of them alone.

1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags raised. 5 MODERATE flags by: Buffett, Biologist, Historian, Anthropologist, Psychologist. 0 MINOR/NONE. Outside the Tribunal, Bezos, Burry, Cook, Jobs, Sherlock and the Futurist also say MODERATE. Only Lynch says MINOR. Here the agreement is real, because the experts reached it from different evidence and not from one calculation. The brand layer is intact: Pepsi consideration among 18–34s rose from 25.1 to 35.3 over 2021–26 (YouGov). The pricing layer gave way. Walmart is ~14% of revenue (~$13.1B), and it gave Frito-Lay shelf space to store brands and Takis. PepsiCo responded by cutting prices up to 15% on Lay's, Tostitos, Doritos and Cheetos from February 2026. A business that has to cut price 15% to win back volume still has a moat, but it does not own a toll bridge. The Historian calls the trajectory "undetermined, leaning survivor" (50/30/20), so the 14x Casualty cap does not apply. The rule gives the normal Quality range of 15x–18x. The Franchise Premium fails on two of its tests. GAAP operating margin is 12.2% (core 15.9%), and ROIC is 17.3% TTM and 14.7% in FY2025, short of both the 35% and 20% gates. Nothing is contracted (no backlog), so the recurring-revenue test fails too.

2. The Bull-Bear Balance

Lynch, answered on his own terms. Lynch's report is the strongest bull case in the file, and on the facts he is mostly right. The business is not broken. No segment loses money, and ROIC is back to 17.3% TTM. PBNA's 3.9% GAAP margin becomes 11.7% before $2,196M of listed charges, $1,523M of them "impairment and other". Q1 PFNA volume rose 2%, the first North American food volume growth in more than two years. Elliott's $4B stake (September 2025) forced the December 8, 2025 agreement to cut ~20% of the US lineup and close three plants. Lynch sizes GLP-1 at roughly 10% of households × ~5% of grocery spend, a ~0.5% drag (~1.5% in snacks). The Futurist's 0.2–0.5 points a year is the same calculation, not a second witness. Both multiply a hypothetical 10–15% adoption rate (FinancialContent, which the dossier labels conjecture) by the dossier's 5.3% grocery-spend study and by a 3× snack multiplier that each of them sets as JUDGMENT. I take it as one estimate built on a hypothetical adoption rate and a judgment multiplier. On that single estimate, GLP-1 slows growth but does not break the thesis. Nothing in the verdict depends on it. Lynch's own scenario table is what decides against him. His probability-weighted three-year return is ~8.9% a year, below the 9.74% hurdle. His bull case ($9.50 EPS in 2029 at ~19x, ~16% a year) needs both 7.4% EPS growth and a re-rating. With the 10-year at 5.28% and the dividend yield (4.70%) below it, I will not count on the re-rating. Lynch's evidence is why this is not a PASS or a SELL. His numbers are why it is not a BUY.

Every expert's BULL CASE. - Bezos: volume recovery and productivity raise FCF per share. - Buffett: price cuts bring volume back, restructuring lifts beverage margins, and growth turns into cash without new debt. - Burry: TTM FCF of $9.28B could support a return near the hurdle. - Cook: SG&A rose 0.5% against revenue growth of 2.2%, which is operating leverage. - Jobs: a simpler lineup and new drinks turn brand recognition into owner earnings. - Psychologist: snack volume recovers while international grows. - Sherlock: Elliott, the SKU cut and re-franchising lift margins back toward 10.8% and ROIC toward 17%. - Futurist: ~6–7% international organic growth plus productivity lift core EPS growth toward 7%. - Biologist: the price cuts restarted volume, and re-franchising frees capital. - Historian: the price cuts are the survivor's move, following the Coca-Cola re-franchising template. - Anthropologist: a 4.70% yield plus 5–7% core EPS growth clears the hurdle. - Lynch: about 16% a year to 2029.

All twelve depend on the same two things: North American volume holding after the price cuts, and margins rising anyway. Twelve bull cases are really one bet.

The bear case at full strength. Burry's fracture is real. Inventory rose 10.0% in FY2025 against COGS growth of 3.2%; DIO went from 46 to 50 days, and Cook's turnover fell from 8.5x to 7.7x. Acquisitions blur the picture: poppi ($1.65B) and Siete ($1.2B) added $804M of goodwill (to $18,916M), and PepsiCo also took on Alani Nu distribution. Revenue went $91.5B → $91.9B → $93.9B over FY2023–25. The cash argument is stronger: - Guided FY2026 dividends of ~$7.9B equal 103% of FY2025 FCF ($7.67B), and total cash returns of ~$8.9B equal 116%. - Borrowings rose ~$4.0B after FY-end, to $53.21B at June 30, 2026 (against cash of $10.72B). This is mostly seasonal. H1 2026 FCF was $1.09B, while H1 cash returns at the guided run-rate were ~$4.45B (half of $7.9B in dividends plus half of $1.0B in buybacks). That leaves a ~$3.4B H1 gap, which accounts for most of the $4.0B. Only a ~$0.6B residual is unexplained, plus any change in cash; FY-end cash is not in the dossier. The real point is the gap itself: the dividend is paid ahead of the seasonal cash and funded with debt until H2 arrives.

Management also softened its outlook. In December it expected "the high end" of 2–4% organic growth in H2. On the Q2 call it had "line of sight to the low end of our long term, 4%-6%", and said it must "optimize the return" on the price cuts. In other words, the volume was bought with price. The Q2 gap between reported and organic growth was 4.0 points against a guided ~2, so part of the growth was bought too. I weight the bull argument at 40% and the bear argument at 60%. The bears do not prove the business is declining. They prove the price assumes it will not.

The Private Buyer test. EV is ~$214.3B ($171.8B + $42.5B net debt). That is ~14.3x FY2025 GAAP EBITDA of ~$14.95B and ~14.4x core operating profit of $14,912M. Yahoo's 12.12x implies a higher TTM EBITDA of ~$17.7B, a different basis. A controlling buyer taxed at the guided ~22% core rate earns NOPAT of ~$11.6B. TTM capex of $4.17B exceeds D&A of $3.45B by $0.72B; subtract that and about $10.9B is left, a 5.1% unlevered yield. Capitalize that at an 8.0–8.5% cost of capital with 3.5% growth, haircut it for restructuring that recurs every year, and it supports roughly $124–141 a share [JUDGMENT]. So private-market value is at or a little above today's price, and well above my buy zone. The only reason for the difference is the cost of capital. A controller borrowing at ~8% with tax-deductible interest can pay today's price. A minority holder has no control, a dividend that yields less than Treasuries, and a payout above FCF, so must earn 9.74%. The same gap shows why Burry's short is the wrong trade. It would be a short on an asset priced near its private value, paying 4.70% a year in dividends, with an activist on the register. And nobody buys a $214B enterprise except to break it up. Lynch's sum-of-parts finds no breakup discount: PFNA at 15x leaves ~13.9x for everything else.

3. Decision Logic and Valuation

The ceiling (hidden value). Bezos finds no cash incinerator. Every segment is profitable, and shutting PBNA would remove a profitable 30.0% of revenue, not uncover hidden profit. The profit engine is PFNA: $27,528M of revenue, $6,173M of GAAP segment profit (22.4%), and $6,545M before charges. Burry's cost-dumping check is clean: SBC is 0.3% of revenue, and owner earnings already deduct it. Buffett and the Futurist both describe a moat being defended with money, not a widening one. No ceiling adjustment.

The floor (quality). - Buffett: "durable but under pressure". No pricing power and no anti-fragility. - Jobs: "Sugared Water", not insanely great. - Biologist: a "Symbiont (Healthy)" while its retail hosts reset the rent it can charge, and those hosts are consolidating. - Anthropologist: "Cultural Utility (Functional)". The brands survive the next generation, but legacy volume per household probably does not. - Cook: operations grade C. - Psychologist: management are "Owners" who name the pressure and act on it. - Sherlock: a "Dividend Annuitant in a Reluctant Reset", which moved only after the activist arrived.

A high-quality staple with a pressured core belongs at the bottom of the 15–18x range on owner earnings, with no raise.

The stress test. The dossier's adjusted stress test is anchored to FY2025 FCF of $7.67B. It shows FCF of $5.39B at a 10% revenue decline, $3.10B at −20% and $0.82B at −30%. Adjusted FCF turns negative at about a 35% revenue decline. The company survives a 20% decline with net debt at 2.8x EBITDA and interest covered 10.3x. But $3.10B of FCF against $7.9B of dividends means the dividend gets cut. So Burry's question has a split answer: the business survives, but the yield thesis does not.

The owner-earnings base. FY2025 owner earnings were $7.62B ($5.58 a share); TTM owner earnings were $8.78B ($6.43). The TTM figure is 15% higher, leans on a $4.70B FCF quarter in Q4 2025, and contains one-offs that cannot be dated. I use the midpoint, $8.20B, or $6.01 a share. The FY2022–25 average of $7.19B, $9.54B, $8.33B and $7.62B ($8.17B, or $5.99 a share) lands in the same place by a different route. FCF per share went from $5.24 in FY2024 to $5.60 in FY2025 and $6.80 TTM. GAAP EPS was $6.00 in FY2025 and $7.66 TTM, on net income of $8.24B and $10.45B. The dossier's cross-check did not verify the TTM figure, which is one more reason not to build on it.

The raw dossier's Yahoo page also shows quarterly actuals of $2.29, $2.26, $1.61 and $2.20. The last two match the reported core EPS growth of +9% and +4%, so these are core EPS: ~$8.36 TTM, a ~15.1x multiple, and a FY2026 consensus of $8.55 (14.7x). That is the most favorable multiple for the bulls, and it overstates what an owner actually receives, though by less than the $3.4B GAAP-to-core gap would suggest. Much of that gap is non-cash impairment: $1,946M of "impairment and other", $1,523M of it in PBNA. The recurring cash item that core earnings exclude is restructuring. The Q1 2026 10-Q shows restructuring cash payments of $145M and $232M for the two quarters it compares. Annualized, that is roughly $0.6–0.9B a year [JUDGMENT], or ~$0.33–0.53 a share after tax. The stronger reason is the company's own guide of FCF conversion of at least 80%: 80% of $8.36 is $6.69, close to TTM owner EPS of $6.43. Owner earnings are what pay the dividend.

The 4–6% core constant-currency EPS guide is the wrong yardstick for that, because it removes currency but keeps acquisitions. The Dec 8, 2025 outlook expected acquisitions to add ~1 point to FY2026 reported growth, and poppi plus Siete cost ~$2.85B. Owner earnings never deduct that spend, so capitalizing the full guide would treat bought growth as free and perpetual.

I therefore source base growth from organic measures: the 2–4% organic revenue guide (2.5% YTD) plus ~0.5 point from buybacks net of SBC and productivity [JUDGMENT], which gives 3.5%. As a cross-check, the 4–6% EPS guide less the ~1 point acquired is ~3–5%, and 3.5% sits in the lower half of that range, where my earlier 4% sat in the full guide. The other consistent treatment would keep 4% growth but deduct the 2025 acquisition spend, averaged over two years (~$1.4B a year), from owner earnings. That gives a base value of ~$90. I use the organic route, the more generous of the two. On that basis, the price asks for growth at or above the top of the organic-equivalent range, from a company whose North American organic revenue is −0.5%. 4. Final Decision

Decision: WAIT. The business clears the quality bar. The Tribunal raised no SEVERE flag, ROIC is 17%, distribution reaches 200+ countries, and 40.7% of revenue is international and growing organic volume. PASS (a quality call) would therefore be wrong. The price does not clear the bar. At $125.89, dividend yield plus the 4–6% guide gives 8.7–10.7% against a 9.74% hurdle. On organic-equivalent growth of 3–5% it gives 7.7–9.7%. That is fair value at best, with no margin of safety. I reject SELL: the stock trades at or below what a controlling buyer would pay, a short pays the 4.70% dividend, and Elliott is a catalyst. HOLD is an answer for existing owners. The 1.5–2% positions held by Sherlock, the Biologist, the Anthropologist and Lynch are reasonable for someone who already owns the stock, not for new money.

Moat Tribunal Result: Uncertain. The uncertainty is about pricing power, not the brand. MODERATE: Buffett, Biologist, Historian, Anthropologist and the Psychologist, joined by Bezos, Burry, Cook, Jobs, Sherlock and the Futurist. Lynch alone dissents toward MINOR, and no one argued SEVERE.

Buy Zone: $74–$92

  • Fair value limit, $92. This is the grid value at 3.0% growth and 9.74%, half a point below my 3.5% base. The half point is a margin for error in a growth rate that is itself a judgment. It is not a second charge for the dividend-cut risk, which the bear scenario already prices. At $92 the stock is 15.3x owner EPS, 12.0x TTM GAAP EPS and 11.0x TTM core EPS, with a 6.4% dividend yield and a 6.5% owner yield.
  • Absurdly cheap floor, $74. This is the grid value at ~1.5% perpetual growth: 12.3x owner EPS, 9.7x TTM GAAP EPS and 8.9x TTM core EPS, with a 9.2% TTM FCF yield and an 8.0% dividend yield. Buffett's $70–75 trigger and Burry's and Cook's ~$70 yield screens sit nearby. The pipeline's no-growth Graham floor of $51.48 sits below it.

I did not raise the floor for moat quality or the ceiling for hidden value. On owner earnings, the ceiling sits at the bottom of the 15–18x Quality range. The floor sits below that range: it is a Graham-style ~10x on TTM GAAP earnings. Both bounds come from the grid, not from the band.

Why is it priced here? Partly the price fell, and partly earnings recovered. Since March 31, price-to-sales has fallen from 2.27 to 1.90 (−16%), the cleanest measure of the price move. EV/EBITDA fell further, from 16.27x to 12.12x (−25%), but EV/revenue fell only 13% (2.69 → 2.33). So about half of the EV/EBITDA compression is TTM EBITDA recovering as the FY2025 charges roll off, not a de-rating. Price-to-book (10.41 → 8.30) also reflects a growing book. The stock got cheaper while earnings improved. That is the bull's point, and I concede it. I see two reasons for the ~16% price fall, both JUDGMENT: - A 5.28% Treasury out-yields the 4.70% dividend (the five-year average yield is 3.14%). The dossier does not date the rate move, so I cannot show it caused the fall. - The market learned that snack volume now has to be bought with price.

A bad Oct 8 print could push the price into the zone without anything changing in the franchise. That is the setup to wait for.

Moat half-life. About 20 years for the brand layer, based on YouGov's young-cohort gain, a 16% share of US liquid refreshment against Coca-Cola's 20%, and distribution in 200+ countries. About 7–10 years for pricing power, which erodes for three reasons: - Walmart and Costco are bringing private-label production in-house. - GLP-1 may take ~0.2–0.5 points a year off volume. This is one estimate, built on a hypothetical 10–15% adoption rate [MEDIA, conjecture] and a 3× snack multiplier [JUDGMENT]. - Regulation is reaching the snack aisle: SNAP restrictions are enacted, and front-of-pack labels, an ultra-processed-food definition and dye phase-outs are proposed.

The Historian's point-of-no-return list is the one to watch: dividends above FCF for a second year, North American organic growth negative after the price "optimization", impairments on poppi or Siete, and borrowings rising with no stated use.

Too Uncertain tripwires: 0 of 5 tripped. - No SEVERE flags. - The load-bearing variable is North American volume response to price, a commercial fact reported every quarter, not a regime event. - No expert passed on the grounds that the outcome is unknowable. - I never had to write "we can't know". - The buy zone is only 8% below my own central value.

The dominant variable can be calculated, and I calculated it.

Position Size: 0% at $125.89, with Moderate conviction. At or below $92 the position could go up to 3%, the Moderate cap. These items remain unresolved: - The discount rate: today's price equals my central value at ~8.5% and my fair-value limit at ~7.9%. At 9.0% it is still a WAIT. - North American volume: whether it holds after the price cuts. - Borrowings: the ~$0.6B residual of the ~$4.0B rise left after the seasonal H1 gap, plus any change in cash (FY-end cash is not in the dossier). - The dividend guide: $7.9B against $5.92 × 1,364.9M = ~$8.08B. A mid-year rate increase is the likely explanation, but the Q1 excerpt is truncated.

What would change the call at today's price. Three things would need to show up in the Oct 8 print and the Q4 print: - North American organic revenue positive without further price cuts. - Core operating margin up year on year. - H2 2026 FCF of at least ~$7.8B, the amount needed to cover $8.9B of cash returns after H1's $1.09B. H2 2025 delivered $8.18B.

If they do, my base growth rises from 3.5% to 4.0% and the central value to ~$105. That is still below $125.89. To buy at today's price you have two options. You can believe in ~4.7% organic per-share growth forever. Or you can accept a required return of ~8.5% (buying at my central value) or ~7.9% (buying at my fair-value limit), well over a point below 9.74%. I accept neither yet.

1. CULTURAL ROLE

PepsiCo is two different cultural objects under one roof, and the dossier says they should not be valued as one.

Convenient foods (58% of net revenue [SEC]; PFNA is 29.3%, LatAm Foods 11.2%, Asia Pacific Foods 4.9% [CALC]) are closest to a ritual with a utility floor. A true ritual does not need a 15% price cut to bring volume back. It reads as a habit that lapsed when the shelf price rose, which makes it a price-sensitive habit, not an identity.

Beverages (42% of net revenue [SEC]; PBNA 30.0%, IB Franchise 5.3% plus beverage share of the international segments) is a status-neutral utility with a tribal-rivalry overlay. Pepsi is the second most "Considered" carbonated brand in the US [MEDIA per YouGov]. Pepsi is the default alternative: culturally real but a number two. Gatorade is the one franchise with a plausible ritual role (sport and recovery), supported by Q2 Gatorade volume and revenue growth [MEDIA].

Brand-as-verb test. Nothing in the dossier shows "Pepsi," "Lay's," or "Doritos" functioning as a category verb or a cultural shorthand. This is a gap in the dossier, not evidence of absence, and I do not score it either way.

Embedded cultural moments. Celebration, sport, school lunch, stadium and restaurant fountain. The 10-K channel list (DSD, warehouses, distributors serving restaurants, schools, stadiums, e-commerce [SEC]) confirms the footprint. That footprint is exposed to exactly the moments policy is now targeting: school-lunch dye bans (West Virginia, California) and SNAP restrictions on soda and snacks [MEDIA per moat threat search].

2. TRIBAL DYNAMICS

Tribes. (a) Mass-market snackers and soda drinkers, household-led; (b) Gen Z and 18-34s, who are the target of Starry, Mountain Dew dirty soda and the Celsius/Alani Nu distribution [MEDIA]; (c) bottlers and retailers, who are the institutional tribe that matters most for revenue.

Captive vs Devotee test. The honest answer is that PepsiCo's consumer is closer to a devotee-lite and its shelf is closer to a captive. Evidence: - Consumers are not prisoners. Walmart reportedly warned that shoppers resisted higher prices and cut Frito-Lay shelf space for store brands and Takis [MEDIA]. Shoppers defected to private label and a challenger snack within one price cycle. Communities do not defect that fast. - The retailer is the gatekeeper, and it is concentrated: Walmart plus Sam's Club ~14% of net revenue, ~$13.1B [SEC; $ CALC], with loss of the customer described as a material adverse effect on PFNA and PBNA [SEC]. The cultural gatekeeper here is not an influencer. It is a buyer at Walmart who can re-slot a shelf, and who is also the subject of the private pricing lawsuit (Event B) [MEDIA]. - Carrefour is a 2024 event (delisting over price increases in four European countries, resolved by early April of that year) [MEDIA per AP via NY1]. It still shows that European retailers will walk away from a PepsiCo price increase. I treat it as a dated precedent, not a current datapoint.

Tribal resentment. The dossier has no NPS, churn or advocacy data (Steve Jobs extract: "NPS, churn and retention: not in the dossier"). Resentment is visible in a different form: the policy tribe. The San Francisco suit alleging manufacturers engineered addictive products for children names PepsiCo (dismissal secured for PepsiCo on July 2, 2026 [MEDIA per Weil]); the FDA/USDA ultra-processed food definition, the front-of-package labeling rule (0910-AI80) and the bipartisan UPF warning-label bill are all in motion [MEDIA, status Proposed]. A food company that becomes a political target is no longer a neutral cultural fixture. I flag this as the single biggest anthropological shift in the file.

3. GENERATIONAL ANALYSIS (the "My Kids" test)

Evidence for. Pepsi consideration among all US adults rose 23.4 -> 25.8 over five years, but among 18-34s it rose 25.1 (2021) -> 35.3 (2026), a 10.2-point gain vs 2.4 for all adults [MEDIA per YouGov BrandIndex]. The young cohort is adopting the brand faster than the old one. That is the opposite of a relic's pattern. Gen Z is one in five Americans with $143B of purchasing power [MEDIA per Food Institute / Consumer Goods Technology]. Digestive-health (prebiotic) soft drinks grew from $197M in the US (2020) to roughly $440M (2024) [MEDIA], and PepsiCo is playing in it (poppi bought for $1.65B [MEDIA], a prebiotic Pepsi launch, Starry), as is Coca-Cola (Simply Pop).

Evidence against / caveats. 1. Consideration is not consumption. There are no purchase or volume data by cohort in the dossier. A rise in consideration while North America beverage organic growth is +1% [MEDIA, period unstated] says brand warmth is not yet turning into volume. 2. 73% of Gen Z and Millennials value experiences over tangible products [MEDIA per Food Institute]. This is a generic survey statistic. It tells me the competitive set for the young consumer's wallet is wider than soda and chips, but it does not measure PepsiCo. 3. Generational behavior shift in the product layer is real and measured, not hypothetical: households with a GLP-1 user cut grocery spending ~5% within six months, a 5.3% reduction in a transaction-linked study [MEDIA, status Enacted in the moat table]. Healthier-snack consumption grew +33% vs +15% traditional (Innova) [MEDIA]. PepsiCo's answer has been to buy (poppi $1.65B, Siete $1.2B, ~$2.85B or ~1.7% of market cap [CALC]) rather than build, which is consistent with a preservation-oriented culture that absorbs the next generation's tastes after someone else has proven them.

Values the next generation prioritizes (transparency, health, authenticity, speed) versus what PepsiCo's core serves: the core snack and soda portfolio serves speed and indulgence, not transparency or health. The product-layer response (Gatorade Lower Sugar, Gatorlyte, prebiotic Pepsi, ~20% fewer US SKUs [MEDIA]) is real but small relative to the base.

Generational survival assessment: the brand survives the next generation; the volume case for the legacy snack and soda SKUs is what is contested. The brand layer passes the "My Kids" test. The product layer is not yet proved.

1. TAM vs SAM

  • TAM: not in the dossier. Nothing in it sizes a market that is expanding. Salty snacks, carbonated soft drinks, sports drinks and juices are mature categories where growth is price/mix plus emerging-market penetration.
  • SAM share: US liquid refreshment beverage share ~16% for PepsiCo against ~20% for Coca-Cola [SEC, citing IRI]. Carbonated soft drinks: Coca-Cola ~48% (2023) against PepsiCo ~26% [MEDIA per "Coke vs Pepsi Market Share Trend 2026"]. The company is #2 in beverages, with a franchise lead in salty snacks.
  • Where the profit pool is: PFNA earns $6,545M before listed charges, 38.8% of core segment profit of $16,865M, on 29.3% of revenue [CALC from segment table, SEC]. International Beverages Franchise earns a 37.1% margin on 5.3% of revenue. PBNA earns 11.7% before charges on 30.0% of revenue. 2. Workflow defensibility

This is not a workflow business. The dossier says so: lock-in is "not applicable", and Walmart Connect retail media is a channel tool, not lock-in [MEDIA per PepsiCo CMO interview]. What defends it instead is: - Distribution: direct-store-delivery, company-owned bottlers, 200+ countries [SEC]. - Shelf and promotional funding: rebates, placement fees, displays [SEC]. - Brand: Pepsi consideration among 18-34s rose from 25.1 to 35.3 over five years [MEDIA per YouGov BrandIndex].

Defensibility is that of a prosumer-grade habit with an industrial distribution advantage, not professional lock-in. One moat score set gives switching costs 4.8 and network effects 1.2, against intangibles 8.2 [MEDIA per Invetso, a single source]. The gatekeeper is Walmart at ~14% of revenue (~$13.1B), whose loss "would have a material adverse effect" on PFNA and PBNA [SEC]. That same customer is expanding private label and is the subject of the pricing litigation (Event B).

3. Structural vs cyclical

  • Cyclical or temporary:
  • The Q1 2026 return to North American food volume growth, the first in over two years [MEDIA], was bought with price cuts of up to 15% on Lay's, Tostitos, Doritos and Cheetos [MEDIA per So Yummy]. Volume that has to be purchased with price is a different asset from volume that arrives with price power.
  • Management said it needs to "optimize the return on some pricing investments" in the second half [MEDIA per Q2 call], and the high-end second-half organic growth it promised in December was walked back to "line of sight to the low end of 4-6%" [MEDIA].
  • Year-to-date reported growth of +7.3% against organic +2.5% shows a 4.8-point gap [CALC]. In Q1, PBNA's +9% was +2% organic and 7 points acquired [SEC]. Reported growth is partly purchased.
  • Structural headwinds in the dossier:
  • GLP-1: households with a user cut total grocery spend by about 5% within six months (5.3% in one linked-transaction study) [SEARCH per the threat table sources]. Status: enacted.
  • Healthier-snack consumption +33% against +15% for traditional [MEDIA per Innova via foodingredientsfirst.com].

  • Private label vertical integration at Walmart and Costco [enacted].
  • Structural tailwinds:
  • Emerging-market volume. Q2 organic volume grew in Asia Pacific Foods, EMEA and IB Franchise [SEC/MEDIA].
  • Digestive-health soda: $197M (2020) to ~$440M (2024) [SEARCH per the threat table], growth of about 2.2x. At 1.6% of PBNA's FY2025 revenue of $28.2B [CALC, years differ], that is a promising category and a rounding error.
  • Productivity: "record productivity savings" [SEC/MEDIA], a margin lever that cannot compound forever.

Net: the structural headwinds are concentrated in the highest-margin pool, and the tailwinds sit in lower-margin geographies and in small acquired brands.

4. The disintermediation scenario

The specific behavior changes that could disintermediate PepsiCo's core function: 1. GLP-1 receptor-agonist drugs reduce snack and soda consumption directly. This is a demand shift, not a bypass of the channel. 2. Retailer private label with in-house production (Walmart, Costco): the customer that controls shelf, price gap and retail-media access replaces the brand. Walmart is ~14% of revenue and cut Frito-Lay shelf space for store brands and Takis [MEDIA].

I cannot cite evidence in the dossier for AI shopping agents or any other technology-driven bypass, so I do not model one.

Sizing GLP-1 honestly. The hypothetical in the dossier is 10-15% of the US population on these drugs [MEDIA per FinancialContent, hypothetical]. At 5.3% of household grocery spend: 10-15% x 5.3% = 0.5-0.8% of grocery spend [CALC]. Even if snack and soda spend fell three times as much as total grocery (my assumption [JUDGMENT], the dossier has no snack-specific data), that is a 1.6-2.4% drag on volume, about 0.2-0.5 points a year over a decade. GLP-1 alone is a headwind, not a 20% scenario. A 20% decline requires GLP-1, private label, regulation and price war acting together. That is the scenario below.

7. Verdict

Does the future justify paying a premium today? About half of the price ($59.9-$68.6 per share) is a growth premium. It requires 4.1-5.1% perpetual growth in per-share cash flow, which is at or above the top of what a business with 2.5% YTD organic growth, flat-to-negative North America volume and a 2-4% organic guide can plausibly sustain without permanent margin expansion. International at roughly 6-7% organic is real and helps, but it is the lower-margin 40.7% of the company.

Is it achievable? At the low end (4%), yes. At 5% or more, only if the price investments restore volume without damaging margin, and if the re-franchising or restructuring agenda delivers. The probability-weighted value is $97-112 against a price of $125.89.

Building the future or defending the past? Defending, with a small and well-chosen option on the future (poppi, Siete, international). The most dangerous number in the file is dividend cover of 0.97x of FY2025 FCF (0.85x of TTM FCF), because the one thing the company cannot do in a volume downturn is keep paying it.

Growth verdict: Priced for Perfection, in a mild form. The stock is 26.6% below its high and within 0.6% of its 52-week low, so this is not a euphoric price. It is a fair price for an outcome that requires guidance to be met and no structural slippage in North American volume. I would wait for the price to compensate for the risk: roughly $100-112 at the 9.74% hurdle. The Oct 8 earnings report is the near-term event. A confirmation of volume growth without further price investment would raise my base-case growth and move the trigger up.

The fracture: inventory is building faster than the business

The clearest operating warning is inventory: it rose 10.0% in FY2025, against revenue growth of 2.2% and COGS growth of 3.2% [CALC]. DIO climbed from 46 days in 2023–24 to 50 days in 2025 [CALC]. That is evidence of slower inventory turnover, though the dossier cannot separate acquisition effects from underlying stock buildup.

Demand is soft where it matters most: North America represented 59.3% of FY2025 revenue [CALC], and North American organic revenue was down 0.5% in Q2 2026 [MEDIA]. Management said the second half required optimizing returns on pricing investments [MEDIA]. Price cuts and the reported return of food volume growth offer a credible recovery case, but those claims are [MEDIA], not a demonstrated, sustained volume trend.

The forensic checks do not show cooked cash flow

SBC was $0.29B in FY2025 [SEC], about 2.2% of TTM operating cash flow of $13.46B [CALC; periods differ]. It is not large enough here to explain away FCF: TTM FCF was $9.28B, while FY2025 FCF was $7.67B [CALC]. The owner earnings calculation also deducts SBC [CALC]. The adjustment-gap test is inconclusive. GAAP net income is provided, but adjusted EBITDA is not; the dossier therefore cannot establish whether an adjusted-earnings gap is widening. Accounts receivable is listed as unavailable [SEC], so there is no DSO or channel-stuffing test. Don’t infer a clean bill of health from those missing checks.

Capital returns become strained in the downside case

The dossier’s adjusted stress test estimates FCF of $3.10B after a 20% revenue decline, versus $7.67B at the base [CALC]. FCF turns negative at approximately a 35% decline [CALC]. The test does not model a five-year path, management actions, or balance-sheet evolution, so it cannot establish that the company would “implode.” It does show the cash buffer shrinking sharply under a large sales shock.

At the current guide, dividends of roughly $7.9B plus $1.0B of repurchases mean about $8.9B of cash returns [SEC]. That exceeds the stress-test FCF at a 20% revenue decline by about $5.8B [CALC]. Even at the FY2025 FCF level, guided returns exceed FCF by about $1.2B [CALC]. The dividend alone is 103% of FY2025 FCF [CALC]. The dividend guide also does not reconcile with the $5.92 trailing annual dividend multiplied by the latest 1,364.9M shares, which implies about $8.08B [CALC]. That discrepancy weakens confidence in the carry figures.

Debt is a pressure point, not yet the specific break

FY2025 debt maturities due inside 24 months total about $10.8B [CALC from the 10-K schedule]. June 2026 borrowings were $53.21B and cash plus short-term investments were $10.72B, for net debt of $42.50B [SEC]. That is material refinancing exposure, but the dossier does not provide current refinancing rates or a maturity schedule updated to June 2026. I cannot claim that refinancing alone breaks the thesis. Nor can the revenue stress table establish runway in years.

Valuation and short discipline

At $125.89, TTM FCF yield is 5.4% and owner yield is 5.1% [CALC]. Both are below the dossier’s 9.74% local cost of equity: the US 10-year of 5.28% plus a 4.46% total equity risk premium at beta 1 [DATA/CALC]. The TTM P/E is 16.4x [CALC], equivalent to an earnings yield of about 6.1% [CALC], also below that hurdle. The yield gap supports caution, but it is not proof of overvaluation by itself: management guides core constant-currency EPS growth of 4–6% [SEC], and that growth could close the return gap if delivered.

A rough price at which TTM FCF yield reaches 9.74% is about $70 per share, using TTM FCF of $9.28B and 1,364.9M shares [CALC]. This is a yield-based screening threshold, not a forecast or a full intrinsic-value estimate.

Verdict

The evidence supports a small, risk-controlled short, not a claim of accounting fraud. The thesis rests on a concrete demand and working-capital fracture: North American organic revenue fell in Q2 while FY2025 inventory and DIO rose faster than sales. In a 20% revenue decline, the dossier’s own stress test leaves FCF below guided cash returns. The thesis fails if lower prices restore sustained North American volume and productivity savings lift cash generation enough to cover dividends from FCF.
