Munger's Verdict

CSU.TO — Constellation Software Inc. — Munger Synthesis (revised after Reality Check, Pass 1)

Date: 2026-09-26. Price: CAD 2,872.98 (TSX). The pipeline labels the company's financials CAD, but they are USD, because CSU reports in USD. I convert at ~1.38 CAD per USD only where I compare a financial figure with the CAD share price.

The whole council admires this business, and almost none of it wants to buy at this price. Of twelve experts, eleven raise a MODERATE moat flag, one raises a MINOR flag, and none raises a SEVERE flag. The vote is 2 BUY, 1 WAIT, 4 HOLD, 4 PASS and 1 SELL. The PASS votes do not grade quality: Buffett calls CSU "a high-quality acquirer with a credible portfolio moat, not a cigar butt." They reject one number that nobody could pin down: how much cash belongs to one CSU common share. So I start there.

1. Fixing the arithmetic before judging anything

The dossier stacks three errors, and the council split along them.

Currency. CSU's statements are "presented in U.S. dollars, which is Constellation Software Inc.'s functional currency" (note 2(c)). The pipeline's "CAD 11.62B" 2025 revenue is the company's own $11,623 million, while the CAD 2,872.98 price is real Canadian money. So the 6.1% owner yield is $3.72B of USD owner earnings ÷ 21M shares ÷ a CAD price.

Four experts explicitly called that ratio currency-safe: Sherlock, Futurist, Lynch and the Anthropologist. The Historian and the Biologist used it without making the claim. Five caught the mix: Burry, Bezos, Cook, the Psychologist, and Buffett ("a ratio is protected from currency error only when both inputs use the same currency"). Correcting the currency alone turns 6.1% into 8.5%, so the ratio was never safe in either direction.

Minority interests. The pipeline's 2025 net income of $0.72B is total consolidated net income before non-controlling interests, and it matches AlphaQuery's $715.62M. Net income attributable to CSU common shareholders was $512M, or $24.15 per diluted share. The pipeline's $720M sits 40.6% above $512M (39.8% using $715.62M); the roughly $200M+ difference belongs to minority holders of Topicus.com and Lumine Group. If per-share math does not strip that roughly 30% minority slice, it is inflated. I use $512M and $24.15 as the common-shareholder anchor. I apply 512/715.62 = 71.5% as a proxy to cash and to claims (see the debt bridge below). It is a proxy, and cash attribution could differ.

Two cash flows. Financial Physics shows TTM free cash flow of $3.92B (owner earnings $3.72B). The four cash-conversion quarters are:

  • For FY2025 to reach $3.77B, H1 2025 FCF would have to be $2.33B.
  • Q2 2025 operating cash flow was about $0.43B (implied by Q2 2026's +10.6%), so Q2 2025 FCF was about $0.41B.
  • That leaves Q1 2025 at about $1.92B, against $0.88B in Q1 2026, in a year when revenue grew about 20%.

I therefore treat $3.92B as a presumed definitional mismatch pending the company's reported consolidated operating cash flow line, not as a rival measurement. The dossier does not show what the Physics block adds back. I use $2.77B, which sums quarter by quarter (Cook verified it).

The result. Attributable owner earnings are $2.77B × 71.5% = $1.98B. That is $94.37 per share, or CAD 130.23.

This figure is unlevered. It is operating cash flow less capex, taken before lease payments (which IFRS 16 puts in financing). It is also before interest, if CSU classifies interest paid in financing; the dossier does not say which. Set against enterprise value (price plus CAD 138.23 of attributed net debt and leases, derived below), the unlevered yield is 4.3%, or 4.5% on the share price alone. That is not the pipeline's 6.1%, and it is not the 6.3% consolidated FCF yield Bezos, Cook and Burry computed before the minority haircut. An equity yield after interest would be lower still. Against the 8–10% local cost of equity (Canadian risk-free rate of ~3–3.5% plus a 5–6% equity risk premium), the zero-growth case fails either way; the question is entirely growth.

Cook's 14% quarter. Q2 2025 was about $0.43B of operating cash flow on $2,844M of revenue, a 15% margin, so the same quarter was weak a year ago. The year-over-year slippage is about one point, not eight. The dossier's flag (+17.3% revenue against +10.6% operating cash flow) is still one quarter.

Forensics.

  • Receivables went $0.89B → $1.14B → $1.29B → $1.47B from 2022 to 2025. That is +65% against +38% revenue growth ($8.4B → $11.6B), and days sales outstanding rose from ~39 to 46 (Burry).
  • Payables rose faster: $1.08B → $1.43B → $1.59B → $1.94B (+80%). Days payable outstanding went 92, 95, 90 and 98 against cost of goods of $4.30B, $5.46B, $6.45B and $7.25B. Working capital has been a source of cash, and part of the operating-cash growth is supplier credit that will not compound forever.
  • Inventory of $0.05B–$0.07B (days inventory falling from 4 to 3) is immaterial.
  • Debt and goodwill. Debt rose $2.29B → $3.68B → $4.53B → $5.79B while goodwill went $0.8B → $1.3B → $1.5B → $1.8B.
  • The debt bridge. At 2026-06-30, borrowings were $5.38B and cash $2.89B, giving net debt of $2.49B. Add $0.45B of leases, which sit in financing under IFRS 16 and so must be counted as debt, for $2.94B.
  • The dossier does not split borrowings between the holding company and Topicus/Lumine. So I charge common shareholders the same 71.5% I credit them with on cash: $2.10B, or CAD 138.23 per share.
  • The IRGA/TSS membership liability is a debt-like claim of minority and former owners. The dossier names it only through a 2025 revaluation charge and gives no balance, so it is flagged absent. Every $1B of it would lower each zone figure by CAD 47 (if attributed at 71.5%) to CAD 66 (if charged in full).
  • Debt maturities are not disclosed.
  • Stock pay and R&D print 0.0% every year. That is an unverified data gap, plausibly because CSU prefers mandatory management share purchases and R&D is buried in operating-group costs. The peer block's 0.0% versus a 9.0% median proves nothing.
  • Share count is flat at 21M from 2022 to 2025, with no buyback data.
  • Amortization. Total depreciation and amortization of $1,383M, mostly amortization of acquired intangibles, dwarfs $0.08B of capex, so GAAP understates cash.

The P/E. The 45.1x P/E against a 21.4x peer median (+23.6x) survives the corrections by coincidence. TTM net income of $1.36B × 71.5% ÷ 21M × 1.38 = CAD 63.94 per attributable share, or 44.9x. Buffett's 86x uses FY2025's depressed $24.15.

2. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags raised by: none. 5 MODERATE flags by: Buffett, Biologist, Historian, Anthropologist, Psychologist. 0 MINOR/NONE. Beyond the tribunal, Bezos, Burry, Cook, Jobs, Sherlock and Lynch are also MODERATE, and the Futurist is MINOR.

The Historian returns Survivor Trajectory at 65%, so the 14x cap does not apply.

The moat is real but unmeasured:

  • What supports it: switching costs across 140 industries described as "extremely high" (E024); micro-verticals "too constrained to justify VC funding for disruptive startups," a scale barrier (E025); and 77% recurring revenue.
  • What is missing: no net revenue retention, churn, NPS, price-increase or customer-ROI data exist, and Burry's customer-economics blind spot stands.
  • The threat: one source (LongYield) estimates 25–35% of the portfolio is AI-exposed.

That calls for the normal Quality Floor of 15–18x.

Franchise Premium fails criterion 2.

  • Margin route: the 7.6% TTM figure is a net margin, the dossier shows no operating margin above 35%, and CSU already failed this route on 2026-09-24.
  • ROIC route: 20.1%, 7.9%, 18.6%, 15.0% and 18.7% for 2022 through TTM clear 20% in one year of five. The peer block's differently defined 6.8% (peer median 11.4%) cannot be averaged in. Goodwill of $1.8B stays in the denominator.

This rule decision matters. The Franchise band's 25x top would put the ceiling at CAD 3,118, above the price (§4).

3. Bull–Bear Balance and the Private Buyer

Every BULL CASE line:

  • Bezos: retention plus disciplined acquisitions compound cash per share without issuing shares.
  • Buffett: cash flow compounds well above the conservative growth in his trigger price.
  • Burry: the 2025 dip proves temporary.
  • Cook: the recovery of the 2026 acquisition cohort persists.
  • Jobs: AI makes the embedded products more useful.
  • Psychologist: the embedded habits hold while AI improves workflows.
  • Sherlock: a rational allocator with a new lane, PEMS (Permanent Engaged Minority Shareholder stakes).
  • Futurist: PEMS plus AI upskilling keep redeployment at 18.7% ROIC.
  • Biologist: AI stays at the pilot stage while ~70,000 targets absorb ~$1.6B a quarter.
  • Historian: CSU compounds through the cycle like Roper, Bloomberg and Adobe rather than stalling like Computer Associates.
  • Anthropologist: the moat needs captivity, not devotion.
  • Lynch: a 91%-retention machine priced on AI fear.

Lynch is right on five points.

  1. The 2025 dip was one-off. Margins ran 7.7% → 6.7% → 7.3% → 4.4% → 7.6% TTM, and net income $0.72B → $0.80B → $1.03B → $0.72B → $1.36B. The 2025 dip coincides with named one-off items: impairments, a revaluation of the "IRGA / Membership" liability, and an equity-method investment written down to cost. Q1 2026 adds payroll taxes and Synchronoss costs at Lumine (a $309M acquisition). The 2026 acquisition cohort went from −16% to +16% margin in one quarter. TTM ROIC of 18.7% and margin of 7.6% are back within the 2022–2024 range.
  2. The company's own measure is surging. FCFA2S (free cash flow available to shareholders) rose 57% year over year in Q2 2026 and 48% in H1. This is from the company's release, which the dossier tags [SEC] for a SEDAR filer. It is the strongest bull evidence in the file, and it is why I will not join Burry's short.
  3. PEMS is unpriced. CSU owns ~12.7% of Sabre and took a board seat in March 2026.
  4. Succession went to an insider. Mark Miller, president since 2025, has been "a driving force for over thirty years," and Mark Leonard remains a director.
  5. GAAP P/E is the wrong lens.

Lynch is wrong on arithmetic. His "owner yield plus growth" (6.1% + 8% = 14.1%) double-counts. The dividend-growth formula adds the distributed yield to growth. CSU distributes 9% of earnings (dividend CAD 5.60, a 0.19% yield), and the other 91% buys the growth. At a constant multiple, his bear case returns about 8.2% and his base about 15%. His 25% owner-earnings compound rate ($1.63B → $3.72B) also rests on the Physics series I treat as a definitional mismatch.

The bear case is structural, not accounting.

  • Organic growth is slow. It was 6% in Q1 (2% after currency) and 4% in constant currency for Q2 recurring revenue excluding Altera (~5% normalized). That is below the historical 5–6% range, and one source says "~2% for years." Currency added 2–3 points in H1. Acquisitions supply 14–18 of the 17–20 headline points.
  • Q2 revenue mix. Revenue was $3,335M, up 17% (+$491M from $2,844M):
  • Maintenance: $2,554M versus $2,144M (77% of revenue).
  • Services: $573M versus $532M (+8%).
  • Licenses: $92M versus $87M (+5%).
  • Hardware: $116M versus $80M (+44%, but only 3.5% of revenue).
  • Quarterly run-rate. Revenue went $2.9B → $3.2B (+8%) → $3.2B (+0%) → $3.3B (+5%), a $13B run-rate that is +5.5% over TTM.
  • Peers. Against the peers, CSU's revenue growth is 15.5% versus 15.6%, its FCF margin 22.9% versus 34.4%, and its gross margin 37.6% versus 77.6% (a mix difference).
  • The threat register, in its own words:
  • Competition for vertical-software acquisitions "may increase purchase prices" (#5). Its evidence says private valuations "have risen accordingly" at the market level.
  • Sourcing "may become more difficult" across ~70,000 targets (#6).
  • Aptean competes directly (#4).
  • All three carry Enacted status, while AI is Speculative.
  • The real analogue. The Historian's Computer Associates case, a roll-up running out of road, is therefore the right thing to watch. But no compression shows in CSU's measured returns yet: TTM ROIC is back at 18.7%.
  • Redeployment exceeds cash. CSU closed $809M of deals in Q1 and committed $786M in Q2 (~$1.6B), which runs ahead of attributable free cash.

I weight the bull argument at 50% and the bear argument at 50%. Lynch's arithmetic fails, but his operating evidence stands. The bear case is a set of preconditions, not a measured decline.

The Private Buyer Test. As a JUDGMENT, a control buyer would pay 20–25x attributable owner earnings for mature, mission-critical vertical software. That gives CAD 20 × 130.23 − 138 = 2,467 to 25 × 130.23 − 138 = 3,118.

Private value brackets today's price, and so does my central value of CAD 2,871. Buyers and I roughly agree on value; we disagree on margin of safety. A CSU minority holder does get the redeployment engine, through the company. What a public buyer at a 10% hurdle lacks is the leverage and the lower hurdle a sponsor accepts, and the cash reconciliation and retention data I still need. The private-equity bid that supports this range is also the force the register says may raise CSU's own purchase prices.

The verdict is set by the ceiling rule, not by the central value. No growth input enters the ceiling. Within the ledger's ranges, nothing moves the ceiling to the price. It gets there only in three cases:

Central value: CAD 2,871. Owner EPS grows at g = organic + reinvestment × incremental return. The inputs are:

  • Organic growth: 3.5%, the midpoint of the 2–5% organic range.
  • Reinvestment: 91%, the dossier's retention rate.
  • Incremental return on redeployed cash: 12%, a JUDGMENT.

That gives g = 3.5% + 0.91 × 12% = 14.4%, and I apply an 18x exit, a 9% discount rate, the CAD 5.60 dividend and less CAD 138.23 of debt.

Why 12%. The recent returns set the upper bound: TTM ROIC is 18.7% and the five-year mean is 16.1%. Both average over deals struck at older prices, and the register's evidence says private valuations have risen, so the next dollar may earn less. The earlier draft's implicit 8–10% equalled my own cost of capital. Nothing measured supports that, so I withdrew it. The dossier also carries the growth rate ROE 23.3% × retention 91% = 21.2%, but it flags that figure as an upper bound, which is why I build growth from its parts instead.

Central value crosses the price at an incremental return of about 12.0%. Today's price is roughly my central case: an expected return near 9%, inside the 8–10% hurdle band, with no margin of safety.

6. Final Decision

Decision: WAIT. CAD 2,872.98 is about what my central case says CSU is worth. On the same equity bridge, earning 10% requires 15.5% compounding at an 18x exit. My central 14.4% falls just short, for an expected return near 9%. A fair price for a good business is not a margin of safety, and the rule band's ceiling is CAD 2,206.

Moat Tribunal Result: Strong, unproven. All five tribunal experts agree the moat is real and none calls it SEVERE. The Futurist is the lone MINOR flag; every other expert raises a MODERATE flag citing missing retention data or AI exposure.

Buy Zone: $1,164–$2,206 (CAD per TSX share). I kept the Graham Floor at 10x because the Franchise Premium fails the ROIC route. I did not raise the ceiling for hidden value because the consolidated cash line is unreconciled. At CAD 1,815 (15x) the case is compelling on the lower cash figure.

Position Size: 0%. Two separate tests govern the scaling:

  • Test (a), the consolidated cash basis: the company's reported consolidated operating cash flow in its four interim statements, Q3'25 through Q2'26. If it sums to about $2.85B (FCF ~$2.77B), the basis stands. If it sums to about $4.0B, the Physics series is right after all.
  • Test (b), attribution: replace the 71.5% × (OCF − capex) proxy with TTM FCFA2S per share as the numerator. FCFA2S is already after interest, lease payments, minority claims and IRGA/TSS payments. When it becomes the numerator, the attribution factor, the lease liability, the IRGA/TSS placeholder and the net-debt charge all drop out of the bridge, and value becomes multiple × FCFA2S per share. FCFA2S will print below both consolidated figures by construction, so it can never "refute" test (a).

Sizing:

  • At or below CAD 2,206: a 3% position (Moderate conviction).
  • At or below CAD 1,815: up to 5%, if test (a) confirms $2.77B and 15x TTM FCFA2S per share (in CAD) is at least the purchase price.

Still open: the consolidated cash basis, cash attribution, the incremental return on redeployed cash (9–18.7%), the exit multiple (15–22x), the IRGA/TSS liability (absent), and AI substitution in the 25–35% exposed slice.

Why would it be mispriced at the ceiling? Fear and complexity. The stock is already down 31% from CAD 4,150.66 on AI fear. The complexity cuts both ways:

  • The currency mislabel hides value.
  • The minority interests inflate value, because 28.5% of consolidated income belongs to others.
  • Purchase accounting depresses GAAP earnings ($1,383M of depreciation and amortization).

1. Cultural Role

CSU's products are utility, full stop — they run payroll, permitting, dispatch, and back-office workflows for governments, utilities, hospitality operators, dealerships, and field-service firms across 140 industries [MEDIA].
