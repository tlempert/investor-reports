3. Decision Logic — Ceiling and Floor

Ceiling: no raise for hidden value. - No cash incinerator. In Bezos' shutdown test, OXXO Mexico (Ps. 8,649M) plus KOF (MXN 10.7B) earn Ps. 19.35B against a consolidated Ps. 19.11B. Every other segment nets to −Ps. 0.24B, so closing them all lifts quarterly operating profit by only about 1.3%. - Cost-dumping cannot be tested. Burry has no segment cost allocation, and the receivables and goodwill fields are defective zeros. - No widening moat. Neither Buffett nor the Futurist finds one. - The retained Heineken stake is the one unvalued asset. FEMSA sold 5.24% for about €2.5B. Sherlock shows that justifying the price at 12.5% needs about $14.5B, or about $43 per ADS, of value outside the DCFs. At the sale price that is roughly 25–30 Heineken points, and nothing in the record suggests a stake that large.

Floor: quality band bottom. - Buffett: some pricing capacity but no clean price test (the +7.4% ticket against about 4% inflation includes basket mix), and no pass on the chaos test. - Jobs: "Sugared Water" — a good habit, but no metric shows customers love it. - Biologist: "Symbiont (Healthy)", with an 88% chance the competitive position is intact in 10 years. - Anthropologist: "Cultural Institution (Enduring)", but "priced rather than visited": 1Q26 ticket was +6.6% while traffic was −0.5%. - Cook: operations grade C−. Inventory days rose from 50 to 54, inventory grew 16% against COGS +8.7%, and gross margin fell 60 bps to 40.1%.

That is a high-quality core inside a mediocre consolidated machine. FCF margin is 3.5%. Operating margin fell 2.75 points in four years. Over 2023–25, FCF went $0.64B → $1.33B → $1.60B while revenue went $38.7B → $43.1B → $46.3B. FY2022 FCF was $2.27B, but the dossier gives no FY2022 revenue to set it against.

I charge that weakness once, in the base year. - The base is not a trough. Owner earnings ran 2.53%, 4.64% and 3.67% of revenue over 2023–25. The average, 3.61%, times FY2025 revenue gives $1.67B, against the $1.70B base. So the base needs no uplift and no further cut. - The decline is permanent at the consolidated level, not a capex cycle. Capex is about equal to D&A and is being cut (1H26 −16.2%), so nothing is being built against validated demand. The margin drop comes from mix: the expansion segments now net to about zero operating income. - The band position comes from the (hurdle, growth) grid in section 4, not from the margin series. The 2Q26 turn enters there, as the high end of the growth sweep. The Tribunal band is 15–18x and the Graham floor 10x.

Stress test. The dossier has no STRESS TEST block (scorecard item 10), so I derived one. Inputs: FY2025 revenue Ps. 841.0B (about $46.3B), EBIT about $3.87B, FCF $1.60B, a 25–35% decremental margin and a 30% statutory marginal tax rate. On those inputs FCF before lease cash turns negative at roughly a 14–20% revenue decline, with capex held at $2.12B (about equal to D&A of $2.17B). Lease cash is fixed in a downturn and sits outside that FCF, so the true breakpoint comes earlier, by an amount the dossier does not size. The test should be re-run once the 20-F gives lease payments. Cutting capex toward maintenance extends the breakpoint. Burry's 20% test: the business survives, with Fitch 'A' and net debt of $7.52B including leases (about 1.25x the roughly $6.0B of EBITDA), but the dividend does not. For a USD holder, the likelier 20% shock is the peso, not volume. FY2025 revenue grew 7.6% in pesos but 24.63% in actual-rate USD, and the currency can move the other way.

To be plain: no price in the dossier's two-year record reaches the zone. The low was $75.16 and the average $99.89. A buy needs a price below that whole range, which means one of those Fear catalysts has to arrive first. Waiting may mean waiting a long time, and that is acceptable.

So the replication time is a floor on the half-life, and the erosion that does occur comes from taxes, discount formats and traffic, not from replication. KOF's half-life is the Coca-Cola contract's, shortened by a state that now taxes "light/zero" drinks too. The capital-allocation risk is shorter. A family trust holds about 39%, the new CEO began his tenure this year, and expansion segments with falling operating income sit beside a dividend paid partly from asset sales. That is the Historian's Tesco and Sears rhyme, and the Psychologist's "Owners, provisionally."

FMX (FEMSA) - Ecosystem Biologist Analysis: Competitive Ecology

Scope note. The biological question (is this a healthy species?) and the investment question (is the price right?) are separate. The species looks reasonably healthy. The price assumes a discount rate the dossier's own cost-of-equity work does not support. A healthy species can be a bad purchase at the wrong price.

Data caveats that bind this analysis (from the dossier, not mine): net income is basis-ambiguous (majority vs consolidated, $1.07B vs ~$1.91B inferred); the forensic block's zeros for debt, receivables and goodwill and the zero accounts payable are defects, not facts; the competitive-landscape section is a results recap; there is no franchisee or store-level economics, so the rent extraction rate is not measurable. I use the peso-based segment facts where I can, and name the basis of every dollar figure.

1. SPECIES CLASSIFICATION

FEMSA is not one organism. It is a colony of two mature organisms with a set of young, unproven growths attached.

Organism A: OXXO Mexico (proximity retail). About 38% of implied consolidated revenue and about 45% of operating income in 2Q26 (Ps. 86,708M revenue, Ps. 8,649M profit) [CALC for shares, SEARCH per Zacks]. 24,455 stores, 3.5-3.8% annual store growth. Biologically this is a symbiont with its consumer host, edging toward keystone in its niche: the dossier's evidence is density (91% penetration claimed for Tiendas 3B, a competitor, shows the niche is crowded, but OXXO's own network is the benchmark) and daily-ritual purchase frequency. The customer is 100% individual consumers at the till, with no contract lock-in [ESTIMATED, UNVERIFIED]. That matters for the lock-in versus love test: nobody is locked in. A customer who stops going to OXXO pays no switching cost. So any durability is habit and convenience (love-like), not captivity, which is the healthier form, and also the more fragile one, because habit can be redirected by a cheaper or closer alternative.

Organism B: Coca-Cola FEMSA (KOF), the bottler. Revenue Ps. 76,318M, operating income about MXN 10.7B, about 56% of consolidated Ps. 19.11B operating income [CALC]. KOF is a dependent symbiont: it lives inside the Coca-Cola Company's franchise, with territory rights ("no entry without The Coca-Cola Company's approval" [MEDIA]). The host (the brand owner) decides the rules. A bottler is not a keystone: remove KOF and the Coca-Cola Company simply re-licenses the territory. Remove the Coca-Cola Company and KOF has a distribution fleet without a product. The dependency is asymmetric. KOF is also the organism in the state's crosshairs (IEPS).

Note OXXO profit plus KOF operating income is Ps. 19.35B against consolidated Ps. 19.11B [CALC]: the five-segment colony earns essentially all of its profit from two organisms, and the rest (Americas & Mobility, Europe, Health, Spin, corporate) nets to about -Ps. 0.24B. That is a colony with two functioning organs and several growths not yet paying their metabolic cost. Operating income declined in Americas & Mobility, Europe and Health in 2Q26 [SEARCH per Zacks / FEMSA release].

Parasite test. Is FEMSA extracting rent from captive hosts? Mostly no on the OXXO side: no captive customers, average ticket +7.4% includes price and basket mix (headline inflation ~4%, core "stubborn" [MEDIA]), so part of the ticket gain is real pricing, but traffic was -0.5% in 1Q26 and +2.0% in 2Q26, so the host is not being squeezed to exit. On the KOF side the extraction is by the state and the franchisor, not by KOF. The one place a parasitic reading is plausible is Spin (consumer credit using "FEMSA's customer data and ecosystem" [SEARCH per CFO commentary]): credit to the unbanked is rent extraction from a host that has little alternative. It carries non-creditable tax losses and an undisclosed book. Treat as an unproven growth, not a core organ.

Classification: Symbiont (OXXO), dependent symbiont (KOF), with a non-trivial growth of unproven expansion tissue.

2. HOST HEALTH

  • Host population (Mexican consumer). Dossier: "soft but stabilizing" per the CEO; wages and remittances helping; Banxico rates cited at 7.5-11% (range as stated) dampening spending [MEDIA]. Minimum wage up 26.6% over two years ($248.93 to $315.04 per the threat register), which is a tailwind to the host's purchasing power and simultaneously a cost headwind to OXXO's staffing. Net: the host is stable-to-modestly-growing, not shrinking.
  • Host consolidating? No. The host is atomized (individual consumers). Leverage over the customer is neither gained nor lost through consolidation. The relevant consolidation is on the competitor side: Walmart Mexico earmarked US$6B for expansion, Chedraui plans 140 openings in secondary cities [MEDIA].
  • Could the host evolve past this species? Two channels. (i) Hard-discount convenience: Tiendas 3B has 24 visits per quarter vs Bodega Aurrera's 21 but a basket of six items at MX$114 vs Bodega Aurrera's 11 items at MX$325 [MEDIA]. Bodega Aurrera wins the larger basket, Tiendas 3B wins frequency, which is OXXO's own game. A rival that matches OXXO's frequency at a lower price point is the closest thing to a direct predator in the dossier. (ii) Government-run retail: 25,000+ "Tiendas del Bienestar" in rural areas [MEDIA]. These target areas "not reached by private stores", so they are not today's OXXO host territory, but a state that funds 25,000 stores and buys and brands staples is a state that can expand its mandate.
  • Rent extraction rate. Not measurable: no store-level franchise P&L, no KOF customer data (dossier scorecard item 7 is a failure). The proxies: OXXO Mexico gross margin pressure (-60 bps consolidated gross margin to 40.1% in 2Q26), and operating profit +12.3% on revenue +11.8%, which implies roughly flat-to-slightly-rising operating margin. The extraction rate is not rising fast, and not falling either. 1Q26 showed OXXO operating income +20.92% on revenue +8.26% [MEDIA], with adjusted EBITDA margin 14.2% vs 12.4%: strong operating leverage in 1Q, less in 2Q.
  • Customer-consumption health. Sugary-drink consumption is the problem: front-of-package warning labels (enacted 2020, "significant sales-related reductions" without amounts), a school ban on sweetened beverages (proposed), GLP-1 adoption (speculative; 7-11% processed-snack and beverage spending reduction among adopters per one source), and the IEPS increase. KOF volume +2.4% in 2Q26 and record December volumes suggest the host is still consuming, but the ecosystem's regulatory pressure is directional and compounding.

3. INVASIVE SPECIES THREAT

Ranking by the dossier's own status labels, and by how fast I would expect them to colonize:

  1. IEPS tax, enacted. Sugar-sweetened beverage tax from MX$1.64 to MX$3.08 per liter, "about 87%"; cigarettes ad valorem 200% from 160%; for the first time "light" or "zero" beverages included [truncated snippet]. This is not an invasive species, it is a change in the climate. It strikes KOF, which is more than half of operating income. The dossier gives no pass-through assumption, no elasticity, no date for the effective impact, and no amount, so I cannot size it in dollars from the dossier. What I can say: an 87% rise in a per-liter tax is far larger than a ~4% headline inflation, so full pass-through means a price increase that the host will notice, and partial pass-through means margin compression. Either path hurts the organism that carries the most profit.
  2. Hard discount and big-box (Tiendas 3B, Bodega Aurrera, Walmart's US$6B, Chedraui's 140 openings). Same rules, same niche, direct competition. Colonization speed in retail is slow (store build-out takes years) but they are already established. This is an ordinary predator, which the incumbent has seen before.
  3. State retail (Tiendas del Bienestar). Different rules entirely: no profit constraint, subsidized procurement. Currently in a different niche (rural). Low immediate threat, tail risk.
  4. Quick-commerce platforms (Mercado Libre, Rappi, iFood, Amazon, Chinese low-cost platforms). Status: speculative. These are the classic "adjacent ecosystem crossing over" species. Convenience retail's moat is "I need it now and it is 200 meters away"; quick commerce attacks the same need with logistics. Colonization in LatAm has been slower than hyped, because delivery economics are poor in low-ticket baskets. A 10-year horizon is long enough that this is a real risk, a 3-year horizon makes it minor.
  5. USMCA review, labor cost, security. Environmental stressors rather than invaders. Labor costs: 26.6% cumulative minimum wage rise and a staged 40-hour week; non-deductible OXXO Mexico labor cost already pushed the 2Q26 effective tax rate to 34.8% versus a 30% statutory rate [SEARCH per FEMSA 2Q26]. Extortion and gang violence: stores closed in Nuevo Laredo, which is a localized, not systemic, effect in the dossier, but it is a cost on the unit economics of a store network.
  6. GLP-1, front-of-pack labels, school ban. Slow-acting climate change in the beverage and snack habitat. The label law is already enacted and apparently already priced into KOF's numbers (volume still +2.4%).

The peso moves reported margins and net income by non-cash amounts (9M2025 FX loss Ps. 4,917M vs 9M2024 gain Ps. 9,258M [SEARCH per FEMSA 3Q25]). Diversifying or concentrating? The ecosystem is concentrating on the competitor side (Walmart, Chedraui) and diversifying on the consumer-channel side (quick commerce, discount, state). For FEMSA itself, management is deliberately diversifying (Valora, Delek, Health, Spin, a new segment structure), but the diversification is into tissue that currently earns about zero operating income in aggregate. A biologist would call that speculative budding, not yet differentiation.

Capex was cut 16.2% in 1H26 (Ps. 15,067M, 3.4% of sales), "reflecting a cautious approach to investments". Cutting capex lifts free cash flow in the short run and shrinks the organism's future surface area if sustained. OXXO store-opening capex is up in 2Q, which is the right place to hold.

Is it co-evolving? Yes, with caveats. Evidence of adaptation: the consumer-focused strategy "launched in the second half of 2025" is credited for OXXO's 2Q26 turn (SSS +9.5%, traffic +2.0% after -0.5% in 1Q26) [MEDIA]; a restructuring to align Spin with OXXO with benefits ramping through 2027; a new segment structure; a new CEO (Jose Antonio Fernandez Garza-Laguera, from Nov 1; "as I begin my tenure"); Americas & Mobility revenue +12.9% and operating income +34.0% in 1Q26 (an unlabeled table says +120.7%, basis unidentified). 10-year arc. 1. DEAD ANALOGUES (the casualties)

FEMSA is three things at once: a dense small-box consumer retailer (OXXO Mexico, ~38% of revenue and ~45% of operating income in 2Q26 [CALC]), a franchised beverage bottler (KOF), and a controlled holding company (a trust holding ~39% [MEDIA, undated]) that is bolting on credit (Spin), fuel (Delek) and Swiss convenience (Valora). The casualties that matched this combination are not Kodak or Blockbuster, which were killed by a technology substitute. They are the dominant retailers killed slowly by a better-priced format, while management paid out cash and diversified.

A&P (Great Atlantic & Pacific Tea). [UNVERIFIED, general knowledge] - Peak around 15,000-16,000 stores in 1930 and the largest US retailer into the 1960s. Supermarkets and then discounters took share. It failed to rebuild its small-box network. The 1970s "WEO" price-cut program lost money and was abandoned. - Foreign controlling holder (Tengelmann) from the late 1970s. Chapter 11 in 2010, liquidated in 2015. - Decline: about 50 years from share peak to first bankruptcy. Management said "we are competitive" until the losses stopped being deniable. - Match to FMX: the threat is a hard-discount format (Tiendas 3B at 91% penetration and 24 visits per quarter versus OXXO's rivals, Bodega Aurrera, Walmart Mexico's US$6B expansion, Chedraui's 140 openings [MEDIA per cited retail pieces]) attacking a basket-light, convenience-priced network. Bodega Aurrera wins the basket (11 items, MX$325 vs 3B's 6 items, MX$114), which is the A&P-versus-supermarket shape.

Sears. [UNVERIFIED, general knowledge] - Passed by Walmart as #1 US retailer around 1990-91. Used its retail traffic to build credit (Sears card, Discover) and financial services, then spun Dean Witter Discover (1993) and Allstate (1995). - Later years: capex starved, billions spent on buybacks, real estate sold to fund the parent (Seritage, 2015). Chapter 11 in October 2018. - Decline: about 28 years from being passed to bankruptcy. - Match to FMX: Spin by OXXO ("credit capabilities using FEMSA's customer data and ecosystem" [MEDIA]) is the retail-traffic-to-credit playbook. The dossier already shows non-creditable tax losses at Spin [SEARCH per FEMSA 2Q26 results] and an undisclosed credit book. The Heineken stake sale (5.24% for ~EUR 2.5B, undated [MEDIA]) funding a payout that exceeds FCF rhymes with Sears' asset sales funding the parent. The rhyme is partial: FEMSA's core is still growing, Sears' was not.

Woolworth (US variety stores). [UNVERIFIED, general knowledge] - Thousands of small neighborhood stores, outflanked from 1962 by Kmart, Walmart and Target. Exited US variety retail in 1997 (became Venator/Foot Locker). - Decline: about 35 years. It kept the format and tried new categories rather than repricing. - Match to FMX: the small-box convenience network's value rests on proximity, and proximity loses value when low-price boxes sit within walking distance. The dossier cannot show whether OXXO's traffic is holding against 3B (traffic +2.0% in 2Q26 after -0.5% in 1Q26 [SEARCH per FEMSA releases]). Two quarters do not settle it.

Common pattern in all three: the collapse was never fast. The shares of the dominant incumbent eroded for decades while earnings looked fine, because ticket and price carried comps. OXXO Mexico 2Q26 same-store sales +9.5% is traffic +2.0% and ticket +7.4%, so about three-quarters of the comp is ticket [CALC]. That is the same composition the casualties showed before the traffic gave out.

2. SURVIVOR ANALOGUES (the adapters)

Seven-Eleven Japan / Seven & i. [UNVERIFIED, general knowledge] - Same format as OXXO. It survived by controlling logistics (combined distribution, fresh food) and by monetising footfall with low-risk fee income (Seven Bank ATMs, from 2001), not credit. - Even the survivor has been stressed: weaker US 7-Eleven traffic and the 2024-25 Couche-Tard approach, which was withdrawn in 2025. - Lesson for FMX: the footfall-to-finance adaptation worked where the financial product was fee-based. Spin is credit, which is the Sears variant. Management says it will build the credit book "with discipline", which is not yet evidence.

Tesco, 2007-2020. [UNVERIFIED, general knowledge] - A dominant home-market grocer (UK share around 30%) hit by Aldi and Lidl. It lost share, expanded into the US (Fresh & Easy, exited 2013 at a loss of over GBP 1B), then suffered an accounting scandal in 2014. A new CEO shrank the foreign adventure, cut capex, reinvested in price in the core, and later bought Booker (2018). - Lesson for FMX: Tesco is the closest behavioral analogue. A new CEO (Fernandez Garza-Laguera, from 1 Nov, "as I begin my tenure" [MEDIA]), a restructuring, a capex cut (1H26 Ps. 15,067M, -16.2% [SEARCH]), and simultaneous US (Delek, 249 sites for $385M) and Europe (Valora) exposure. Tesco survived, but equity holders at the 2007 peak waited well over a decade. Survival and shareholder return are different outcomes.

Coca-Cola FEMSA through the 2014 Mexican sugar tax. [UNVERIFIED, general knowledge] - The 1 peso/liter IEPS tax took effect in January 2014. KOF volumes dipped in low single digits and recovered, with price pass-through and mix shift. - The 2026 step is larger: MX$1.64 to MX$3.08 per liter, about +87%, plus new treatment of "light/zero" beverages, enacted [MEDIA per cited tax pieces]. Cigarettes move from 160% to 200% ad valorem. - Lesson for FMX: the beverage franchise has already shown it survives a fiscal shock. The 2Q26 KOF read (volume +2.4%, operating income +9.1% [SEARCH per KOF 2Q26]) predates the full effect of the new tax. The current test is bigger than 2014's.
