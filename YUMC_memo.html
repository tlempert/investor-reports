Yum China Holdings (YUMC) as an investment

A scale monopoly that has not yet proven it can charge for it, priced almost exactly at what that means.

WAIT, Moderate conviction1 — my central estimate is $43.5 a share against a market price of $40.992, too thin a gap to buy. The central risk is not the restaurants but what outsiders cannot verify: how much cash sits in China3, and how much of the loyalty base buys because it wants to, not because the price is right4. The paradox: the footprint is enormous and un-copyable5, yet the same data proving that scale shows customers won by cutting price, not raising it4. Decision: hold nothing above $37.80, buy at $37.80 or lower, wait above it1.

What you would own

You would own KFC, Pizza Hut, Lavazza, Huang Ji Huang, Little Sheep and Taco Bell5, under exclusive, perpetual rights to KFC and Pizza Hut in mainland China plus outright ownership of Little Sheep and Huang Ji Huang5. Across 19,000+ restaurants in 2,700+ cities, with 590M+ loyalty members and 94% digital ordering6, the moat is footprint and supply chain, not a toll the customer pays. The switching cost a diner feels is habit, not lock-in — nothing stops them choosing Luckin or Mixue instead4.

The bull case and the counterargument

The strongest bull case: franchise-mix shift (KFC net-new-store franchising 30%→37%, Pizza Hut 10%→31%12) lowers capital per new store, KCOFFEE scaled ~44x in two years, and the Pizza Hut buy-in ends a 36-year license fee2 — a moat widening on its own terms, not through pricing.

The counterargument is economic: Pizza Hut's ticket fell 5% while transactions rose 5% in Q1 20264. Illustratively, 100 diners at ¥50 make ¥5,000; 105 at ¥47.50 make ¥4,987.50 — near-flat revenue from a 5-for-5 swing (simplified, not the real mix). About 48% of 2025 sales moved through delivery6; the own-app-versus-aggregator split isn't disclosed, so that toll is real but unquantified13. The moat could weaken if the transaction streak breaks, or delivery commissions outrun volume.

How I would make the decision

Purchase thesis: pay 11–12x operating owner earnings or less, so even the bear case needs no multiple expansion.

Conditions that would support buying

  • Price falls to $37.80 or below without a break in transaction growth.
  • Average ticket at KFC or Pizza Hut stabilizes or rises for two quarters.
  • The Pizza Hut fee or the 10-Q balance sheet is disclosed.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Price sits between $37.80 and the central value, as it does today.
  • Delivery's aggregator share and cost trend remain undisclosed.
  • Same-store sales stay near 1% with no pricing recovery.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Same-store transactions turn negative for two consecutive quarters.
  • Franchisee store payback lengthens past three years.
  • A second material debt raise follows the Pizza Hut bridge loan.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

In 2025 the company returned $1.50B — $1.14B buybacks plus $0.36B dividends (39% payout on $929M net income)22 — 161% of net income, 179% of FCF, $0.66B above FCF23. On the corrected $14.04B cap that's a ~10.7% shareholder yield23, above the cost of equity only because it exceeded FCF. Returns shouldn't sustainably exceed FCF; management caps them at ~100% from 2027, slowing share shrink from 6.3% to ~3.8%1. The 2024–25 per-share buyback prices in the raw data are implausible and excluded24.

Price discipline without false precision

The buy zone is $31.00 to $37.801: floor is no-growth value with cash, ceiling is central value less the margin of safety. The two-year closing range runs $36.99 to $56.9025 — the low end sits inside the zone, an ~8% decline would reach it, but the stock hasn't closed there in two years; a standing order there is a plan, not a certainty.
