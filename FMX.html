Munger's Verdict

FMX (FEMSA): Silicon Council Final Synthesis

Charlie Munger, 2026-10-04 (revised after Reality Check pass 1; see CORRECTION LOG). Price $115.68; market cap $39.41B [MEDIA per market extract]; pipeline share count 340.68M [CALC]. The dossier does not establish whether that count covers every share class. Every USD figure is pesos converted at one flat rate, 1 MXN = 0.055112 USD (about Ps 18.14 per USD) [CALC], applied to every year (E004, E034). Growth in pesos and growth in USD are different numbers here, and I label which one I mean. There is no VIC pitch for this ticker.

The short version: OXXO Mexico is a fine business. FEMSA the security is priced at roughly a developed-market required return on the cash its holder actually owns: about 6.5–7.5% on my attributable base, against a 12–13% local hurdle. Take away the share of Coca-Cola FEMSA that belongs to others and the Heineken stake the source says FEMSA has exited, and the gap is large under any input the dossier supports.

1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags raised by: none. 4 MODERATE flags by: Buffett, Biologist, Historian, Psychologist. 1 MINOR/NONE: Anthropologist (MINOR). Outside the Tribunal, Sherlock and Lynch also say MINOR and the other five experts say MODERATE. Nobody says SEVERE. The Historian calls a Survivor Trajectory at 58% confidence (historian.md), so the Casualty cap does not apply. The synthesis rule therefore allows the normal 15–18x quality band, though it does not require it (§3 explains why I don't use it).

The council agrees on the shape of the moat: - OXXO Mexico is a keystone. It has 24,708 stores, +253 in the quarter and +832 in twelve months. One source says its density "would take a new entrant 10–15 years to replicate". - Coca-Cola FEMSA holds a contractually protected territory. It is a private grant from The Coca-Cola Company, which holds the Series D shares.

They disagree about the consolidated company: - Group operating margin fell from 11.09% (FY2021) to 8.34% (FY2025), a 2.75-point slide. - The bottler passed through only about 85% of the tax-and-inflation hit, according to its CEO as reported in the media [MEDIA]. - The dossier ESTIMATES that about 100% of OXXO and Health revenue comes from individual end consumers, who can walk to the next corner for nothing. A B2B layer exists in Coca-Cola FEMSA's sales to retailers.

Result: Uncertain. The core is strong; the whole is moderate.

2. The Bull-Bear Balance

Lynch's case and my answers

Lynch (lynch.md) came looking for pessimism and found that the market is the optimist. At a 12% hurdle the price needs FCF to grow about 23.5% a year for five years. Barclays raised its target to $116 from $105 at Equal Weight, which is the current price. A director bought 2,100 shares on Aug 11, 2026 for $248,073 ($118.13 a share). The dossier has no insider-selling data. Lynch's own probability-weighted three-year value is about $117 including half credit for Heineken, roughly +0.4% a year. His 12% entry price is $83, or $92 with the stake. The counter-weight himself says not at this price. I take his counter-arguments one by one:

  1. "g = 0.0% is an artifact." Accepted. The 0.0% is ROE 14.7% times retention 0% under a 139% payout. That is mechanics, not a forecast. A chain growing stores 3.4% a year with average ticket +7.4% is not a no-growth business, so my central case uses 5% USD growth, not 0%.
  2. "The net-income collapse is below the operating line." Accepted, and it is why I value owner earnings and not the FY2025 net-income row ($1.07B, 2.3% margin, basis unconfirmed).
  3. Net margin went from 9.3% (2023) to 2.3% (2025).
  4. The FX line swung by Ps 14,175M (about $0.78B, non-cash): a Ps 9,258M gain in 9M 2024 against a Ps 4,917M loss in 9M 2025.
  5. But net interest rose from Ps 5,656M to Ps 9,822M, and the FY2025 effective tax rate was 37.59%. Those costs are real and recurring.
  6. "Margin erosion is mix and reclassification." Partly accepted for Europe and Health, where management moved distribution expenses into cost of goods. Even so, consolidated operating margin slipped 10 bps to 8.3% in a quarter when revenue grew 9.3%. Q2 operating income rose 7.2% to Ps 19,110M. Gross margin was 40.3% (company release) against 40.1% (Zacks).
  7. "The dividend gap includes non-controlling holders." This matters more than any other point Lynch makes, and no expert priced it (§3).
  8. His private-buyer arithmetic (EV including leases about $46.9B, EBITDA proxy $6.1B, so 7.7x) is answered below.

Weights

I weight the bull argument at 25% and the bear argument at 75%. These are weights on arguments, not scenario probabilities. The bulls are right about the business and wrong about the price. The bears are right about the price. Burry overreaches by asking for a short against an A-rated staple whose operating trend is improving.

The correction the council missed: consolidated is not attributable

Every expert DCF divides consolidated cash flow by FEMSA's shares. FEMSA consolidates Coca-Cola FEMSA, and others own part of that company. The dossier's own Step 0 shows how much: - 9M 2025 net consolidated income was Ps 20,359M, about $1.12B. - 9M 2025 net majority income was US$1.72 per ADS, about $0.59B.

On reported net income, 47% belonged to someone else. The $39.41B market cap buys only the majority claim. But a net-income ratio is the wrong base for splitting cash flow, so I treat it as an upper bound: - Parent-level costs inflate it. Interest (net interest Ps 9,822M against Ps 5,656M) and tax (37.59% effective) fall largely on the parent, which lowers majority income and so raises the computed minority share. Only the Ps 4,917M FX loss can be identified and stripped; adding it back gives about 38%. - The share count may inflate it too. Majority income was computed as US$1.72 per ADS × the pipeline's 340.68M. If that count understates the full ADS-equivalent count, majority income is understated and the minority share overstated.

Operating basis, my central estimate. - OXXO Mexico's domestic operating profit of MX$8.649B is about 45% of Q2 consolidated operating income of Ps 19,110M. That is Lynch's [CALC], and it mixes a MEDIA and an SEC figure from periods that may differ. - That leaves at most about 55% of operating income across Coca-Cola FEMSA, Americas & Mobility, Europe and Health. Only the Coca-Cola FEMSA part has outside owners. I put it at roughly 40–50% of group operating income. - The dossier does not state FEMSA's economic interest in Coca-Cola FEMSA. It says only that The Coca-Cola Company holds the Series D shares. Taking FEMSA's share as roughly half is a JUDGMENT from outside the dossier. - Half of 40–50% gives a minority claim of about 20–27% of operating earnings.

I adopt 25% (JUDGMENT, swept 20%, 25%, 38%). The 38% end is the net-income upper bound. - That leaves $1.70B × 0.75 = $1.275B attributable, or $3.74 a share: a 3.2% owner yield at $115.68. - Net debt. The central case subtracts all of the consolidated $1.28B of net debt. Parent-only net debt is not in the dossier. Subtracting only the attributable 75% adds about $0.94 a share, shown in the sweep. - Share count. It enters every per-share value. Because 340.68M is market cap ÷ price, per-share values and the price are on the same footing whatever the class coverage. It stays on the unresolved list.

Heineken: the source says FEMSA has exited

The direct evidence. The raw source behind the dossier's EUR 359M line is headlined "Mexico: FEMSA exits Heineken with EUR 359 million sale" [MEDIA per inside.beer]. It speaks of the completion of the divestment. - The refined dossier transcribed this as "completed a Heineken divestment" and dropped the word "exits". Supporting evidence, weaker: - FY2023 net income of $3.62B (9.3% margin) was an undated step-up. - The borrowings fell by about $2.5B in 2023. - Morningstar records the plan to return excess cash through special dividends.

Links I withdraw: - The $0.00B long-term financial assets line. Leases

IFRS 16 lease liabilities of $6.24B ($18.32 a share, 15.8% of price) sit outside net debt. Bezos, Buffett, Cook, Jobs and the Psychologist subtracted the full $18.32. - On an FCF basis that is right. FCF ($1.60B) is operating cash flow minus capex, before lease payments, which IFRS 16 puts in financing. - On owner earnings it double counts. Owner earnings reproduce from FY2025 operating cash flow of MXN 71.1B [SEARCH per KoalaGains], which is $3.92B at the flat rate. Subtract total D&A of $2,166M and SBC of $0.05B (0.1% of revenue), and you get $1.70B.

There is a label conflict, and I disclose it. - The dossier's footnote defines owner earnings as operating cash flow minus PP&E depreciation minus SBC. - But the $2,166M it subtracts is labelled "Total D&A (Latest)", in a forensic block that stops at FY2024, while the cash flow is FY2025.

I prefer the total-D&A reading. Lynch's EBITDA proxy (EBIT of about $3.95B plus this D&A, $6.1B) reconciles with Q1 adjusted EBITDA annualized (about $6.2B), which is what you would see if right-of-use depreciation is included. So leases are charged once already, and I do not subtract the liability again (JUDGMENT).

What the other reading would do. With PP&E depreciation only, the maintenance charge would be lower, which raises the base. But leases would then be uncharged and the $18.32 would have to come off, which lowers it. The double-count bound is shown in the sweep. On the evidence, the lease side is if anything the generous direction.

1. Species classification

FEMSA is two organisms in one body, and they belong to different categories.

OXXO (Mexico) is a keystone species in its habitat. The evidence: - Revenue of Ps 86,708M in Q2 2026, +11.8%, about 37.7% of consolidated quarterly revenue [SEC per FEMSA 2Q26 via narrative; CALC]. - Same-store sales +9.5%, composed of traffic +2.0% and average ticket +7.4% [CALC check: 1.020 x 1.074 = 1.095]. - Store count 24,708, +832 over twelve months [MEDIA per Mexican press]. OXXO is the dominant node in the neighborhood retail food web: small suppliers, bottlers and fintech/payment services (Spin) all route through it. - Domestic chain operating profit +12.3% to MX$8.649B [MEDIA per Mexican press], and Q1 2026 OXXO Mexico operating income +20.9% on revenue +8.3% [SEC per FEMSA 1Q26]. - The "nearly 29% of the overall retail market" claim [MEDIA, threat register] is a single unsourced-quality figure and I do not lean on it.

Would removing OXXO collapse the ecosystem? It would not collapse Mexican retail (Walmex, Chedraui, Soriana, 3B and informal shops would absorb it), but it would remove the densest proximity distribution channel, so "keystone" is accurate for its niche and not for Mexican retail as a whole. This is lock-in by density, not by captivity: shoppers can leave tomorrow, they just don't, and ticket +7.4% with traffic still positive says the shopper is not escaping.

Coca-Cola FEMSA is a symbiont with an asymmetric partner, not a keystone. The bottling territory is "contractually protected" but it is a private grant from The Coca-Cola Company [SEC per 2024 20-F; MEDIA]. Biologically this is a tenant farmer on a landlord's soil: both benefit, but one party owns the soil. Evidence of the asymmetry in the data: the CEO said the company passed through roughly 85% of the combined tax and inflation hit, protecting share at the cost of margin [MEDIA per Coca-Cola FEMSA Raises Mexico Soda Prices (2026)]. A true keystone sets terms; this one absorbed 15% of the shock. Q1 2026 volume -1.6%, transactions -3.0% [SEC per FEMSA 1Q26].

Health, Europe (Valora) and Americas & Mobility are young colonies in new habitats. Operating income fell in all three in Q2 2026 [SEC per FEMSA 2Q26 via narrative], though management attributes part of the Europe and Health gross-margin move to a distribution-cost reclassification, which makes segment margin comparisons not like-for-like. Segment absolute numbers are not in the dossier, so I cannot say whether these are "mid-investment" or "deteriorating". That is an honest data gap, not a finding.

Honest classification of the whole: Symbiont (Healthy), with a keystone core. I do not see a parasite signature. Parasites extract from captive hosts; OXXO's shoppers are free to leave and the pricing power is modest (ticket +7.4% against Mexican inflation, traffic positive).

2. Host health

Hosts: Mexican consumers (60.5% of revenue, $28.21B of $46.65B [MEDIA per TradingView]), small retailers served by the bottler, and the Coca-Cola Company as franchisor.

  • Consumer host: described as "soft but stabilizing" [MEDIA per Q4 call coverage]. Traffic turning positive "after several quarters of decline", with the World Cup contributing [SEC per FEMSA 2Q26], means part of the recovery is a one-time event. I discount it.
  • Wage host effect: the minimum wage rose from 248.93 to 315.04 (+26.6% over two years) and a workweek cut from 48 to 40 hours is phased in [MEDIA threat register; phase-in years blank in the snippet]. This is a two-edged signal: higher wages feed consumer spending, which supports OXXO's ticket growth, but 369,000+ employees (conflicting counts: 381,480 vs more than 369,000 [MEDIA vs SEC]) make FEMSA one of the largest labor-cost payers in the system. Wage inflation is a cost the species cannot hide.
  • Policy host effect: the sugar-sweetened beverage tax rose 87% (MX$1.64 to MX$3.08 per liter) [MEDIA per Mexico Raises Health Taxes / EL PAIS]; estimated take about Ps 41B (US$2.2B) in 2026. The state is a second predator taking a share of the bottler's host, the shopper's wallet.
  • Rent extraction rate: operating margin fell from 11.09% (FY2021) to 8.34% (FY2025) [SEARCH per KoalaGains], and 8.3% in Q2 2026, down 10 bps [SEC per FEMSA 2Q26]. Gross margin is 40.3% [SEC per FEMSA 2Q26] (Zacks reports 40.1% and -60 bps; the company release is primary). The organism is not extracting more rent per peso of host revenue; it is extracting about 2.75 points less than four years ago. Part of this is mix (low-margin fuel and Valora), part may be competition; the dossier cannot separate them.
  • Consolidation effect on leverage: modern trade (Walmex, USD 6B expansion; Chedraui, 140 openings) is growing, informal shops still hold a share [MEDIA per Mexico Retail Market Size]. Customer consolidation here is on the competitor side, not the customer side. Individual shoppers have no bargaining power; the competing retailers do.
  • Could the host evolve to not need FEMSA? For OXXO, the plausible path is the shopper shifting to larger-format discounters (Bodega Aurrera Express, about 1,480 units within 2,606 Bodega stores [MEDIA]) or to Supercito-style 350 m2 stores with broader grocery assortment [MEDIA per Chedraui coverage]. For the bottler, the route is lower sugar consumption by policy.

4. Ecosystem resilience vs brittleness

Points of failure, ranked by what the dossier supports:

  1. Single-country concentration: Mexico is 60.5% of revenue [MEDIA per TradingView]. A peso, USMCA, or tariff shock hits the majority of the body. The USMCA formal review began July 2026 and a Section 301 10% tariff on non-USMCA-compliant imports has applied since July 24, 2026 [MEDIA threat register; sources disagree on dates and scope]. 84% of Mexico-US trade stayed tariff-free as of August 2025. The retail operation consumes domestic goods, so direct tariff exposure is limited; the indirect hit comes through peso and consumer income.
  2. Franchise dependence: the bottler's territory exists at the pleasure of The Coca-Cola Company [SEC per 2024 20-F]. Not a near-term threat, but it caps how much of Coca-Cola FEMSA's economics FEMSA controls.
  3. Policy brittleness in beverages: the sugar tax is enacted; the bottler is absorbing 15% of the cost shock. The water-permit item (CONAGUA, Al Jazeera) is speculative [MEDIA]; I assign it little weight.
  4. Balance-sheet fat reserves: cash and short-term investments $6.42B against borrowings $7.70B (net debt $1.28B [CALC]), but IFRS 16 lease liabilities of $6.24B (about $18.32 a share, 15.8% of price [CALC]) are excluded. With leases, the organism is more levered than the anchors suggest. Fitch affirmed A (Feb 13, 2026) and AAA(mex) (Nov 14, 2025) [MEDIA per Fitch], so the immediate survival risk is low.
  5. Dividend overdraw: dividend of $5.90 a share, a 139% payout [CALC] and 0.80x FCF coverage ($2.01B against $1.60B; owner earnings 0.85x) [CALC]. A different basis shows dividends paid MXN 49.9B against FCF MXN 32.6B (about 0.65x), though this may include non-controlling holders [SEARCH per KoalaGains]. How the gap is funded is not stated. In ecological terms the species is living partly off stored fat (disposals, cash); that is sustainable only while fat remains.

Is the ecosystem diversifying or concentrating? Five segments in 18 countries is real diversification on paper, but the diversified segments are the ones with falling Q2 operating income. The concentration is in the part that earns the profit. That is a brittle diversification: the income is Mexican while the geography is spread.

The stress test shows adjusted FCF of $0.91B at -30% revenue and turning negative at roughly -70% [CALC]; the "Simple" column ($14.00B at base) is 8.8x actual FCF and I discard it. A 3.0-3.7% adjusted margin means survival is not at stake in a 30% shock, but dividends are. Revenue per se is not the failure mode; the dividend is.

Historian of Disruption: FMX (FEMSA), analysis date 2026-10-04

Scope note. The dossier supplies the FMX facts and carries their tags: [SEC], [CALC], [SEARCH], [MEDIA]. The analogue companies below come from my own historical knowledge, not the dossier. Their figures are approximate and I mark them as such. Where I say "the dossier does not say", it does not.

FMX is not a classic disruption case. There is no Netflix-style entrant. The threats are slow: modern-trade competition, policy (sugar tax) and cost inflation. So the question is "what happens to dominant physical-footprint retail and bottling franchises when the footprint stops being scarce and the cost base keeps rising", not "when does the product die".

1. DEAD ANALOGUES (The Casualties)

A&P (Great Atlantic & Pacific Tea Co.), US grocery, roughly 1930s-2015. - Position: the largest US grocer for decades, roughly 15,000 stores at its 1930 peak and the dominant share of US food retail (approximate). - What killed it: bigger-format, lower-cost entrants (supermarkets, then Walmart supercenters) and a cost structure and store base built for the old format. The decline ran about 40 years, not 4. The 1972 "WEO" price-cutting push failed because margins could not carry it. Bankruptcy came in 2010 and liquidation in 2015. - Management behavior: cost-cutting and store closures that kept the cash flowing while the franchise thinned. There was no collapse moment. - Match to FMX: the "physical density as moat" structure. OXXO's moat claim is density and brand trust that "would take a new entrant 10-15 years to replicate" [MEDIA per Business & Moat Analysis (2026)]. That claim was also true of A&P in 1950. The difference is that the threat to OXXO is a smaller format (Chedraui Supercito at about 350 sq m, Walmex Bodega Aurrera Express at about 1,480 units [MEDIA]), not a bigger one.

Kmart, US discount retail, 1962-2002 (Chapter 11), approximate. - Position: more than 2,000 stores at its 1990s peak, then passed by Walmart in sales around 1990. - What killed it: it treated Walmart's logistics and everyday-low-price model as a regional nuisance, then tried to catch up with capex it could no longer afford. The decline took roughly 12 years from the point Walmart overtook it to bankruptcy. - Match to FMX: Walmex "earmarked USD 6B for expansion" and rolled out 1.7M smart shelf labels in July 2026 [MEDIA]. The Kmart lesson is that the incumbent's tell is capex falling while the entrant's capex is rising. FMX H1 2026 capex is Ps 15,067M, 3.4% of sales, down 16.2% [SEC per FEMSA 2Q26]. FY2025 capex of about $2.12B [SEARCH per KoalaGains, CALC conversion] against D&A of $2.17B [CALC] is roughly 0.98x. That is maintenance-level spending, not investing ahead of the curve.

Mexican soda volumes under tax, 2014 onward (a policy analogue, approximate). - No company died here, but the pattern is instructive. After Mexico's 2014 tax of about one peso a liter, published studies put taxed-beverage purchases down about 6% in year one and about 10% by year two. Industry volumes partly recovered with price and pack changes. - Match to FMX: the 2026 tax moves from MX$1.64 to MX$3.08 a liter, +87% [MEDIA per EL PAÍS]. Coca-Cola FEMSA Q1 2026 transactions were -3.0% and volume -1.6% [SEC per FEMSA 1Q26]. The company passed through "roughly 85%" of the tax and inflation hit to protect share at the cost of margin [MEDIA per Coca-Cola FEMSA Raises Mexico Soda Prices (2026)]. Volume fell in Mexico in Q2 2026 [MEDIA]. - This is a margin and volume drag, not an extinction event. The Coca-Cola franchise is a private grant that cannot be taken by a competitor [MEDIA per Moat Analysis], but it cannot be defended against the legislature either.

2. SURVIVOR ANALOGUES (The Adapters)

Seven-Eleven Japan (Ito-Yokado), 1970s onward. - It faced the same "density plus small box" setup and won by moving from product-selling to service-and-data selling: daily fresh food, private label, bill payment, and later ATMs (Seven Bank, 2001, approximate). - Adaptation started when the chain was growing, not shrinking. Tesco, UK grocery, 2014-2018 (approximate). - Faced Aldi and Lidl and an accounting scandal. It survived by cutting its dividend, shrinking space, cutting prices and rebuilding trust. - Match to FMX: the survivor move was the dividend cut. FMX does the opposite, with payout at 139% [CALC] and dividends of about $2.01B against FCF of $1.60B [CALC]. This is the cleanest behavioral contrast in the analysis.
