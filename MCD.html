Munger's Verdict

MCD (McDonald's Corporation): Munger Synthesis, 2026-10-05

Price $231.89. The hurdle is the dossier's 9.74% local cost of equity at beta 1: the 5.28% U.S. 10-year plus Damodaran's 4.46% total ERP (4.23% mature-market + 0.23% country). Beta is not in the dossier. Beta 1 is the pipeline's default when beta is missing, not an estimate of MCD's risk, and the dossier's evidence leans defensive. Revenue rose every year. In the stress table, FCF falls only ~22% on a 20% revenue decline. The Anthropologist and the Futurist both say a defensive beta would lower the hurdle. So beta 1 is probably conservative. I keep 9.74% anyway as an opportunity-cost hurdle: it is ~4.5 points over a 5.28% Treasury, about the least a Munger would ask of an equity. That hurdle turns out to be the whole case, and §5 prints the verdict at both ends of the ledger's 8–10% range.

1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags. 3 MODERATE flags by Buffett, the Biologist and the Psychologist. 2 MINOR by the Historian and the Anthropologist. 0 NONE. Under the synthesis rule that means the normal Quality Floor (15x–18x). The Historian calls a Survivor Trajectory at 60%, not a casualty, so the 14x casualty cap does not apply. None of the other seven experts flags SEVERE either (Bezos, Burry, Cook and Jobs MODERATE; Sherlock, the Futurist and Lynch MINOR). All five tribunal members put the weak joint in the same place, and it is not the brand. The brand is a top-10 global brand worth over $190B, Gen Z's favourite QSR with an idea score of 81, and sits on 45,356 restaurants in 100+ countries. McDonald's owns about 56% of the land and 80% of the buildings in its consolidated markets. The weak joint is the franchisee bargain. Operators rate corporate 1.37/5, down from 1.71. Only 60–65% of the U.S. system adopted the under-$3 price architecture. The royalty on new U.S. units is going up 25%. The National Owners Association has answered with a 15-point Bill of Rights. One discount on that agreement: the rating comes from one survey (Kalinowski/CNBC) and the compliance figure from one trade outlet (Franchise Investor Data). Ten experts repeating them makes one witness, not ten.

2. One witness in twelve hats

Jev's audit found that all 12 experts set their triggers with the same method, a scenario grid or DCF. The cluster at $185–190 is one division done seven times: $10.97 FCF/share × 1.037 ÷ (9.74% − 3.7%) = $188. Sherlock, the Anthropologist, the Biologist, Bezos, Burry, the Historian and the Futurist all ran it. The council's 0-BUY tally rests on the same single claim: at a beta-1 hurdle of 9.74%, the price needs ~4.8% perpetual FCF growth (~4.1% on owner earnings of $8.90B). At an 8.5% hurdle the implied growth falls to ~3.6%, which is the pipeline's 3.7%, and the stock is roughly fair. The Futurist, the Biologist and Lynch each showed this. So the vote is really a vote on a beta nobody has, and I weigh the tally as one argument. My own central value, ceiling and floor come from the same division, adjusted for reinvestment in §5. The decision therefore rests on two things: one division at a beta-1 hurdle, and one growth judgment, 3.7% against the 4.8% priced in.

The facts that bear on that growth judgment: - Against faster growth: - Net income has been flat since the 2023 margin peak. - U.S. comps were +0.8% while Burger King printed +8.5%. That is one U.S. traffic quarter, counted once. - The franchisee data comes from one survey and one trade outlet. - For faster growth: - The international engine: IOM +6%, IDL +8%. - Margin-neutral 2024-to-TTM growth of ~4.6% a year, which was currency-aided (§5).

The balance sheet bears on the multiple, not on growth.

3. Bull-Bear Balance

Lynch first, because he earned it. He calls MCD a stalwart (HOLD, 55%) and finds the narrative worse than the business. On several points he is right. Management warned on 7 May of a Q2 slowdown against the Minecraft lap, so global comps of +1.3% against +3.8% were telegraphed. The franchisees' own six-month outlook is 2.58/5, the best in 11 quarters: operators who are angry at corporate but hopeful about their own sales are bargaining, not leaving. Japan has 10 straight quarters of positive guest counts. Q2 systemwide sales were ~$37B, up 5% (+4% constant currency), with IOM +6% and IDL +8%. Franchised margins rose 4.3% to $3.71B, about 90% of restaurant-margin dollars. The engine still runs.

I part company with his valuation grid, and I test it one input at a time. His base case compounds 7% a year from the $12.89 FY2026 consensus. That consensus is +7.9% on $11.95, helped by a Q2 tax rate of 19.5% against 21.3%, and Zacks says it was cut in the past 30 days. His bear row (4% EPS growth, 16x) gives ~$232. - Change only the growth, to ~2% a year: ~$219. That is the 2023-peak net-income record (net income $8.47B → $8.22B → $8.56B, ~1.5% a year to TTM) plus ~1% of indicative share shrink. - Change only the multiple, to 15x: ~$217.

Either change alone leaves a ~6% capital loss, which three years of ~$24 in dividends roughly offset. That makes a flat bear case, not his +3% a year. Against the margin-neutral per-share record (§5, ~5.6%), his 4% bear growth is fair, so the risk sits in the multiple.

His bull case re-rates the stock to 22x, which is a 4.5% earnings yield. That is below the 5.28% 10-year he himself concedes makes the old 2.26% average dividend yield an unfair anchor, so I give the re-rating no weight. Lynch also scores the stock 0.60 on his own dividend-adjusted PEG, inside his "poor" zone.

How far apart we are: - Buy triggers: his $205–210 BUY trigger (at a 15% IRR) against my $165 fair value limit, about $40–45 apart. - Fair values: his ~$240 against my $179, about $60 apart.

Nearly all of that gap is the hurdle and the re-rating, not the business.

Every expert's BULL CASE line: - Bezos: restaurant growth and loyalty lift FCF per share without squeezing franchisees. - Buffett: traffic recovers, and the ~210M loyalty base turns into durable cash growth. - Burry: the value menu restores traffic, and FCF beats the 4.8% the price implies. - Cook: the value menu, new units and loyalty lift cash without hurting operators. - Jobs: brand, loyalty reach and expansion become sustained traffic, and trust with operators is restored. - Psychologist: loyalty and new restaurants lift cash while value restores traffic and operator confidence. - Sherlock: a ~95%-franchised rent-and-royalty annuity with 49 straight dividend raises and 19% ROIC re-accelerates toward 250M loyalty users and 50,000 units. - Futurist: systemwide +5% plus a royalty-heavy model compounds 5% for a decade. - Biologist: the landlord-royalty machine's U.S. value reset restores traffic. - Historian: McDonald's in 2003 and Domino's in 2009 repeat. - Anthropologist: a Gen Z-favourite, 49-year dividend grower delivers a low-variance 9–10%. - Lynch: EPS compounds ~9% to $16.69 by FY2029, re-rates to 22x (~$367).

Every bull case needs one of two things: U.S. traffic recovering, or growth above the record. None needs the moat to widen. I weight the bull argument at 40% and the bear argument at 60%. The bears do not hold the better view of the business. They are right that the price already pays for a pace the record shows only from the 2022 margin trough, or in a short currency-aided window (§5). U.S. comps fell from +3.9% (Q1, mostly check) to +0.8% in Q2. An undated report puts traffic at −4.5%. Burger King U.S. printed +8.5%. Restaurant Business says the value push "has not had the expected impact on profits."

Private Buyer Test. Enterprise value is ~$203B ($164.1B market cap + $39.04B net debt). That is 16.4x 2025 operating income of $12,393M, or ~13.9x EBITDA on an unreconciled D&A. A sponsor cannot lever a company already at 3.1x net debt/operating income with $14.73B of leases on top, and no strategic buyer exists for a $200B QSR. The private value does not exceed my buy zone. If anything, it sits below the public price.

4. Decision Logic

Ceiling (hidden value). Bezos finds no cash incinerator. There is no segment operating income, and the core P/E cannot be computed. The headline is 19.4x FY2025 EPS. Neither Buffett nor the Futurist sees the moat widening. The Futurist puts the growth premium at $103–119 a share, 44–51% of the price, over a zero-growth value of $112.6–129.1, and grades the stock "Priced for Perfection." Burry's forensics clear the accounting: - SBC is $0.17B, 0.6% of revenue. - The adjusted-vs-GAAP EPS gap is $0.06. - Inventory is $0.06B. - Buybacks have fallen from $3.90B (2022) to $2.06B (2025), not risen into a high price.

Burry's survival test also passes. The stress table, anchored to FY2025 FCF of $7.19B, gives $6.39B at −10% revenue, $5.59B at −20% and $4.79B at −30%. The pipeline says FCF turns negative only at about a −95% revenue decline. The Futurist rightly calls that too gentle, given ~$1.6B of annual interest and fixed rent, and the cost model rests on one year (FY2022). Even so, at −20% net debt/FCF goes from 5.0x to 7.0x, and the ~$5.5B dividend eats nearly all of it. The business survives; the dividend growth does not. There is no hidden value, so I do not raise the ceiling.

Floor (moat and quality). Buffett finds pricing power, but qualified: Q1's +3.9% came from check, not visits. Jobs grades it "Sugared Water": a powerful brand with no evidence of enduring love. The Biologist calls it a "Symbiont (Healthy), with parasitic drift at the franchisee interface." The Anthropologist calls it a "Cultural Institution (Enduring)." Cook gives operations a B−: SG&A rose from $2.82B to $3.04B, faster than revenue's +5.5%, and Q2 operating income grew 3% on revenue of +3.7%. The Psychologist trusts management as "Owners, provisionally," with no Q&A text to test them on. That is a quality compounder, not a franchise monopoly.

The Franchise Premium test fails on one of four criteria, and one failure is enough: 1. 0 SEVERE flags: passes. 2. Margin route: passes. 2025 operating margin was 46.1% ($12,393M ÷ $26.89B), Q2 was 47.0%, and net margin held at 31.7–33.2% for three years. ROIC of 18.9–21.4% misses the 20% bar. 3. Recurring revenue above 75%: fails. Company-operated sales were $2.53B of $7.10B Q2 revenue (~36%), so rent and royalties are ~64%. 4. Organizational switching costs: passes. The test applies to the franchisees, because they pay the rent and royalties. They cannot cheaply re-flag a $1.5–2.7M site, and the company's land and buildings make defection costly (the Biologist). Whether they are in revolt is a separate question from whether they can leave.

The premium denial rests on criterion 3 alone. So the quality range is 15x–18x. I lift the absurdly-cheap floor from 10x to 12x, on FCF per share (§5), because the Tribunal is clean and the dividend has risen 49 years in a row.

5. Valuation

Owner EPS is $12.58 ($8.90B ÷ 707.6M). It deducts ~$2.27B of implied depreciation, not the $3.58B of capex, which runs 1.6x depreciation and ~32% of OCF during ~2,300 openings. The "$457M D&A" line does not reconcile. FCF per share is $10.97, and 2025 FCF of $7.19B gives $10.16. Q2 OCF of $2.81B, up 40.5% on revenue of +3.8%, is an unexplained year-on-year swing. The cash-conversion table puts it inside the range of the other three TTM quarters ($2.41B–$3.43B), so the jump may reflect a weak prior-year quarter. I keep TTM as the base and show the 2025 base below. GAAP TTM EPS is $12.42 ($8.79B ÷ 707.6M).

Growth costs capital. I take 3.7% as the long-run rate: unit growth of ~5% a year toward 50,000 restaurants, minus licensed-market mix and operator friction. The Futurist argues for 3.5–4.0%. Unit growth is what the capex above depreciation buys, so owner earnings cannot grow at 3.7% for free. I value with the value-driver identity:

value = owner EPS × (1 − g ÷ RONIC) × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g)

The incremental return (RONIC) is not disclosed. Two dossier-anchored values bracket it: - 19.2%, TTM ROIC. That is a total return, not an incremental one. It understates the company's incremental return to the extent that franchisees fund the equipment. - 25.1%, the rate implied by current net reinvestment. Capex less depreciation ($3.58B − $2.27B = $1.31B) is 14.7% of owner earnings, and 3.7% ÷ 14.7% = 25.1%.

Central value: $179, the midpoint of $174 (RONIC 19.2%) and $184 (RONIC 25.1%), at 9.74% and 3.7%. The 2022 ROIC of 17.8% gives $171. The old uncharged owner-earnings leg ($216) was growth without its cost. It survives only as an upper bound, outside the central.

6. Final Decision

Decision: WAIT. McDonald's is a business I would own. $231.89 is a fair price only if the currency-aided ~4.6–4.8% pace is permanent. This is not a PASS. The PASS votes from Bezos, Buffett, Cook, Jobs and the Psychologist all carry trigger prices, so they are refusals on price, not on quality. §4 reports six quality readings. Two are reservations: Jobs's "Sugared Water" and Cook's B− (falling returns, slower U.S. sales). Buffett's "qualified" pricing power is a partial third. The Biologist, the Anthropologist and Sherlock (a "Rational-leaning Compounder with a Utility-grade revenue stream") hold the business view I share.

Burry's SELL (0.5% short) is the sharpest case. Franchisees must deliver the value strategy, and they are resisting it. His own key risk answers him: the brand and the cash can defeat a short. I would not short a 49-year dividend raiser that stays FCF-positive at −30% revenue. His fracture is still the one variable to watch.

Moat Tribunal Result: Strong. All five tribunal members agree the brand and real estate are intact, and none flags SEVERE. Buffett, the Biologist and the Psychologist dissent to MODERATE at the franchisee interface. The Historian and the Anthropologist hold MINOR.

The Munger Buy Zone: $132 (absurdly cheap) to $165 (fair value limit), both on TTM FCF per share. I raised the floor from 10x to 12x for moat quality. I did not raise the ceiling, because no hidden value survived Bezos's shutdown test.

Why would it be mispriced at $165? Fear. The stock is already down 20.68% in a year while the S&P 500 rose 15.00%. The souring narrative has several strands: U.S. comps, franchisee headlines, Burger King's +8.5%, and the late-June removal from Russell growth and defensive indexes, which a truncated media item suggests may have brought mechanical selling. Short interest is only 1.92% of float, so nobody is betting on collapse. Another −20% would be fear pricing a franchise problem as permanent before the franchisee P&L has been seen. Today it is not mispriced. It is priced at about my value on a hurdle 1.2 points lower than mine.

Balance sheet and allocation. These are not broken, but they are not a cushion: - Net debt is $39.04B: 5.0x TTM FCF and 3.1x operating income, with 8.2x interest coverage. - Debt rose from $36.14B (2022) to $40.15B (2025). - Total payout was ~104% of 2025 FCF, and dividends alone are ~70% of TTM FCF. - The 24-month maturities are unknown, and refinancing happens against a 5.28% 10-year. - The dossier quarantines the 736M (2022) share count. The comparison it permits, the 707.6M cover count against the ~716.6M FY2025 diluted-average estimate, is ~1.3% lower on non-identical bases. That suggests a fading cannibal, but only indicatively.

Moat Half-Life: ~20 years for the global brand and real estate. The U.S. franchisee bargain could erode inside 5–10 years if the Historian's Subway arc (about ten years from peak to distressed sale) is the right analogue. The Historian puts the U.S. relationship at "mid-transition."

Remove McDonald's and consumers would redirect to Burger King, Chipotle and others; Burger King U.S. comparable sales were +8.5% in Q2 against McDonald's U.S. +0.8% [MEDIA per Zacks; SEC release excerpt], which is what a replaceable species looks like. McDonald's is keystone only to its own franchisees, whose $1.47-2.73M investment [MEDIA per Franchise Chatter] is sunk into its brand, supply chain and real estate.

Evidence that the relationship is symbiotic: - ~95% of restaurants are franchised [MEDIA per Wikipedia / Bull Case Theory]; franchised restaurant margin is $3.71B in Q2, ~90% of total restaurant margin dollars [MEDIA per Zacks]. The company is essentially a rent-and-royalty collector with a consumer brand attached. - The brand brings the host something real: a top-10 global brand [SEC, CEO letter; MEDIA per Interbrand/Kantar], Gen Z's favourite QSR brand [MEDIA per Dig Insights], ~210M 90-day active loyalty users [SEC, CEO letter], and Japan's 10th consecutive quarter of positive comparable guest counts [MEDIA per Q2 call transcript summary]. - Franchisee six-month business outlook is 2.58/5, the best in 11 quarters [MEDIA per CNBC, Kalinowski survey]. The hosts are not collapsing.

Evidence for parasitic drift (lock-in and love look different under a microscope): - Operators rate corporate 1.37/5, down from 1.71 in October 2025 [MEDIA per CNBC]. An outlook that is improving while the rating of the counterparty is falling is what captive hosts look like: they stay because exit is costly, not because they are pleased. - Only 60-65% of the U.S. system executed the recommended under-$3 pricing architecture. Non-compliance entered business reviews on 2026-08-04 and "affects things like growth and eligibility" [MEDIA per Franchise Investor Data]. The franchisor is using growth eligibility to enforce its pricing choice. The National Owners Association answered with a 15-point Franchisee Bill of Rights asserting menu-price control. This is a dispute over who owns the margin. - Higher royalty on new U.S. restaurants by 25% [MEDIA per Restaurant Dive]; the 2022 FDD rent range is 10% to 15.75% on new traditional units [MEDIA per supplied threat register]. The articles themselves predict operator backlash. - Three pending Black-franchisee discrimination suits (more than 40 former franchisees in the federal one; narrowed by a Chicago judge on 2026-09-22) [MEDIA per Reuters / Loevy]. Amount not quantified. A host-class grievance, not just a legal cost. - Italy's AGCM is investigating franchise terms [MEDIA per Reuters]. A regulator is questioning whether the contract is lopsided.

Net: symbiont, honestly labelled "Healthy" only at the consumer and brand level. The franchisee interface is the weak joint.

2. Host health

There are two hosts. They have to be read separately.

Host A: the consumer. The dossier is mixed. - Global comparable sales +1.3% in Q2 vs +3.8% in Q2 2025; first half +2.5%. U.S. comparable sales Q1 +3.9%, Q2 +0.8%, H1 +2.3% [SEC release excerpt; MEDIA per Zacks]. U.S. systemwide +2%; IOM +6%; IDL +8% [MEDIA per Zacks]. Growth is coming from abroad. The U.S. is the soft limb. - U.S. same-store sales +0.8% with overall traffic -4.5% [MEDIA per Hey Lefty, undated]. Low-income traffic still down even as affordability scores improved [MEDIA per Restaurant Dive / Restaurant Business]. Q1's +3.9% was "primarily driven by positive check growth" [MEDIA per Zacks], meaning price carried it, not visits. Restaurant Business: "evidence the value push has not had the expected impact on profits" [MEDIA]. - Q2 had a known headwind: management warned on 7 May 2026 of a meaningful Q2 deceleration against the 2025 Minecraft promotion [MEDIA per Q1 call highlights]. So part of the +0.8% is a hard comparison, and I do not read it as proof of a broken consumer. But a price-led comp with falling traffic is the textbook sign that the host's tolerance is being spent, not grown. - Total addressable habitat is growing slowly: global QSR $321.6B in 2026 to $461.7B by 2033, 5.3% CAGR [MEDIA per Persistence Market Research]. Full-service restaurants projected to outpace QSR in 2026 [MEDIA per VettedBiz]. Gen Z private-label spend is 18.4% of CPG budget [MEDIA per Yahoo Finance]. The host is price-sensitive and has alternatives.

Host B: the franchisee. This is where the rent is actually paid. - Host population is expanding in count: 45,356 restaurants at end-2025 [MEDIA per Wikipedia], target 50,000 by end-2027, i.e. +4,644 (+10.2% [CALC]) in two years, against nearly 2,300 gross openings in 2025 [SEC]. Gross openings, not net: the net pace needed (~2,320 a year) is not disclosed against closures, so I cannot say whether the 2027 target is a stretch. Two years of ~2,300 gross openings with any closures falls short. - Can the host evolve to not need this species? Poorly. Franchisees cannot cheaply re-flag a $1.5-2.7M site, and the real-estate layer (company owns ~56% of land and ~80% of buildings in consolidated markets [MEDIA per Wikipedia]) makes defection costly. A real-estate spin-off was considered and rejected as too risky for franchisee relations [MEDIA per Nation's Restaurant News], which tells me management itself reads the franchisee relationship as the system's load-bearing wall. - Rent extraction rate. The dossier does not supply what the average franchisee earns, so the true share of host economics captured cannot be computed. What can be computed is the company's take. Q2 franchised margin $3.71B [MEDIA per Zacks] over franchised sales of roughly $34.5B (systemwide ~$37B less $2.53B company-operated [SEC release excerpt; MEDIA per Zacks]) = ~10.8% of franchised sales [CALC, approximate, mixes SEC and MEDIA figures]. That is rent plus royalty net of the company's own occupancy cost. The FDD rent band (10%-15.75%) is consistent with that scale. The generic "12-20% EBITDA" and "30% cash-on-cash" franchise benchmarks in the dossier are not McDonald's data [MEDIA per Franchise Times; EatBreadless], and I do not use them to estimate franchisee profit. Blind spot: the single most important host-health number is missing. - Consolidation question: the dossier does not say whether franchisee ownership is consolidating, so I cannot judge bargaining-power shifts. The National Owners Association's Bill of Rights is the sign that hosts are organising.

1. DEAD ANALOGUES (The Casualties)

Decline took about 10 years and was invisible in corporate royalty revenue for the first half, because royalties are paid on sales, not on franchisee profit. Subway had no such real-estate base.

Howard Johnson's, 1960s to 1990s. [BACKGROUND recall] The largest U.S. restaurant chain of its era, about 1,000 restaurants at the peak, heavily franchised, tied to a single format and roadside traffic. It lost to fast food and interstate-era changes over roughly 20 years, was sold to Imperial Group (UK) in 1980, and the last units closed decades later. Lesson: a dominant format that is "good enough" can bleed for 20 years without a single dramatic quarter. Relevance to MCD is the slow-bleed shape only; MCD's format has been refreshed repeatedly.

Sears Holdings, 2005 to 2018 (behavior analogue only). [BACKGROUND recall] Roughly $6B of buybacks while store investment was starved. I include it only to define the extraction signal. 2. SURVIVOR ANALOGUES (The Adapters)

McDonald's, 2002 to 2006 (its own precedent). [BACKGROUND recall] Over-expanded into the late 1990s, reported its first quarterly loss in early 2003, and the stock fell from about $48 (1999) to about $12 (March 2003). Domino's, 2008 to 2010. [BACKGROUND recall] Comps went negative in 2008, management publicly admitted product quality was poor in 2009 ("Pizza Turnaround"), reformulated, and invested in digital ordering ahead of rivals. The stock went from low single digits (late 2008) to well over $100 by the mid-2010s. They adapted early, spent at a margin cost, and kept operator economics healthy. MCD parallels: Edge platform with Google, ~210M loyalty users toward 250M by end-2027, Ready on Arrival in top six markets [SEC, CEO letter]. Caveat: AI voice ordering was pulled in 2024 for errors and is back at only five U.S. locations [MEDIA per ABC News]; that is a pilot, not an adaptation at scale.

Starbucks, 2008. [BACKGROUND recall] Founder returned in January 2008, about 600 U.S. stores were closed, and focus went back to the core experience. Relevance: MCD's "Accelerating the Organization" restructuring ($47M Q1 2026 vs $66M, in Other operating (income) expense, net [SEC 10-Q]) is a small cost action, not a store-base reset.
