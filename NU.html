4. THE DISINTERMEDIATION SCENARIO

The specific technology and behaviour change: Pix-based instalment credit ("Pix Parcelado", pilot stage [MEDIA, threat register #2]) combined with Open Finance data portability (enacted, #4). Together they let any app, retailer (MagaluPay, Riachuelo), rival neobank (Mercado Pago, 88M monthly active users in Q2'26 [MEDIA]) or an AI shopping agent (my JUDGMENT; not in the dossier) originate instalment credit straight from the customer's bank account, with data to underwrite it, bypassing the credit card. Cards are 66% of Nu's $39.4B book ($26B [CALC]) and revolving rates exceed 400% annually [MEDIA], which is exactly the price level a cheaper instalment rail undercuts. A second route is regulatory: a proposed cap on revolving rates averaging 450% at 100% (Senate bill, status proposed [MEDIA]).

Scenario: credit demand or credit pricing drops 20% over 5 years (a 4.4% annual decline, versus the +37% portfolio growth now).

  • Credit revenue run-rate is $3,604.8M x 4 = $14.4B [CALC]. A 20% loss is $2.9B/year.

  • Price-led loss (rate cap or instalment-rail price competition; costs do not fall):

  • Moat effect: volume-led erodes the data flywheel slowly. Price-led attacks the 61%-of-revenue line directly and leaves the float and fee lines (39% of revenue) standing.
  • Offensive or defensive?

1. Dead analogues (the casualties)

Banco Nacional (Brazil, 1994-1995) [BACKGROUND]. A large Brazilian private bank that depended on inflation "float" income. When the Real Plan ended high inflation in 1994, that float income disappeared. The bank had been concealing bad loans and was taken over by the Central Bank in 1995. Several other Brazilian banks failed or were rescued in the same regime change (Economico, Bamerindus). Match to Nu: not accounting concealment. The relevant point is regime dependence.
