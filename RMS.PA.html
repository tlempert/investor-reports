Munger's Verdict

Munger Synthesis: Hermès International SCA (RMS.PA), Euronext Paris, €1,286.00, 2026-10-06

All figures are in EUR. Hurdle: LOCAL (EUR) COST OF EQUITY 8.19% = EUR 10-year 3.18% [LIVE: FRED, 2026-08] + mature ERP 4.23% + France CRP 0.78% (total ERP 5.01%) [DATA: Damodaran, January 2026], beta 1. The low end of the bracket is 7.41% with the CRP at 0%. No SEC filing exists for this listing, so every pipeline table is [CALC] and company figures arrive through search excerpts.

1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags raised by: none. 1 MODERATE flag by: Psychologist. 4 MINOR/NONE (Buffett, Biologist, Historian, Anthropologist, all MINOR). Outside the Tribunal, Jobs flags MODERATE, Lynch NONE, and the other five MINOR. The Historian returns a Survivor Trajectory at about 80% confidence, so the Casualty cap does not apply. Nobody on the council thinks the moat is breaking. The Psychologist's MODERATE is about evidence, not erosion: there is no cohort, retention or Q&A data to test the "my kids" question. Jobs's MODERATE is about the same gap, since there is no love metric. The twelve-item threat register is regulation, litigation and ESG cost (CITES, ESPR/CSRD, greenwashing, Russia export limits, Cavalleri, a tariff-refund investigation, a French wealth-tax proposal, counterfeits). Not one item attacks the pricing mechanism. The sharpest item is Cavalleri. Plaintiffs allege "incredible market power", and Hermès denies wrongdoing and says exclusivity is brand identity. Judge Donato dismissed the federal claims with prejudice in September 2025 for failure to plead a relevant market or market power. An appeal is reported, with no date or damages figure. Result: Strong. That puts us on the Quality row: 10x floor, 18x top. The Franchise row is not available, and the reason is the kind of business, not its quality (§5).

2. What the council agreed on, and how many witnesses that is

The tally is 5 PASS (Bezos, Buffett, Cook, Jobs, Psychologist), 1 SELL (Burry), 3 WAIT (Sherlock, Futurist, Anthropologist) and 3 HOLD (Biologist, Historian, Lynch). Nobody votes BUY. The Jev audit is right to warn that all twelve triggers come from one method: an explicit above-cap growth period, then the 3.18% cap, discounted at 8.19%. Nine experts ran ten-year periods. Burry, Cook and the Psychologist ran five-year periods. So the €825–€1,150 cluster of trigger prices (Buffett's €825–900 at the bottom, the Historian's and Anthropologist's €1,150 at the top) is one calculation run twelve times, not twelve confirmations. The experts differ mainly in which FCF base they chose (€3.88B company "adjusted" or €4.21B pipeline), which period length they used (five or ten years) and which growth rate they assumed (5–8%). What does count as independent is the evidence underneath, which spans five key-metric families. Cook's working capital, Burry's forensics, the Historian's analogues, the Biologist's ecology and the Anthropologist's ritual all reach the same finding, and it is a finding about the business, not the price: the business is intact. On the price there is really one witness. So I re-derive it myself in §4 and do not count heads.

3. Bull-Bear Balance and the Private Buyer Test

Every bull case, as written: Bezos says pricing power plus artisan capacity could deliver the ~8.6% FCF growth the price needs. Buffett says high-single-digit cash growth without weaker exclusivity could justify the price. Burry says pricing power, a 41% margin and net cash could support growth above his 6%. Cook says faster cash growth at 41.0% margins could justify the premium. Jobs says controlled distribution and artisan capacity sustain growth at exceptional margins. The Psychologist says distribution, capacity and pricing power support enough growth. Sherlock says a debt-free family house compounding at the 10.3% net-income CAGR it has shown since 2022 would re-rate from a two-year low. The Futurist says supply-governed scarcity lets growth persist and the market re-rates a franchise 44% below its €2,300 high. The Biologist says that if growth holds near the +7.7% revenue consensus, the multiple is earned. The Historian says this is the structural opposite of the dead brands, so 8% for a decade is plausible and the price is roughly fair. The Anthropologist says the Birkin/Kelly ritual compounds at 8–10%. Lynch says this is a 41% recurring-margin franchise growing 6–9% at constant FX that only has to hold today's multiple to earn about 11% a year for three years.

Lynch, addressed rather than dismissed. His strongest point is correct, and I adopt it. Most of the 44% fall is multiple, not earnings. FY2025 net income was €4.52B against €4.60B, down 1.7%. Recurring operating income rose 7% to €6.56B, and the margin went from 40.5% to 41.0%. Q2 2026 accelerated to +7% at constant FX, and H1 available cash flow rose 18%. The narrative broke ("Hermès never decelerates"), but the business did not. His three-year €1,750 target is FY2029 EPS of about €58 (the €6.1B Simply Wall St narrative [MEDIA]) at 30x. That target has two weaknesses, and Lynch names both himself. First, the EPS is an aggregator estimate, and its own 7.8% CAGR sits below the 10.8%/9.9% forecasts printed beside it. Second, the 30x is "the one I cannot underwrite": at 25x the same earnings give €1,457, or 4.3% a year, which fails the hurdle. My required-growth table (§4) says the same thing in owner-EPS terms over his short horizon. Over three years, Lynch's case is a bet that the multiple holds. Over a decade, my grid lets the multiple compress and asks only for growth (§4). It is a reasonable bet for an existing owner, which is why HOLD is the right word for his 2% starter. It is not a margin of safety for new money. Burry overreaches the other way. His SELL is a valuation call with no operating fracture behind it: receivables fell 12.5%, inventory fell 7.9%, and he sizes his own short at 0% and calls a valuation-led short "vulnerable."

I weight the bull argument at 40% and the bear argument at 60%. The bears do not win on the business. They win because every bull case above contains the word "could" or "if growth holds", and the decomposed cash record does not yet show the growth the price needs. The headline series move a lot depending on the starting year:

  • Pipeline FCF (€3.67B, €3.47B, €4.07B, €4.21B) compounds at 4.7% from 2022, 10.1% from 2023 and 3.4% from 2024.
  • Owner earnings (€3.40B, €3.45B, €4.15B, €4.32B) compound at 8.3% from 2022, 11.9% from 2023 and 4.1% from 2024.
  • Company adjusted FCF rose 3.2% in 2025 (€3.76B to €3.88B).

Two items distort those series, so I strip them out. Working capital: net working capital (inventory plus receivables less payables, from the year-end WORKING CAPITAL and FORENSIC rows) went €1.50B, €2.15B, €2.60B, €2.37B. That is a €0.65B build in 2023, a €0.45B build in 2024 and a €0.23B release in 2025. The French contribution: €0.34B in 2025. Its cash timing is undated, so I show the series both with and without it.

  • Owner earnings before working-capital moves were €4.10B (2023), €4.60B (2024) and €4.09B (2025), or €4.43B in 2025 with the French contribution added back. That is about 0% a year over 2023–2025, or 3.9% with the contribution added back.
  • Pipeline FCF on the same basis was €4.12B, €4.52B and €3.98B (€4.32B with the contribution added back). That is −1.7% a year over 2023–2025, or +2.4%.

These come from rounded year-end balances that include currency translation, so they are approximate. On profit rather than cash, net income adjusted for the French item grew 5.5% in 2025 (€4.86B) and recurring operating income grew 7%. The most recent datapoint, H1 2026 available cash flow +18%, is one half-year on a company-defined measure and could include the same working-capital timing.

So the bear weight does not depend on double-charging the French tax. Even with it added back, underlying cash grew about 2–4% a year over 2023–2025. Profit grew 5.5–7%. Operating investments ran at 1.25x D&A, which absorbs the difference. Netting working capital out does not strengthen the record either: the 2025 release flatters 2025 by about the same amount the builds depressed 2023–2024. The weight stays at 40/60. The H1 +18% is the one fact that would move it, once a full year confirms it.

Private Buyer Test. There is no control buyer. Insiders hold 66.98% [SEARCH per Yahoo Finance], and the Yahoo profile lists the company as "a subsidiary of H51 SAS", a structure the dossier does not detail and Sherlock declines to rely on. Institutions hold 10.33% of shares (31.30% of float), and free float is about a third. The superinvestor registry names no informed holder and shows no cost basis, only passive index lines (three Vanguard funds and Schwab). No sophisticated entry price exists to compare with ours. A hypothetical owner wanting an unlevered 8.19% would pay my central value of €1,171 (§4). One content with 7.41% would pay €1,382 on the same 7% growth. The market's €1,286 sits between those two, so the market's marginal buyer either accepts the low end of the CRP bracket or underwrites 8.1% growth on my €3.75B base. The gap between the private value and my buy zone is therefore about 0.8 points of discount rate. Whether that 0.8 points is right depends on revenue geography, and the dossier does not contain it. One more datum: the media EV series shows €206.90B in March 2026 against a ten-year mean of €122.34B, and today's EV of about €122.4B is back at that mean on earnings four times the 2015 level of €972.6M. The page's currency is ambiguous (HESAF is the USD line), so this is a direction, not a number. The de-rating is real and it is large, but de-rated is not the same as cheap.

4. Valuation: the hurdle zone, worked

Owner earnings base: €3.75B = the company's own €3.88B "adjusted free cash flow" [SEARCH per Yahoo Finance] less €0.13B of FY2025 SBC [CALC, FORENSIC BLOCK]. Owner EPS is €35.71 on 105M shares. The company figure starts from operating cash flow, which adds back equity-settled SBC by construction, and nothing in the record shows the €3.88B takes it back out. So I charge SBC once, in the base. I do not also charge the €0.2B of H1 2026 repurchases "dedicated to employee share ownership plans", because that is the cash settlement of the same cost, and charging both would count it twice. I start from the company figure rather than the pipeline's €4.21B FCF or €4.32B owner earnings (OCF less PP&E depreciation less SBC) because of how leases are treated. Under IFRS 16, lease payments sit in financing, and the BALANCE SHEET block tells us to charge them against owner earnings or add the €2.28B of lease liabilities to debt. The company figure appears to do the first; the gap is €0.33B in 2025 and €0.31B in 2024 (€4.07B vs €3.76B). So I charge leases in the flow and add the €12.30B net cash at face. That is borrowings of €0.01B against €12.31B cash, and the €2.35B forensic "debt" is essentially leases. Burry and Cook used €3.88B and also deducted €2.28B from cash, which counts the leases twice and costs about €22 a share (on my structure, €1,076 becomes €1,054 at 6%). One more possible double count is cash: if the €3.88B includes interest received, the grid capitalises net financial income (€207M, pre-tax) and also adds the principal at face. The record does not say whether it does. As a bound, removing the whole €0.207B pre-tax gives a €3.54B base and a 6% case of €1,022. I do not adopt that cut on an unknown, but it sits inside the zone and cannot raise the verdict. The base also carries the French "exceptional contribution on profits of large companies": €0.34B in FY2025 (€4.86B adjusted vs €4.52B reported, about 7.5% of reported net income and 5.2% of recurring operating income) and about €0.26B in H1 2026 (€2.5B ex-item vs €2,238M reported). It sits inside the TTM window, and its end date is not in the record. I treat it as recurring until a budget says otherwise.

Reverse DCF. At €1,286 the market cap is about €134.7B and EV about €122.4B, giving EV/FCF of 29.1x, EV/recurring operating income of 18.7x and a trailing P/E of about 29.8x on EPS of about €43.2 (Yahoo prints 31.38 at its own date). Price is 36.0x my owner EPS. Perpetual growth matching the price is about 4.6% at 8.19% and 3.8% at 7.41%, and both exceed the cap. So the price needs an explicit above-cap period. The pipeline's 14.8% "sustainable growth" (ROE 25.5% × 58% retention) is a retention rate, not a perpetual one, and I do not use it. P/B of 7.15x on book of €179.89 divided by ROE is P/E by identity and adds nothing.

5. Decision Logic: Ceiling and Floor

Ceiling (hidden value): not raised. Bezos finds no cash incinerator and no segment accounts, so there is no core to strip out. Burry's cost-dumping check has nothing to audit: there are no segments, and the stress model uses "industry default" stickiness rather than Hermès's own cost history. His forensics are clean. SBC is €0.13B (now charged in my base), 0.8% of revenue and 2.3% of €5.60B OCF (€0.06B, €0.10B, €0.14B and €0.13B over 2022–2025). Receivables are €0.42B, 2.6% of revenue. Goodwill is €0.2B and there is no acquisition habit. Under Burry's −20% test the business is still cash-generative (€2.97B). Two pieces of possible hidden value exist, and I deliberately do not count either. The first is the French contribution: if it ends, the 6% case rises from €1,076 to about €1,163, but it recurred in H1 2026. The second is €1.23B of long-term financial assets (about €11.7 a share), whose nature is unstated. The €12.3B cash pile is already counted at face. It earns little (net financial income of €207M vs €283M, roughly 1.7%), the payout is 42%, and the cash is 9.1% of market cap. That is a conservative choice that costs owners return, and Sherlock calls it a Neutral Steward, conservative to a fault. Buffett and the Futurist confirm a durable moat, not a widening one.

Floor (moat and quality): Quality row, not lowered. Buffett's verdict on pricing power is hedged, in his own words: "Pricing power looks strong, but its ultimate test remains open … Without a price versus volume split, I cannot say." The dossier confirms the price-versus-volume split is not supplied. The economics behind his "looks strong" are gross margin of 71.1%, 70.3% and 72.2% (2025/2024/2023) and ROIC of 71.8%, 70.2%, 65.2% and 60.3%. He also says the business is not anti-fragile, because demand is discretionary. Jobs rates it "Insanely Great." The Biologist classifies it as a Symbiont (Healthy) with about 93% 10-year survival. The Anthropologist calls it a Cultural Institution (Enduring). Cook grades operations A−: DIO 203 vs 226, turnover rising from 1.54 to 1.80, DPO down from 67 to 49. The four franchise-premium tests:

  1. 0 SEVERE flags: pass.
  2. Economics: unproven. Hermès's moat is markup, so the test belongs on the operating-margin route, not the ROIC route the rule reserves for redeployment compounders. Recurring operating margin is 41.0% (2025) and 40.5% (2024), but only two years are sourced against the three required. ROIC of 60.3–71.8% is printed without the pipeline's definition, so I cannot say whether goodwill sits inside it.
  3. Revenue >75% recurring: fail. These are discretionary purchases through owned stores, with no contracts or backlog. Sherlock calls it franchise-recurring, not contractual.
  4. Organizational switching costs: fail. The Biologist and Futurist agree there is no lock-in, only desire and access.

One of four passes, so the band is the default Quality row, 10x–18x. Tests 3 and 4 were written for platforms, and a Birkin fails them by kind. That is precisely why the band cannot govern here, and why I do not let its €643 ceiling masquerade as a valuation.

6. Final Decision

Decision: WAIT. The fundamentals clear every quality bar this council has. The price does not clear the hurdle with any margin.

1. Dead analogues (the casualties)

Pierre Cardin, 1970s to 2000s [OUTSIDE DOSSIER]. - Position: a leading couture and ready-to-wear name in the 1960s and 70s. - Cause: it licensed the name across an enormous range of categories (commonly cited as hundreds of licences worldwide). The name moved from aspirational to ubiquitous. - Speed: the decline took decades, not quarters. That is the key lesson.
