Munger's Verdict

MUNGER SYNTHESIS — FERRARI N.V. (RACE)

Analysis date 2026-10-08. Price $386.39 (NYSE close 2026-10-06), market cap $67.8B on 175.40M shares. Filings are in EUR, converted at one rate, 1 EUR = 1.119445; the company's own guide uses ~1.16, 3.6% higher, which would lift every USD cash figure below by the same 3.6%.

1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags. 1 MODERATE flag by: Psychologist. 4 MINOR by: Buffett, Biologist, Historian, Anthropologist. Outside the Tribunal, Bezos and Jobs also flag MODERATE, for different reasons. Bezos: "the current price requires prolonged cash-flow growth while scarcity limits volume expansion." Jobs: "electrification tests Ferrari's sensory identity." On the EV the evidence cuts both ways: a company-sourced report [MEDIA] says the Luce met its 2026 sales target within two months, while an undated, Reuters-attributed report [MEDIA] says the second EV slipped to 2028 or later. The Historian calls a Survivor Trajectory at 72% (Rolex and Patek after quartz, Porsche after the Cayenne), so no Casualty cap applies. Nobody on the council claims the franchise is decaying. The 2025 mix moved toward combustion (ICE 58% / hybrid 42%, from 48.7% / 51.3%), the EU's 2035 ban looks softened rather than tightened, and Lamborghini's and McLaren's electric cars are dated 2029 and 2030. The moat is intact. The Tribunal puts Ferrari on the Quality row of the band, and the franchise tests below decide whether it moves up to the Franchise row.

2. Franchise premium tests — 3 of 4

  1. Tribunal — PASS. 0 SEVERE.
  2. Pricing power — PASS, brand route. Gross margin 49.8% / 50.1% / 51.7% (FY2023–25, average 50.5%, no year below 48%). Operating margin 27.1% / 28.3% / 29.5% (average 28.3%, no year below 23%) [SEC XBRL 20-F; CALC]. Burry and Sherlock correctly note that the dossier's "five straight years" is wrong: 2021 was 51.3% / 25.2% and 2022 dipped to 48.0% / 24.1%. The expansion is four years long, not five.
  3. Durable demand — PASS, route (b), demand exceeding supply. Three successive 6-Ks (2026-02-10, 05-05, 07-30) say the order book "extends towards the end of 2027", later "entirely covers 2027", and it excludes the Manuale. Shipments have been held near 13,700 (13,663 → 13,752 → 13,640) while revenue rose. An earlier, undated [MEDIA] Section J source says the order book "covers production well into 2026". So the order book covers 2026 and 2027. Its value, deposits and cancellability are undisclosed, so "sold out" would overstate it. The rule names Ferrari's order book as its own example.
  4. Customers stay when prices rise — UNPROVEN, route (b). Revenue per car is computable for two full years: EUR 437k → 486k → 524k, +11.1% on volume +0.7% in FY2024 and +7.9% on volume −0.8% in FY2025. There is also one quarter: cars and spare parts +8% in Q2 2026 on deliveries −3.7% (3,366 vs 3,494). A third full year needs the 2022 shipment count, which the dossier lacks. "Revenue per car" also includes sponsorship, and no inflation series is supplied. The rule asks for three sourced years, so the test fails on evidence, not on reputation.

So the band sits on the Quality row, 10x–18x, and it cannot govern. The hurdle zone governs.

3. The Bull-Bear Balance

Bull cases, as written. Bezos: personalisation and richer mix compound cash without diluting exclusivity. Buffett, Burry and Cook make the same argument, with Burry putting a number on it (~14% FCF growth for ten years). Jobs: younger collectors carry pricing power across powertrains. Psychologist: management protects exclusivity. Sherlock: personalisation (>20% of cars revenue), the order book and capex normalisation let FCF compound 14–16%. Futurist: EBIT grows 11–12% a year and margins reach the mid-30s. Biologist: FCF grows ~12%, yet that still returns only ~9.6% from here. Historian: the scarcity machine survives the EV transition. Anthropologist: a devotee community, not a captive one. Lynch: ~13% a year to 2031 if 35x holds.

Lynch, addressed rather than dismissed. He is right on four counts. Shipments fell because of a planned seven-model changeover. The order book covers 2027. Guidance was raised on all three lines (revenue ~EUR 7.60B from 7.50B; industrial FCF ≥EUR 1.55B from 1.50B; adjusted EBITDA ≥EUR 2.97B from 2.93B at ≥39.0%). And an existing holder has no valuation reason to sell. His own arithmetic is the strongest bear exhibit, though. On consensus EPS (FY2027 10.55, probably EUR) the hurdle is earned only if the P/E holds at about 34x. A move to 24x returns −1.7% a year. Weighting his four scenarios with his own judgment weights (20/35/25/20, which are his opinion, not measured probabilities), he gets ~6.9% against a 10.2% hurdle. I publish no scenario weights of my own. His $340–365 trigger is built on a 30x exit. That is a bet that the multiple persists, not that the business grows. I accept his facts and decline his exit multiple.

What the bears are actually saying. Not one of them argues the business is impaired. The bear case is the price. FCF yield is 2.4% ($1.61B) and earnings yield 2.7% ($1.83B, 37.0x). Carry is 1.09% from the dividend, or ~2.1% with 2025's >EUR 1.3B of remuneration. Against 10.2%, the gap is ~7.8 points. The dossier's perpetuity at r = 10.19%, g = 5.32% is 21.6x FCF; the market pays 42.1x.

Jev's warning applies to me. All twelve experts reached their trigger through one basis, a growth DCF, and all twelve took the same 10.2% hurdle from the dossier. Twelve PASS/WAIT votes are one witness wearing twelve hats. If the hurdle is wrong, they are all wrong together. Section 5 shows exactly how wrong.

I weight the bull argument at 35% and the bear argument at 65%. The bulls win on the business. The bears win on the arithmetic of the price.

Private Buyer Test. No strategic buyer can exist: Exor (21.33% of capital) and Trust Piero Ferrari (10.67%) control 52.0% of the votes on one source, ~48.4% on another. A financial buyer would face the market's $69.5B EV (net debt $1.70B = $3.37B borrowings − $1.67B cash, 0.55x EBITDA) for $1.61B of FCF. That is a 2.3% yield no LBO underwrites, and this arithmetic alone carries the test: private market value sits below the quote, not above it. The strategic-holder signal is undetermined. On one occasion Exor reduced its position when Ferrari reached 50% of its GAV (it is now 38–39%). Exor's CFO frames that as concentration management, and the excerpt is truncated [MEDIA per Exor H1 2026 call]. Lynch reads it as a portfolio rule, not a valuation call. Exor's 56% discount to NAV and ~EUR 4B of cash [MEDIA] say nothing about what it would pay for Ferrari. Spier (6.48%) and Tangen (5.35%) held at 2025 Q4, but the dossier has no cost basis for either, so I cannot say whether they agree with us at a lower price. Ferrari's own buyback (1,218,103 shares at EUR 293.48) bought ~15% below today at the pipeline rate of 1.119445 ($328.5 vs $386.39). Short interest is 1.98%: there is no crowded bear to fade.

4. Decision Logic — Ceiling and Floor

Ceiling (hidden value). Bezos found no cash incinerator. There are no segment losses, and the EV programme's capital is undisclosed. No cost dumping can be shown either. The 2025 EBITDA-to-net bridge (EUR 2,772m − D&A 662m − tax 464m − finance 46m = EUR 1,600m) reconciles exactly. Buffett and the Futurist confirm a durable moat but not a widening one, since personalisation has been flat at ~20% since FY2024 [MEDIA]. Burry dismantles the pipeline's $2.38B owner earnings (3.5% owner yield). It deducts only ~$0.31B of PP&E depreciation against $1.08B of capex and $741m of total D&A, and its SBC input reads zero, a tag gap. Ceiling not raised.

Burry's stress test. The dossier's adjusted stress column is anchored to FY2025 FCF of $1.57B. At −20% revenue ($6.40B), adjusted FCF is $1.17B (18.3% margin). At −30% it is $0.97B. FCF turns negative only at about −80%. The business survives easily. The equity does not: $1.17B is a 1.7% yield on today's cap.

Floor (quality). Buffett confirms pricing power, though not anti-fragility. Jobs grades the product "Insanely Great". The Biologist calls it a healthy symbiont. The Anthropologist sees an enduring institution, with under-40 buyers at 40% of new clients (up from 30%) [MEDIA per CNBC], ~85% repeat buyers [MEDIA] and a two-year waiting list [MEDIA per YPulse]. Quality row, no downgrade.

5. Valuation

Owner earnings. I use TTM net income, $1.83B, or $10.43 a share. It charges the full D&A ($741m, which includes amortisation of capitalised development) as the maintenance bill, and it is already after IFRS 2 share-based-pay expense, so the XBRL SBC gap does not inflate it. FCF does carry the SBC add-back. The last tagged years show SBC at 0.3% of revenue (2021–22) [SEC]. Applied to $8.23B, that is ~$0.025B, or $0.14 a share, which takes the central from $242 to $238 (−1.5%). It is immaterial, and the zone already rounds over it. The $0.22B gap down to FCF ($9.18/share) is capex above D&A (1.46x, for the seven-model changeover and Luce) plus financial-services receivables (EUR 107m in FY2025). That reinvestment funds the growth I assume, so the DCF discounts FCF, not owner earnings. Charging the growth capex and then also crediting the growth would count it twice. The ROE × retention "g" of 27.6% (ROE 45.4%, P/B 15.5x on $24.93 of book) is an identity, not a forecast, and I do not use it.

Hurdle. 10.2% = 5.32% (US 10-year) + 4.23% ERP + 0.64% revenue-weighted CRP, at beta 1. The bracket runs to 12.0% on the HQ-only Italy CRP (2.46%, a total ERP of 6.69%). Terminal growth stays at or below the 5.32% cap. The currency caveat matters. The cash flows are EUR, grown at EUR rates and converted at one fixed rate, while the hurdle and the cap are USD. Holding the rate flat sets the EUR–USD rate differential to zero. If euro rates sit below dollar rates, the euro should earn that differential back, so this construction understates value. The dossier carries no EUR risk-free rate and no forward rate, so I cannot correct it from sourced inputs. The size of the bias: a 1.2-point differential acts roughly like discounting at 9%, which moves the central to ~$306 and a 9%-margin ceiling to ~$278. Both are still well below $386. The FCF here is levered (operating cash flow is after interest), so net debt is not deducted a second time. The pipeline's anchors ($99.32 Graham, $159.74 / $240.95 at 8.4% growth) print no discount rate and carry 8.4% beyond the cap, so I do not cite them.

6. TOO UNCERTAIN tripwires

None of the five tripwires fires. There are 0 SEVERE flags. The load-bearing variables are the discount rate and how long growth lasts, not a regime event. Tariffs (25% on EU autos into an Americas segment that is 31.5% of revenue), EU CO₂ fines (€95 per g/km) and the 23,000-car brake recall are earnings items, not binary bets. The tariff entry is undated and marked "Enacted" in the register, and I could not date it. It is excluded from the FCF base, so the zone overstates value if Ferrari absorbs it. Lynch's illustrative bound puts the size on it: absorbing 10% of Americas revenue costs about EUR 225m of EBIT, roughly 12% of FCF after tax, which would take the central to about $213. No expert passed on the grounds that the outcome is unknowable. This is not "too hard". It is "too dear".

7. Final Decision

Decision: WAIT. The fundamentals are clear, and the price is far above the margin-of-safety ceiling.

Why the zone moved. The 2026-10-05 run set $170–$230 at $388.33. This run sets $160–$220, a 4% move at the ceiling. That falls under the 20% threshold. No new filing drove it; the price moved under 1%, and the difference is this run's own DCF settings (10%/4% central, 12.0% floor case).

Why is it mispriced? It isn't, in the direction that would let us buy. The market is paying a scarcity premium for one of the few businesses that can raise prices ~8–11% a year on flat volume, On a flat-FX model, it is pricing that premium at an ~8% USD discount rate. That gap combines an equity-risk view and a currency view, and I cannot separate them from the dossier. It is not a mistake I can exploit. Even generous settings on both (a 9% rate) leave the central at ~$306. A brand is worth admiring; a 2.4% yield on it is not worth buying.

Moat half-life: ~25 years. The Rolex analogue says scarcity brands outlive their technology shifts. The risk vector is the 10–15-year EV identity arc, and the first readings (Luce take-up, standard-model resale near list: Roma ~$243–250k, 296 GTB ~$338k) arrive from Q4 2026.

Position Size: 0% at $386.39. At or below $220, the position would be up to 3% (Moderate conviction). Four questions remain open: the true cost of equity (beta), the EUR–USD rate differential (set to zero), how long growth lasts, and whether capex normalises after the changeover. The order book's value, deposits and cancellability are also undisclosed. The condition that came closest to changing the verdict is the hurdle. At 8%, Ferrari is fairly priced today.

1. Cultural role

Ferrari is a status symbol with a ritual wrapped around it. It is not a utility. A buyer could get more speed, comfort or reliability elsewhere. The purchase buys membership.

  • The ritual layer is Formula 1 and the Scuderia. Sponsorship, commercial and brand revenue was EUR 218m in Q1 2026, +14% [SEC: Q1 2026 filing]. The Scuderia is expected to earn the most team sponsorship in F1 [MEDIA per Yahoo Finance F1 Business Outlook 2026]. The racing team works as a recruitment funnel for the tribe. It is not just a marketing line.
  • The status layer is allocation. Ferrari does not sell to whoever pays. It manages demand "with discipline in every market reflecting our exclusivity model" [SEC: 6-K 2026-02-10]. A roughly two-year waiting list [MEDIA per YPulse] and an order book that "entirely covers 2027" [SEC: 6-K 2026-07-30] mean the scarcity is deliberate. Shipments rose from 7,255 (2014) to 13,640 (2025) [SEC: 20-F FY2025] and then plateaued, which fits a brand protecting its scarcity.
  • The brand-as-verb test cannot be run. The dossier has no "Ferrari-as-adjective" or search-share data [UNVERIFIED]. I treat the brand's cultural weight as inferred from behaviour (repeat purchase, auction records, waiting lists). It is not directly measured.
  • Cultural moments: this is a milestone purchase (exit, success, collection). It is not tied to a daily or yearly cycle. Ferrari rides wealth events, not habits.

2. Tribal dynamics

Captive vs Devotee test: devotee. Nobody is locked in. There is no contract, no switching cost and no workflow integration (customers are about 100% individuals [UNVERIFIED estimate]). If the tribe stays, it stays by choice, and the evidence points that way:

  • About 85% of business comes from repeat customers [MEDIA per Exor NV intrinsic-value article].
  • 19 Mecum Ferrari auction records were broken, including all five halo cars. Selected limited editions (LaFerrari, Enzo, SF90 XX) reportedly reach 2-3x original MSRP [MEDIA per Hagerty; Premier Auto Rentals France].
  • Gross margin rose from 48.0% (2022) to 51.7% (2025) and operating margin from 24.1% to 29.5% [SEC XBRL, CALC], on flat shipments (13,752 to 13,640). Customers pay more for the same number of cars. A captive base resents that. A devotee base pays it.

The tribe is stratified, and I will not flatter the evidence. The halo-car resale data is not proof for the volume models. The same sources show standard models trading near list: Roma about $243-250k, 296 GTB about $338k, Purosangue $400-430k [MEDIA per Premier Auto Rentals]. The tribe has an inner ring (collectors who get XX Programme and limited series) and an outer ring (buyers of standard models, closer to affluent consumers). Cultural strength is proven for the inner ring. For the outer ring it is inferred, and the outer ring supplies most of the volume.

Gatekeepers: the company controls its own gate through allocation and personalisation, which exceeded 20% of cars and spare parts revenue [MEDIA per Quartr; Investing.com]. External gatekeepers are auction houses and heritage media (Mecum, Hagerty), who currently reinforce the brand. Former chairman Montezemolo is a heritage-voice gatekeeper who is currently critical of the EV (see section 3). The excerpt is truncated.

Tribal resentment signals: the brake class action (Bosch and Ferrari, alleging knowledge since at least 2015 [MEDIA per McCready Law; plaintiff-side, unadjudicated]) and the "23,000 Ferraris Recalled" item (undated in the dossier) are the only friction items. Neither shows a tribe in revolt. Reputation and recall events belong to the safety-reputation category, not to the status category, and I rate them minor unless they spread.
