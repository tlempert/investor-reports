Starbucks as an investment

A brand winning back customers faster than it is winning back margin.

I would not buy Starbucks at $94.86. My conviction is Moderate: the central risk is that the one North American margin print the file can date shows the recovery has not reached the till, even as traffic has turned. The paradox: the moat looks intact — Rewards habit, scale, brand — while the unit economics it protects remain about 590bp lighter than two years ago7. Decision: WAIT, 0% position, buy zone $26–$4918.

What you would own

Company-operated stores are the core: 82.7% of FY2025 revenue ($30,744.8M27; 82.7%27) from roughly 41,304 stores in 89 markets, 67% licensed26. International and Channel Development (grocery, foodservice, the Nestlé alliance) round it out32. The switching cost a reader would feel is the Rewards app: 34.6M members, a claimed 5.6x visit-frequency lift, about 40% of US company-operated sales30 — a habit, not a contract, now facing a loyalty backlash.

The latest quarter

The newest hard number is Q3 FY2026: consolidated operating margin 14.4%, up 430bp year over year, EPS $0.85 (+70%); global comps +7.9%; adjusted EPS guidance raised to $2.55–$2.6511. Whether the 14.4% is GAAP or adjusted is not stated. My interpretation: encouraging and unusable — one unpinned, consolidated figure cannot show whether North America specifically has recovered.

The bull case and the counterargument

The bull case: three straight FY2026 quarters of comp acceleration (+4% → +6.2% → +7.9%) and two guidance raises mean the FY2025 trough was self-inflicted and correctable [12, 13; filing/search]. The counterargument is economic: the one dated North American margin print since — 9.9%, versus 18% two years earlier, in the +6.2%-comp Q2 FY2026 quarter10 — sits roughly 5.6 points below the path my normalized case requires. Each 100bp of North American margin is worth about $0.18 of owner earnings per share9, so this is not a rounding gap. Traffic and margin have decoupled: price and volume rose together in two of three FY2026 quarters [12, 13], yet the segment carrying most of the store base has not shown a matching margin print. The moat weakens if that decoupling is structural share loss rather than a lag in operating leverage.

How I would make the decision

I would buy inside $26–$49 while North American margin evidence keeps pointing toward, not away from, the 17.3% my normalized case assumes.

Conditions that would support buying

  • North America margin at or above the normalized path, for two consecutive quarters
  • The Q3 FY2026 14.4% print confirmed as GAAP
  • A disclosed debt-maturity ladder with no near-term refinancing risk

Conditions that would support waiting

  • The next North America margin still runs below that path
  • Comp gains keep showing up mostly outside North America
  • Dividend payout stays above 100% of GAAP earnings with no stated fix

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • North America transaction declines prove structural across several more quarters
  • Rewards engagement deteriorates following the loyalty backlash
  • North America margin settles at or below FY2025's 13.9% core for a full year

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Estimated buyback prices averaged ~$125 (FY2022), ~$186 (FY2023) and ~$139 (FY2024)21 — all above today's $94.86 — while total shareholder returns fell $3.8B to $2.8B and diluted shares crept back up from a 1,134M trough to 1,140.0M22. The $2.48 dividend, a 2.61% yield, is a 143% payout of FY2025 GAAP net income, covered only ~1.05x by TTM owner earnings20. My interpretation: management bought at higher prices than a value investor would choose and now pays a dividend the year's earnings didn't cover — a consistency problem, not a solvency one, given $3.61B of cash and an undrawn $3.0B revolver24.

Price discipline without false precision

The floor, $26.00, is the deep-distress price: 10x TTM owner earnings, no growth17. The ceiling, $48.75, is the most I'd pay for the normalized, not-yet-recovered business16. The zone is honest only to about a dollar either way, since normalized owner EPS is itself judgment, varied $2.64–$3.684.

Final investment view

Verdict: WAIT — 0% position. This is a real, moderately stressed franchise, not yet a broken one — traffic has turned before margin has. At 36.6x owner earnings, today's price already pays for a fully repaired business; I'd rather own the repair once North America's own numbers, not the consolidated blend, confirm it.
