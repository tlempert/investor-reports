Evolution AB as an investment

An exceptional franchise is available at a fair price, but the earnings cushion is thin.

I would buy Evolution with Moderate conviction. The central risk is that European regulation, weakening cash conversion and a credible competitor are beginning to erode the toll rate before expansion elsewhere can compensate. The paradox is a business with formidable scale and cash economics but incomplete evidence of durable switching costs. At SEK 865.40, I would take a 2% position: the ledger values the business centrally at SEK 1,014, sets a SEK 927 purchase ceiling and SEK 638 floor, and reports an 8.6% margin of safety between central value and ceiling.12

What you would own

Evolution operates studios, dealers and streaming infrastructure for live blackjack, roulette, baccarat and game shows, alongside RNG games. Operators pay recurring commissions of roughly 10–20% of gross gaming revenue; the network reaches 870 operators and holds an estimated 60% live-casino share.3 Customers experience the moat as dependable content, regulatory integration and global studio capacity, although contracts are typically about three years and aggregators may make adding rival products easier.4

The latest quarter

Evolution has no 8-K or 10-Q because it is not SEC-registered. Q2 FY2026 revenue was EUR 517.8M, down 1.2% year over year, while EBITDA was EUR 341M and the margin was 65.9%.7 In the cash-conversion series, revenue fell 0.6% while operating cash flow fell 18.9%; quarterly FCF was SEK 0.30B at a 50% margin.8 My interpretation: one quarter does not establish deterioration, but another weak cash result could remove the valuation cushion.

The bull case and the counterargument

The strongest case is that scale compounds: Evolution’s estimated 60% share, vertically integrated studios and broad distribution should let growth in the Americas and Asia overwhelm Europe’s contraction. Constant-currency revenue grew 4.9% in Q4 FY2025 despite a reported decline of 3.7%.9 The council nevertheless weights the bull case at 45% and the bear case at 55%.10

The counterargument is economic: competitors and aggregators may pressure the commission before displacing Evolution’s distribution. The dossier’s stress test illustrates resilience but not immunity: after a 20% revenue decline, its pre-tax, pre-capex profit proxy remains SEK 0.45B on SEK 1.89B of revenue, a 23.8% margin.11 This is not an FCF forecast and uses generic cost-stickiness assumptions; actual EBITDA margins have recently remained above 65%.7

Primary evidence qualifies both sides. Pragmatic Play is described as the first significant competitive threat, but its estimated share is only about 5%.12 One customer spends more than EUR 204M annually and has an identified in-sourcing risk; the top-five concentration figure of roughly 41% is dated end-2023.13 The UK duty increase to 40% is enacted, while the New Jersey investigation closed without action.14 The moat could weaken through multi-homing, customer in-sourcing or sustained regulatory cost growth.

How I would make the decision

The purchase thesis is that owner earnings stabilize before competition or regulation materially lowers the toll rate.

Conditions that would support buying

  • Cash conversion recovers while reported and constant-currency revenue converge.
  • Expansion markets continue offsetting European contraction.
  • Competitive share remains stable and operator relationships remain direct.
  • Management explains capital returns and the dividend suspension coherently.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Cash flow remains volatile without a working-capital explanation.
  • Segment disclosure remains insufficient to distinguish investment from deterioration.
  • Aggregator exposure and SBC remain undisclosed.
  • The market price approaches the appraisal ceiling without operating confirmation.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Owner earnings continue contracting.
  • The largest customer begins moving live casino in-house.
  • The challenger’s gains become sustained rather than experimental.
  • Regulatory failures threaten licences or distribution.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

The buyback is active and crossed a 5.0122% treasury-holding threshold, while founder ownership was about 10.6%.15 But historical share counts mix issued and outstanding bases, and SBC is unbounded. The council therefore requires buybacks to reduce the true diluted count at prices below intrinsic value; repurchases cannot be credited merely because cash declined or treasury holdings rose.
