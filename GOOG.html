Munger's Verdict

GOOG (Alphabet Inc., Class C): Munger Synthesis, 2026-10-30

Price $278.22. Market cap about $3.42T on 12,294M all-class shares (the 5,579M build-log figure is roughly Class C alone, 5,527M). Net cash is $140.0B, so EV is about $3.28T.

Basis discipline. The TTM P/E of 14.5x is gain-inflated. TTM net income of $235.69B is a 50.0% net margin, against 32.8% in FY2025, and it carries Q2 2026 net income of $112.1B, which includes a $99.0B net gain, mostly unrealized gains on equity securities. Implied TTM EPS is $19.17. The FY2025 P/E is 25.7x on $10.81 diluted EPS, and even that year is not clean: $24.1B of equity gains pushed FY2025 net income ($132.17B) above operating income ($129.0B). EV to FY2025 operating income is 25.4x. TTM ROIC (30.1%) and ROE (44.3%) inherit the same inflation.

On the cash side: - TTM FCF is $19.75B, a 0.6% yield, crushed by $159.8B of capex.

- FY2025 FCF was $73.27B, a 2.1% yield. - TTM owner earnings are $121.15B, a 3.5% yield that uses depreciation as the maintenance proxy.

The hurdle is a 9.4–10.4% local cost of equity. That is the 4.93% USD 10-year plus a 4.46% ERP (4.23% mature plus 0.23% country premium) = 9.39% at beta 1, plus up to 1.0 point for ad cyclicality, the capex cycle and antitrust. I use the HQ end of the country premium because 52% of revenue is outside the US (US 48%, EMEA 29%, APAC 17%, Other Americas 6%).

1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags. 3 MODERATE flags, from Buffett, the Historian and the Psychologist. 2 MINOR, from the Biologist and the Anthropologist. Outside the Tribunal, Bezos, Burry, Cook, Jobs and the Futurist flag MODERATE, and Sherlock and Lynch flag MINOR. Nobody on the council flags SEVERE.

Read the MODERATE flags for what they actually say. None of them argues erosion: - Buffett calls it "a capital-intensive fortress, rather than imminent replacement." - The Historian scores a Survivor Trajectory at 70%, not a Casualty Trajectory, so the 14x cap does not apply. - The Psychologist's flag concerns per-share delivery under founder control.

The franchise data back them up. FY2025 Search paid clicks rose 6% and cost per click 7%. The Services margin went 35.2% → 39.8% → 40.7%. Q2 2026 Search & other revenue rose 17% to $63.3B [MEDIA], though the dossier notes Search growth eased about two points in the latest quarter. Q3 query growth was the strongest in five years [MEDIA]. The cracks are at the edges: Google Network revenue fell 1.9% on impressions down 7%, and publishers and regulators are organizing against it.

The row is Quality (10x–18x). Whether the moat exists is not the question. What it now costs to keep is.

2. The Bull-Bear Balance

Lynch first. He is the counter-weight, and he does not argue BUY: he is WAIT at 58% with a trigger of $245–250. I take his points one at a time.

  1. "The business is not broken." Q3 revenue was $127.6B, up 24.7%. Operating income was $40.6B, up 30%. Q3 capex of $51.4B came in under the $55.2B consensus, and the 2026 guide held at $195–205B. I agree, and I give the Reuters "revenue miss, cash burn worsens" headline no weight as evidence of decay.
  2. The PEG is near 1 (EV about 20x annualized Q3 operating income, about 24x NOPAT). That is true on the accounting line. But depreciation plus SBC inside TTM owner earnings is only $58.4B, against $159.8B of capex. The Q3 margin has already slipped to 31.8% from Q2's 34.1% ($40.8B on $119.8B). The PEG prices earnings before the depreciation from this build arrives. The Futurist and the Historian, on six-year server lives [JUDGMENT], put that bill $50–80B a year higher by 2028.
  3. His sum-of-the-parts:
  4. Services: $139.4B of operating income at 18x after tax, about $2.1T.
  5. Cloud: annualized at 30x, about $0.9T.
  6. Losses capitalized: about −$0.3T.
  7. Plus the SpaceX stake (about $94B) and $140.0B of net cash.
  8. Total: $2.9–3.0T, or $240–245 a share. That is below the price. If the SpaceX mark already sits inside the $140.0B of net cash (see section 4), his sum counts it twice, and the right figure is about $7.65 lower, roughly $232–237. By the counter-weight's own private-value proxy, the market already pays full value for Cloud.
  9. His probability-weighted three-year value of about $330 (30/45/25 bull/base/bear) works out to about 6.2% a year. That is below the hurdle.

The decisive fact of this council is that the optimist cannot get $278 to clear 9.4% either.

Every BULL CASE line: - Bezos: Search keeps growing while Cloud converts backlog to cash faster than infrastructure spending rises. - Buffett: Search keeps its monetization, and Cloud converts contracted demand faster than replacement costs rise. - Burry: Search holds, backlog converts profitably, and utilization restores cash after capex and dilution. - Cook: contracted Cloud demand fills the infrastructure while Search funds the expansion. - Jobs: Gemini turns distribution and TPUs into one experience customers choose. - Psychologist: Gemini deepens existing habits while Cloud converts spend into cash. - Sherlock: the $514B backlog and the roughly 35.5% Cloud margin show the capex turning into contracted, high-margin revenue. - Futurist: backlog at 5.2x Cloud run-rate plus TPU scale lets revenue compound at 15%+ while capex plateaus, with FCF of $400B+ by 2036. - Biologist: a keystone toll-collector compounds owner earnings above 12%. - Historian: the Apple/Microsoft pattern, with owner earnings compounding at 15%. - Anthropologist: a seven-product, 2B-user habit base gets converted into Cloud and AI revenue. - Lynch: earnings compound near 20%, which at 24x makes about $460 in three years.

Eleven of the twelve bull cases are one sentence: the capex converts to cash. Only Jobs's case is about the product. That tells you exactly where the bet sits.

The bear's best facts are Burry's: - There is a $101.40B gap between owner earnings and FCF. - At fine-adjusted TTM operating cash flow of $184.75B, a $200B capex year is −$15.25B of FCF. - Q2 and Q3 FCF were −$5.86B and −$9.06B. Q3 operating cash flow fell 12.6% YoY while revenue rose 24.7% (partly the $5.2B EC fine and a TPU inventory build). - Analysts put 2027 capex above $300B. - Borrowings went from $49.09B at FY2025 to $125.10B, after $37.3B of senior notes issued in 2025. - The share count rose from 12,088M to 12,230M (+1.2%) despite $45.4B of FY2025 repurchases. Net of $24.95B of SBC, those repurchases bought only $20.5B of shrinkage.

Sherlock adds that the 2022–2025 cannibal record was real: 12,849M → 12,088M (−5.9%) on $59.3B, $61.5B, $62.2B and $45.7B of repurchases. It has paused.

I weight the bull argument at 40% and the bear argument at 60%. The bulls have the better facts about the business. The bears have the better facts about the cash. At this price, cash decides.

Independence. The Jev audit finds 10 of 12 experts used a scenario grid or DCF. The tally reads 5 PASS + 1 SELL against 5 WAIT + 1 HOLD, but that is not twelve views.

The published triggers and what separates them: - PASS camp ($140–195), all five years of growth then a 3% terminal: - Bezos: $148, on a $90B base. - Buffett: $140, which is his $163 on a $100B base less 14% headroom. - Jobs: $160, on $100B. - Cook: $149–173, on $121.15B at 5% growth with no net-cash credit. - Psychologist: $195, on $121.15B at 10%. - Burry's SELL reassesses at $180, a 25% margin of safety on $240 ($121.15B, 10% for ten years). - WAIT/HOLD camp ($200–250): - Sherlock: $205–245 (FY2025 FCF at 12%, or $121.15B at 8%, with a 4% terminal). - Futurist: $200–220 (a revenue and FCF-margin ramp). - Biologist: $215–240 ($100B, 10% for ten years, 4% terminal). - Historian: about $225 ($121.15B at 8% with a 3% terminal). - Lynch: $245–250 (three-year probability-weighted multiples). - Anthropologist (HOLD): $200–225 (FY2025 FCF at 12%, 4% terminal).

The inputs that separate the camps are: - the growth duration (five years against ten); - the terminal rate (3% against 3–4%); - the starting base ($90–121.15B, or FY2025 FCF); - the growth rate (5–12%); - whether net cash is credited; - each expert's own headroom (0–25%).

Duration and terminal rate do most of the work. On my $100B base at 9.9%, moving from ten years with a 4% terminal ($238) to five years with a 3% terminal ($174) is −$64, of which the terminal alone is about −$22 ($238 → $216). The clustering of triggers is one calculation in many hats, not agreement. So I do not count heads. I argue the duration and the terminal rate in section 3.

Private Buyer Test. No strategic acquirer or PE firm can buy a $3.4T company, and the dossier holds no private-market value. Lynch's sum-of-the-parts ($240–245) is the nearest proxy. It uses segment multiples rather than my owner-earnings DCF, but its 18x and 30x are judgments of the same kind as my discount rate, so it is a sanity check, not a second witness. "Smart money" doesn't help either. Diamond Hill initiated in Q3 2026 and Brown Advisory featured the stock in Q2, both at about $344–348, and no cost bases are given. Nothing shows informed capital buying below today's price.

3. Valuation: what the owner actually gets

The base. Pipeline owner earnings of $121.15B are operating cash flow of $179.55B less about $58.4B of depreciation plus SBC.

Capex runs well ahead of that: - FY2025 capex was $91.4B against $21.1B of PP&E depreciation, 4.3x. - TTM capex is $159.8B. - About 65% of Q3 spend went into servers and networking. - Item 1A warns that technology changes "could affect expected useful lives" and of capacity "that cannot easily be redeployed."

The raw 10-K adds a point no expert raised. The July 2025 tax law allows accelerated depreciation and immediate R&D expensing, "the effects of which are included in operating cash flows" for FY2025. Operating cash flow is flattered by deferred cash taxes. The $5.2B fine pushes the TTM figure the other way.

I normalize owner earnings to $100B [JUDGMENT]: $121.15B less about $21B of maintenance that the depreciation proxy has not yet charged.

The $21B is not my own derivation. It is the experts' judgment, adopted: Buffett, Jobs ("roughly $21B additional annual maintenance") and the Biologist all start from $100B. I can offer one cross-check, and it is a sanity check, not a derivation: - The depreciation proxy inside owner earnings is about $33.5B [CALC: $58.4B depreciation plus SBC, less FY2025 SBC of $24.95B as a TTM stand-in]. - The server share of one year's spend, 65% × $159.8B, on six-year lives [JUDGMENT] adds about $17.3B a year. - So the haircut is roughly one more year's server cohort reaching the depreciation line.

I sweep the base from $90B (Bezos) to $121.15B (the pipeline).

I do not use the $19.75B of TTM FCF. That would double-penalize a capex cycle that has validated demand: - Backlog was $242.8B at FY2025 and "over $460 billion" after Q1 2026 [SEC]. - It was $514B after Q2 [MEDIA]. That is the least well sourced figure, up 12% in the quarter. - "Just over 50%" was to be recognized within 24 months. That statement is dated 2025-12-31 and describes the $242.8B backlog [SEC]. The backlog has since roughly doubled on undisclosed contracts, so the $514B has no disclosed conversion profile. - It is 5.2x annualized Q2 Cloud revenue of $99.2B, and 1.09x TTM total revenue.

I do not use the full $121.15B either. The Historian's sentence holds: "the first year of an overbuild looks like growth capex and the third looks like maintenance."

Why ten years at 10%, then 4%: the argument for and against.

For a longer runway: - Owner earnings compounded about 26% a year over FY2022–25 ($58.66B → $118.62B). - Revenue grew 8.7%, 13.9% and 15.1% in FY2023–25, and 24.7% in Q3 2026. - The Services margin rose from 35.2% to 40.7%. - Backlog is 5.2x the annualized Cloud run-rate. - 15 products have more than half a billion users each.

For a shorter one: - Only the December 2025 backlog has a disclosed conversion profile. - The counterparties are undisclosed. - 2027 capex is guided "significantly" higher. - Search growth eased about two points in the latest quarter. - Advertising is 73.2% of revenue and terminable at will.

The growth bridge. 10% owner-earnings growth is not the revenue rate. On the $100B base, the owner-earnings margin is 21.2% of TTM revenue ($471.1B).

The depreciation not yet charged: - The Historian's run-rate depreciation estimate of about $72B [JUDGMENT, using the analysts' >$300B 2027 capex]. - Less the about $33.5B depreciation proxy. - Less my $21B haircut. - Leaves about $18B. On about $591B of 2028 revenue (TTM grown 12% for two years [JUDGMENT]) and after a 17% tax rate, that is roughly a 2.5-point margin drag, taking the margin from 21.2% to about 18.8%.

To deliver 10% owner-earnings growth over ten years through that drag, revenue must compound about 11.4%. That is below the FY2024–25 rate but above FY2023's 8.7%, and it gives no credit for further operating leverage. The 8% and 12% rows bracket the bridge, at about 9.3% and 13.4% revenue growth.

Terminal rate. I use 4%, under the 4.93% USD cap and roughly nominal-GDP growth for a business with 52% of its revenue outside the US. Choosing 3% instead lowers the central value to $216 and the ceiling to $200. That is a judgment worth $19 at the ceiling, and I name it as one.

How the zone treats the duration dispute. I do not resolve it by fiat. The zone spans it: the $172 floor sits beside the PASS camp's five-year, 3%-terminal case ($174 at 9.9%), and the $219 ceiling is the ten-year case at the top of the hurdle. A buyer at the floor pays for five years of growth. A buyer at the ceiling pays for ten.

The price implies about 12.2% a year for ten years on $100B, or about 9.6% on $121.15B (both at 9.9%). The no-growth value of $100B at 9.9% is about $94 a share, including cash. The dossier's equivalent at 9.39% on $121.15B is $105 plus $11.

None of the pipeline anchors is usable: - The Graham Floor of $198.20 back-solves to a 5.0% capitalization, which is the risk-free rate, not the hurdle. - The pipeline DCFs ($50.10 on TTM FCF and $248.88 on owner earnings at 15% growth) differ only in their capex treatment, which is the open question itself. - The printed g of 42.4% (ROE 44.3% × 96% retention) is gain-inflated and nine times the growth cap.

Implied multiples. The central $238 is 29.3x owner EPS and 22.0x FY2025 GAAP EPS (which carries gains). The market pays 34.2x owner EPS and 25.7x FY2025 EPS. META trades at 21.0x TTM. The 17.8x "peer median" is GOOG's own gain-inflated 14.5x averaged with its duplicate, GOOGL, so it is meaningless.

Am I overcorrecting a business I grade A−? The capex risk is charged in two pockets, so I name what each one carries. - Pocket 1, the $21B base haircut, is the expected level: replacement spending the depreciation proxy has not yet charged. - Pocket 2, the up-to-1-point hurdle premium, is dispersion. It covers ad-cycle beta, unsized antitrust risk, and the tail of an overbuild: Item 1A's capacity "that cannot easily be redeployed."

These are different risks, a mean and a variance, so I remove neither. Setting the ceiling at the top of the hurdle is the margin of safety applied to pocket 2; it is not a third charge on the mean.

4. Decision Logic: ceiling and floor

Ceiling (hidden value). - Bezos's shutdown test: $129.0B of operating income plus the $7.5B Other Bets loss (which includes a $2.1B Waymo compensation charge) gives $136.6B. Adding the $16.8B Alphabet-level loss gives $153.3B for Services plus Cloud (38.2% on $401.4B), so EV is 21.4x core operating income. - The cost-dumping check does not clear. Services employee compensation stayed flat ($46.2B, $44.6B, $45.1B) while Alphabet-level losses rose $7.6B ($9.2B → $10.5B → $16.8B). Those frontier-model costs are consumed by Search and Cloud, and segment margins exclude them. - Waymo had $382M of Q2 revenue against a $1.8B loss, and its $16.0B round was mostly Alphabet-funded. Closing Other Bets adds about $7.5B pre-tax, roughly $0.50 a share, under 7% of owner EPS. The incinerator that matters is capex, and it cannot be shut off without the moat paying for it. - The SpaceX stake (about $94B, 2.7% of market cap, about $7.65 a share). My policy is that equity marks do not count: the mark moved by an $8.7B loss in Q3. The dossier does not establish which balance-sheet line holds it. Before the June IPO it was a non-marketable holding (FY2025 gains were "primarily" on non-marketable securities [SEC]). After the IPO it may sit either in the $135.23B of long-term financial assets, which I already exclude, or in the $265.10B of cash and short-term investments behind the $140.0B of net cash. - In the first case, the policy is already applied. - In the second, every zone figure already counts it, and applying the policy would lower each by about $7.65, giving a ceiling near $211 and a floor near $164. - I do not move the zone on an unestablished classification. I carry the −$7.65 as an unresolved downside, and I withdraw the earlier claim that the stake is a cushion under the floor. - The long-term financial assets ($135.23B, composition unstated) are excluded. Counting all of them would add about $11.00 a share. That exclusion is now a ledger input.

No ceiling raise.

Floor (moat and quality). - Buffett: pricing power is demonstrated, with limits. Alphabet is resilient, not anti-fragile: advertising is 73.2% of revenue, and advertisers "can terminate contracts at any time." - Jobs: "Sugared Water." Reach is proven; love is unmeasured (no NPS, churn or NRR). - Biologist: Keystone (Thriving), about 93% ten-year survival, and about 70% that the 40.7% extraction rate holds. - Anthropologist: Cultural Institution (Enduring). 12% of Gen Z would sooner use ChatGPT, against 15% of Millennials and Gen X, so there is no generational cliff.

That gives the Quality row, not Franchise.

Franchise premium tests (2 of 4): 1. Pass. The Moat Tribunal returned 0/5 SEVERE. 2. Fail. On the platform route, consolidated operating margin was 27.4%, 32.1% and 32.0% (FY2023–25), under 35%. The test reads the company, not Services' 40.7%. The brand-route numbers would pass (gross margin averages 58.2% on 56.6/58.2/59.7, operating margin averages 30.5%), but that route is for physical-product markups, so using it here would be a misuse. The ROIC route (21.5–29.3%) is for capital-light redeployers, and the dossier does not state whether its ROIC carries the $33.4B of goodwill. A $160B-a-year spender is not capital-light. 3. Fail. Well under 27% of revenue is recurring. Cloud (14.6%) plus subscriptions, platforms and devices (11.9%) is the most it could be, and that second line includes devices and Play, which are transactional. Advertising, at 73%, is terminable at will. The order-book route covers only Cloud, 15% of revenue. There is no retention data. 4. Pass, by route (c), marketplace economics. The Search auction raised price and volume together (CPC +7%, paid clicks +6%) while the TAC rate fell from 20.7% to 20.3% and the Services margin rose from 35.2% to 40.7%. Route (b), price-led growth, is unproven on one year of data.

Decision: WAIT.

1. DEAD ANALOGUES (the casualties)

The relevant question is not "who is Google" but "what kind of franchise is this". It is an intent-capture layer (a place where a user states a need and a merchant pays to answer it), funded by advertisers who "can terminate contracts at any time" [SEC Item 1A], 73.2% of revenue [CALC]. The casualties are the previous holders of that kind of position.

Kodak (film, to 2012). [HISTORICAL] Roughly 85-90% of US film and a large share of camera sales in the mid-1970s; built the first digital camera in 1975; film revenue peaked around 1999-2000; Chapter 11 in January 2012. The decline from the revenue peak to bankruptcy took about 12 years, and the stock looked "cheap" for most of it. Management narrative: digital as an extension of film economics (printing, kiosks, consumables), i.e. try to charge the old toll on the new road. Lesson for Alphabet: the incumbent invented the threat, and revenue kept rising for years after the disruption began, so current growth does not disprove disruption. The dossier shows Search +17% in Q2 2026 and query growth "the highest in the last five years" [MEDIA]; Kodak's film business also grew while digital cameras were shipping.

Yahoo (portal and display, 1998-2017). [HISTORICAL] Peak market value about $125B in early 2000; rejected Microsoft's roughly $44.6B offer in 2008; core assets sold to Verizon in 2017 for about $4.8B. It outsourced search to Google in 2002 and so lost the intent layer to the company that built a better auction. Lesson: the winner of one layer (portals) did not win the next (search) despite being in the room. Mirror image for Alphabet: it is the layer-winner now, and the question is whether an answer engine is the next layer. The dossier's evidence is weak and mixed: ChatGPT at 60.7% of "AI search" against Gemini 15.0% in January 2026 [MEDIA per Sedestral, flagged low quality]; Google's share of queries "below 70%" (undated) [MEDIA]. Note one further Yahoo pattern: by 2012-2015 its Alibaba stake was worth more than the rest of the company. Alphabet's SpaceX stake of about $94B is 2.7% of market cap [CALC], so no stub-value problem, but the $99.0B Q2 2026 unrealized gain [MEDIA] shows the same earnings-quality drift.

Yellow Pages and newspaper classifieds (1995-2012). [HISTORICAL] Local-intent advertising with strong lock-in. US newspaper classified revenue fell from roughly $20B around 2000 to roughly $5B a decade later; R.H. Donnelley and Idearc filed for bankruptcy in 2009 after leveraged deals in 2006-07, with equity down over 90%. Mechanism that matters here: price rose while volume fell (rate cards up, ad counts down) until it stopped working. The dossier shows the same pattern in miniature in Google Network: FY2025 revenue -1.9%, impressions -7%, cost-per-impression +7% [SEC, CALC]. It is only 7.4% of revenue [CALC: $29.8B / $402.8B], but it is the segment exposed to publishers. Contrast: in Search, paid clicks +6% and cost-per-click +7% [SEC], so volume is still rising. Second mechanism: leverage turned a slow decline into a wipeout. Alphabet has net cash of $140.0B [CALC] against borrowings of $125.1B [CALC], so that route to ruin is closed for now.

Capital-cycle analogue (not a disruption casualty): Cisco, Lucent and Nortel, 1996-2002. [HISTORICAL] Cisco's market value peaked near $550B in March 2000 at an extreme multiple, fell about 88% by late 2002, and took more than two decades to regain its nominal peak; Lucent and Nortel were vendor-financing customers that then failed. The franchise survived; the shareholder did not earn the hurdle. This is the analogue that matters most for the price, not the moat. Differences that favor Alphabet: it funds capex from $179.55B of operating cash flow [CALC] and not from junk debt; the multiple is 25.7x FY2025 EPS [CALC], not 100x+. Differences that hurt: servers and networking (about 65% of Q3 spend [MEDIA]) depreciate over years, not decades, so an overbuild hits earnings fast; and the Cloud backlog counterparties are undisclosed [dossier], with an AI-lab TPU arrangement visible in the threat register (Anthropic, multi-gigawatt [dossier threat 10]). If the backlog depends on customers whose own funding depends on capital markets, that is the vendor-financing shape. This is inference, not a filed fact.
