Rightmove as an investment

An exceptional toll road whose power to raise the toll is now the central risk

I would wait, with moderate conviction. Rightmove’s audience remains formidable, but the competition claim targets the pricing power that converts that audience into cash. The council split 5 BUY, 5 HOLD, 1 PASS and 1 SELL, with trigger prices spanning £3.19–£7.38.12 My decision is WAIT with a 0% position: at £4.81, the £5.09 central value offers too little protection; I would buy at or below the £4.58 ceiling, with £3.19 as the floor.12

What you would own

Rightmove is a consumer property-search habit funded entirely by business advertisers. Estate agents, new-home developers, commercial-property firms, mortgage providers and rental businesses pay for listings, leads and premium tools; consumers search free. Revenue by activity is not disclosed. The moat, as customers experience it, is audience dependency: agents can advertise elsewhere, but leaving Rightmove risks losing access to the place buyers visit directly.

Cash flow and shareholder economics

The ledger uses £0.308 of owner earnings per share and a 6.4% owner yield, beside a roughly 6.3% free-cash-flow yield.1 They are close because owner earnings subtract PP&E depreciation as a proxy for maintenance capex, plus stock compensation, from operating cash flow; they are not independent evidence.3 The dividend yield is about 2.2%, well below the 8–9% local hurdle, so growth and repurchases must provide most of the return.3

The latest quarter

The latest disclosure is an unaudited H1 RNS rather than an 8-K: revenue rose 7% from £211.7M to £225.8M, underlying margin was 69%, and underlying EPS grew 6%. Guidance was cut to 6–8% revenue growth while underlying EPS guidance remained at least 5%.4 FY2025 free cash flow of £0.23B was about 105% of £0.22B net income.3 My interpretation: cash conversion remains sound, but the investment ramp has reduced near-term operating leverage.

The bull case and the counterargument

The strongest bull case is that consumer habit, direct traffic above 85%, record agent retention and premium-tier adoption keep strengthening the network while AI is absorbed into the product; LLM referrals remain below 0.5% and flat.6 The competition claim seeks just under £1.5B and has support or expressed interest from more than 250 agencies.5

The economic counterargument is visible in agent economics: in one comparison, the monthly fee rose £93 while commission increased about £50 per property and house prices rose 1.3%.9 These figures have different bases and do not establish customer profitability, which is undisclosed. They nevertheless explain why pricing could face deterrence, settlement terms, customer resistance or a possible regulatory review even if traffic remains dominant.

My interpretation: the audience moat is intact; the exposed leg is the durability of above-inflation pricing. Multi-homing, deteriorating customer economics or compounding LLM traffic could weaken it.

How I would make the decision

I would buy only if price compensates me for uncertain fee durability without requiring the audience moat to fail.

Conditions that would support buying

  • The claim is dismissed or resolved without conduct restrictions.
  • Retention remains strong while agents continue choosing premium tiers.
  • Management explains capital-return funding without weakening the balance sheet.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • The damages process remains unresolved.
  • Growth depends chiefly on further fee increases.
  • Strategic Growth Areas remain undisclosed despite being presented as material growth drivers.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Direct consumer traffic begins migrating persistently to substitutes.
  • Retention deteriorates alongside weaker pricing.
  • Repurchases create leverage without durable per-share improvement.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Shares fell from 825M to 759M between FY2022 and FY2025 while stock compensation remained 2.0% of revenue.3 Management then completed a £90M H1 buyback and guided more than £400M of capital returns over the following 12 months.4 That exceeds annual free cash flow after dividends, so the missing test is whether cash or borrowing funds the difference. Consistent repurchases create value only when their price and financing remain disciplined.

Price discipline without false precision

The buy zone is £3.19–£4.58.1 The lower end requires no growth; the upper end is the £5.09 central value less a 10% margin and sits near the bottom of the council’s quality band.12 The width is honest: litigation, fee durability and capital-return funding do not justify a narrower estimate.
