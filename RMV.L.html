Munger's Verdict

Munger Synthesis — Rightmove plc (RMV.L)

Date: 2026-09-23. Price: £4.81. The dossier's market cap is £3.65B on the FY2025 year-end count of 759M (E057). The pipeline's owner yield uses a smaller, post-buyback count (see §5). Local cost of equity is 8–9% (UK gilt ~4–4.5% + ~4–4.5% ERP), a developed-market rate (E005).

Jobs likes the consumer habit and not the paying customer. Burry has no quarrel with the accounts, only with the toll. Lynch sees a monopoly at a stalwart's multiple. Buffett sees a wonderful company at a fair price. They are all describing the same fact from different chairs: Rightmove raised ARPA 6% to £1,621 in 2025 after 6.5% in 2024 (E012), and 250-odd of the agents who paid it back a damages claim calling that abuse (E092, E125). The decision is how much to pay for a toll whose future increases may be restrained. That restraint could arrive through several channels, and §2 names each one and its source.

1. The Moat Tribunal

Moat Tribunal: 0/5 SEVERE flags. 5 MODERATE flags from Buffett, Biologist, Historian, Anthropologist and Psychologist. 0 MINOR/NONE. Outside the Tribunal, Burry flags SEVERE and Lynch MINOR, and I adjudicate Burry's flag on the evidence in §2. All five MODERATE flags name one cause. None names traffic erosion: traffic is >85% direct/organic (E013) with 75–90% of UK portal time (E011). None names AI: LLM referrals are <0.5% and flat (E119). The shared cause is the pricing-power leg under Newman v Rightmove (CAT 1771/7/7/26, E128). So this is one MODERATE concern seen five times, and the rule gives the normal Quality Floor of 15x–18x. The Historian's call is Survivor Trajectory at 65%, so the 14x casualty cap does not apply.

Stress test (dossier, adjusted column anchored to actual FCF of £0.23B, E010/E032): - Base: £0.43B revenue, £0.23B FCF, 53.2% margin (E033). - −10%: £0.19B (E034). - −20%: £0.16B at 47.6% (E035). - −30%: £0.13B at 43.6% (E036).

Adjusted FCF turns negative only at about a 75% revenue decline (E037). The company survives Burry's 20% test easily. At −20%, the FCF yield on the current price falls to roughly 4.5%, below the hurdle.

2. Bull-Bear Balance

Where any forward constraint on fees could come from. Every channel is named with its source or marked as judgment: - Tribunal order. The dossier describes opt-out collective proceedings… seeking damages (E124), status "Proposed", with a disclosure hearing on 16 July 2026 (E126). No dossier line mentions an injunction, a remedy, a pricing undertaking or a price cap. I therefore assign no weight to a tribunal-set toll. My outside-dossier understanding is that UK collective proceedings are damages actions; that is unverified here and not relied on. - Settlement terms. A settlement could carry conduct terms. JUDGMENT; nothing in the dossier. - Regulator. A 2025 petition for a CMA investigation "gathered thousands of signatures" (E127), and the claim is led by a former CMA panel member (E124). This is a dossier-sourced possibility; no investigation is recorded. - Deterrence and agent pushback. A liability finding on past fees would make future above-inflation rises hazardous; that is JUDGMENT. The pushback is already documented: 250+ supporting agencies (E125), an open letter after a "12% Price Hike" (E129), agents who "cut staff hours" to pay, and fees outpacing commissions for a third year (E130, E133). Fees rose £93/month (+6.5%) in a year when house prices rose 1.3% (E131), and advertising costs rose twice as much as the ~£50 per-property commission gain (E132). This is Burry's "political pressure" channel, and the best-sourced of the four.

Scenario B in §5 is built on the soft channels (deterrence, pushback, a possible CMA look), not on a tribunal order.

The bridge is plausible but not proven. It rests on my scenario weights, which are JUDGMENT, and it says the market at £4.81 charges more for Event A than I do.

6. Final Decision

Decision: WAIT. Fairly-to-modestly underpriced. The central value of £5.09 is about 6% above £4.81. Any single adverse judgment in the sweep (weights 45/35/20, 7% growth in A, a 16x exit) puts value at the price. For a live claim equal to 41% of market cap, a 6% cushion is not a margin of safety.

Moat Tribunal Result: Strong, with one exposed leg. - The network and brand legs are agreed durable by the Tribunal plus Cook, Futurist and Lynch. - The pricing leg is MODERATE for all five Tribunal members. I adjudicate Burry's SEVERE down to MODERATE for the moat, while pricing his equity-duration point in scenario C.

Buy Zone: £3.19–£4.58

  • Floor (£3.19): the pipeline's no-growth Graham Floor (E018), 10.4x owner EPS, a 9.7% owner yield.
  • Ceiling (£4.58): the central value less 10%. That is 14.9x owner EPS, the bottom of the Quality band.
  • Adjustments: no ceiling raise for hidden value, and no franchise floor, because criterion 4 fails.

Why would it be mispriced at £4.58? Fear. A headline ask of 41% of market cap, plus an AI narrative that pressured the shares after November's investment announcement [MEDIA per Online Marketplaces], while measured LLM traffic stays below 0.5% and the AI assistant logs 28M sessions (E137, E093).

Moat half-life. About 15 years for the audience moat: the Historian puts any LLM inflection 5–10 years out, followed by a 10–15-year migration arc. The pricing leg's half-life depends on the soft channels above: agent pushback, a possible CMA look, and deterrence from liability on past fees. The claim's own timetable is undisclosed (E126), so its resolution window of perhaps 2–4 years is JUDGMENT.

Position size: 0% at £4.81; up to 3% at or below £4.58 (Moderate conviction). Three things remain unresolved, and they are why the size stops at 3%: - The claim's outcome and any conduct terms that follow it. - The funding of the >£400M capital return. - The undisclosed base of the Strategic Growth Areas.

The certification step in the CAT process is outside-dossier knowledge; the dossier records only a proposed claim and a disclosure hearing. - Down to PASS: LLM referral share compounds for two straight quarters, or retention falls from its 10-year high while ARPA growth drops to 3% or less.

HISTORIAN OF DISRUPTION — RMV.L (Rightmove plc)

1. DEAD ANALOGUES (The Casualties)

Yell Group / Yellow Pages (UK), ~1998–2015. Dominant local-business-directory monopoly — for decades the default place an advertiser bought local reach, commanding near-100% share of print classified/directory ad spend in its category. Killed by free online search (Google) and online directories that unbundled "find a plumber" from a physical book. The decline was slow at first, then compounding: Yell was still profitable and paying dividends into the mid-2000s while digital took share point by point, then went through repeated debt-restructuring events and ultimately administration in the 2020s. Management's public stance in the early 2000s was that the company was "investing in digital" while the overwhelming majority of revenue and capital allocation stayed with the print cash cow — the textbook casualty pattern: optimize the legacy margin, treat digital as a side bet rather than the business. Full transition from dominance to irrelevance took roughly 10-15 years.

UK regional/local newspaper classifieds (Johnston Press, Trinity Mirror-era local titles), ~1999–2010. These titles held effective local monopolies on property, jobs, and car classified advertising for most of the 20th century — the exact "two-sided marketplace" structure RMV has today, just in print. This is the closest structural analogue to RMV's current position — a dominant vertical-search incumbent — except RMV was the disruptor last time, not the disrupted.

BlackBerry (Research In Motion), 2009–2013. Held roughly 50% of the US smartphone market at its 2009 peak on the strength of enterprise security and a keyboard-first workflow moat. Killed by the iPhone/Android app-ecosystem shift. Co-CEOs Lazaridis and Balsillie publicly dismissed touchscreen input as inferior for serious business use and doubled down on the keyboard/enterprise-security niche instead of building a competing app platform.
