Roche Holding as an investment

A durable franchise priced as though its 2026–29 patent cliff has already been crossed.

I would wait, only moderately reluctantly: Roche is not expensive, but Fr371.6011 is priced above what the council pays before its 2026–29 exclusivity calendar is survived. The risk is timing — Ocrevus, Xolair and Kadcyla all lose exclusivity before the replacement pipeline reports a franc of sales6. The paradox: a 39-year dividend-raising franchise with real diagnostics lock-in1 sits on an earnings line the gate calls under-argued. What you would own

Roche is two businesses on one earnings pool of 796 million shares and Genussscheine11. Pharmaceuticals (CHF 47.7B, 2025) sells medicine on dateable exclusivity — the top five drivers are 35–36% of sales16. Diagnostics (CHF 13.8B) sells consumables into labs that have validated equipment around them, the clearest switching cost here1. Pharma's is weaker: physicians already weigh Briumvi against Ocrevus6. You would own diagnostics lock-in wrapped around a pharma engine that must replace itself again by 2029.

The bull case and the counterargument

The bull case is a repeat pattern: Roche replaced its 2015–19 cliff (Avastin, Herceptin, MabThera) with Ocrevus, Hemlibra and Phesgo, and funds 19 new molecules by 20306. The counterargument is timing: a non-forecast example, 60% five-year erosion of Ocrevus's CHF 6.7B 2024 base at a 75% margin, costs about Fr3.1/share, 18% of owner EPS, in 2029–3112. Evidence cuts both ways: the top five drivers compound at double digits at constant currency1, but "pricing power" is volume and mix — Xolair's +27% is a new indication, Phesgo's +18% a switch off Perjeta, not a price rise6. The moat is weakest in Pharma, 78% repeat prescription, not contracted16.

How I would make the decision

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis
