The bull case and the counterargument

The council’s strongest bull case is that proprietary data could become indispensable to AI workflows while ratings retain institutional acceptance. MA ARR of $3.7B, growing 9%, and retention of 95% support continued demand.5 Neither proves customer returns or pricing power.

The counterargument is economic. The council’s simplified stress example takes a 20% revenue decline and an 80% company-wide decremental margin, removing about $1.0B of FCF but leaving approximately $1.9B; this is conservative illustrative arithmetic, not a forecast.2 Business survival would not protect the purchase multiple.

The filing attributes $38M of recurring growth jointly to pricing and monitored credits, without separating them.3 Research and data lines total $1,907M, or 24.7% of revenue.4 My interpretation: AI distribution could strengthen those products or weaken their customer interface; regulatory changes could also restrict data remuneration.

How I would make the decision

My interpretation: I would buy durable ratings demand and resilient analytics subscriptions when normalized cash generation could justify the purchase price.

Conditions that would support buying

  • Stable margins through ordinary issuance conditions.
  • Continued analytics renewals with disclosed pricing contribution.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Record issuance still dominates earnings.
  • AI adoption grows without customer-economics disclosure.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Broad removal of ratings from capital rules.
  • Persistent analytics retention deterioration or lost pricing control.
