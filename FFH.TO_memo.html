Fairfax Financial Holdings (FFH.TO) as an investment

A profitable underwriter priced almost exactly for what it is, not for what it might become.

I would WAIT, with moderate conviction: Fairfax is a sound insurer priced close to fair value, not a bargain and not overpriced1. The central risk is a USD 77.3 billion investment portfolio, roughly three times common shareholders' equity, that supplied about two-thirds of last year's profit and could just as easily take it away23. The paradox: every named underwriting unit is profitable and mostly organically grown4, yet the trailing earnings that make the stock look cheap are mostly stock-market marks, not underwriting skill5. What you would own

Fairfax underwrites and reinsures P&C risk through Brit and Ki (Lloyd's), Northbridge (Canada), Allied World, Gulf Insurance (Middle East, war-exposed), Fairfax Asia, Singapore Re and Bryte (South Africa)7. It also owns non-insurance businesses — restaurants, a winery, sporting goods, resorts — minor at 17.5% of operating income8, a focus distraction rather than a moat. The glue is insurance licenses across many jurisdictions and the USD 77.3 billion portfolio2, not consumer lock-in: commercial and reinsurance clients renew annually through brokers who can move the business9.
