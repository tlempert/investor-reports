Meta Platforms as an investment

A wide but contested advertising moat, priced as if the AI-capex bet had already paid off.

My view: WAIT, at 0%1. Meta's advertising franchise is excellent; the price is not. The paradox: Family of Apps held a 51.6% operating margin through the buildout2, while nearly $65B a year of AI capex above depreciation has not yet shown what it earns21. Decision: WAIT — 0% position at $751.66; I would buy between the $320 floor and the $527 ceiling and own nothing above it1.

What you would own

Two businesses, one ticker. Family of Apps sells advertising against 3.6 billion daily people across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger, on a self-serve auction with no long-term advertiser contracts3. Reality Labs sells AI hardware — Quest, Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta, a Neural Band — that lost $19.19B on $2.21B of revenue in FY20252. The switching cost runs one direction: an advertiser can move a budget in a week, but 73.6–80.3% of users cross-use the apps daily, so the audience side of the flywheel is habit, not contract12.

Cash flow and shareholder economics

The council's owner yield is 4.3% (TTM owner earnings $82.44B over a ~$1,935B market cap); the free-cash-flow yield is 2.1% (TTM FCF $40.98B) [4, 5; calculation]. They differ because owner earnings deducts only depreciation as a maintenance proxy, while FCF deducts the full $89.33B of TTM capex6 — the gap is almost exactly the growth capex you are being asked to trust.

The latest quarter

Quarter ended 2026-06-30: revenue $60.80B, up 28% year over year; capex of $30.12B consumed 94.5% of operating cash flow, leaving FCF of $1.75B, 3% of revenue6. My interpretation: this is the capex cycle at its most visible, not broken cash conversion — capex is discretionary, and $90.26B of cash covers $83.66B of borrowings even if the build paused7.

The bull case and the counterargument

The strongest bull case: a self-funded platform bet, Amazon/AWS-style, from a core that has not been starved. Price per ad rose 9–12% with TikTok competing2; Reality Labs' loss growth has decelerated every year since 2022, from 35% to 8%9, and none of that opex is a locked lease2; Reels, buried inside "Advertising," runs near $50B annualized, about 20.6% of the total run rate10.

The counterargument is economic, not technological. TTM capex of $89.33B against roughly $24.6B of TTM depreciation leaves about $64.7B a year of net new capacity, near $25 a share21. At the 9.9% hurdle, that tranche must earn about $6.4B after tax every year, forever, to justify its cost; at zero return, three years destroys roughly $75 a share; at exactly the hurdle, it is worth what it cost21. Disclaimer: this is a single-tranche perpetuity on one year's build, not a full model.

Both sides accept the 51.6% core margin2; they disagree whether the gap between owner earnings and free cash flow is investment or cost4. Where the moat could weaken: EU DMA Article 7 interoperability, enacted, and the EU's "pay or consent" finding, still proposed, both target the cross-app data pooling the ad-targeting flywheel depends on12.

How I would make the decision

I would buy once the price separates from the AI-capex bet it carries — at or below $527, paying for the bridge that exists, not the one under construction.

Conditions that would support buying

  • Price falls into the $320–$527 range1.
  • FCF/revenue recovers toward its pre-2026 ~20%+, with a flat-or-lower 2027 capex guide14.
  • TTM ROIC holds near 20% as depreciation ramps4.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Price stays above $527 while the depreciation wave has not cleared the income statement21.
  • Owner-EPS growth keeps running below the 13.6%–20.3% the cross-check needs20.
  • Advertiser-ROI research stays inconclusive on the last-click concern below22.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Family of Apps margin breaks below its 47.3%–53.7% band2.
  • DMA interoperability or "pay or consent" forces open the data pooling the flywheel depends on12.
  • The FTC's appeal reopens a forced-divestiture outcome12.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Share count fell from 2,815M (2021) to 2,521M (2025) even as stock compensation rose to $20.43B, ~10.2% of revenue11; buybacks ran $26.25B in FY202511. Repurchases outrun dilution on a same-year basis — a genuine cannibal, not an illusion. Warning: pre-2025 buyback prices are estimated from share-count deltas, and the FY2025 estimate is physically impossible and was discarded11 — the case rests on share count, not price paid.

Price discipline without false precision

Buy zone $320–$527. The floor is 10x owner EPS, fixed because the Franchise Premium was denied on contract grounds — ads sell without long-term commitments3 — not on product judgment; it is the Graham-floor clause, not a penalty for a weak verdict. The ceiling is a 15% margin of safety below $6201. Both bounds are honest only to their stated width: the revenue sweep alone moves the base case from $531 to $755, so read $320–$527 as a range, not a hidden-decimal point18.
