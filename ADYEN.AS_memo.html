Adyen N.V. as an investment

A durable payments franchise, priced as if its hardest growth is already guaranteed.

I would wait, with Moderate conviction. Adyen is an unlevered, high-quality payments platform, but at €999.99 the price assumes ~17% growth delivered in full and a premium multiple paid for it five years out1. The risk isn't that the business breaks — a fifth of the earnings I'd pay for is rate-sensitive interest on merchant float, not fee income2, and the switching-cost story protecting today's enterprise book hasn't been tested on the smaller merchants a rival is onboarding instead3. The paradox: the same year volume growth fell from 33% to 8%, net revenue held at 18–19% and margins expanded4 — the best evidence of a moat and of unproven pricing power are the same facts. Decision: WAIT. Buy zone €593–€740, against a central value of €838, 19% below today's price5.

What you would own

Adyen is a single integrated payments platform — authorization, risk, issuing and acquiring — on infrastructure it owns rather than licenses6. It sells a payment rail merchants route more volume through over time, so switching means re-platforming settlement and fraud rules. Two 2026 acquisitions, Talon.One (loyalty) and Orb (usage billing), extend it into adjacent workflows7. The switching cost shows in the data: merchant share of wallet rises from under 20% in years three to seven to over 40% after year twelve3, while the top ten customers' revenue share fell from 53% (2017) to 11% (2025), no customer above 10%8 — diversifying even as each merchant sends more volume through it.

The bull case and the counterargument

The council splits 1 BUY, 1 WAIT, 4 HOLD, 6 PASS, no SELL15; its moat verdict is Uncertain — real switching costs at the core, pricing power unproven16. Eleven of twelve still build the bull case: growth converts to cash via rising share of wallet, falling concentration, margin expansion, and a debt-free balance sheet15. The dissent: "pricing power remains unproven," and a recession wouldn't clearly make the business stronger16 — an economic objection, not technological. FY2025 net income rose 14% to €1.06B while free cash flow fell 44% to €0.90B9. My interpretation: most of that gap is merchant float reverting, not deteriorating economics, though a second year of the 69% receivables jump against 18% revenue growth would undercut it17. The share-of-wallet curve is one company-sourced statistic nearly every member cites, not independent corroboration3. The moat could weaken at the entry point: a rival's Shopify integration is "basically maintenance-free" against a documented three-week Adyen build, and Adyen "generally refuses to underwrite smaller merchants directly"18. My interpretation: switching costs likely run ~12 years at the core, 5–7 years at the SMB edge, where that rival is already live19.

How I would make the decision

Purchase thesis: a good business priced for a narrow set of good outcomes, so the entry price must supply the margin of safety no dividend does13. Five council members size a non-zero starter stake today (4%, 2%, 2%, 2–3%, 1–2%)24; a starter stake doesn't fix a return below the cost of equity.

Conditions that would support buying

  • Price falls into €593–€7405.
  • FY2026 net-revenue growth confirms 21–23%14.
  • A finance-income line sizes the rate-sensitive share at or below 20%2.

Conditions that would support waiting

  • Price stays above the ceiling while required growth exceeds the base case22.
  • The FY2025 volume slowdown stays unexplained beyond one data point25.
  • Cash conversion hasn't returned toward 86–87%26.

Conditions that would invalidate the thesis

  • Volume and net-revenue growth decelerate together for two straight quarters.
  • A take-rate disclosure shows pricing power eroding at the enterprise core.
  • A second year of receivables outgrowing revenue by the FY2025 margin.

Capital allocation deserves its own test

Share count rose 31M→32M, 2023–2025, no dividend, no buyback27; stock-based pay held at 1.5% of revenue vs a 21.9% peer median28 — cheap dilution, but dilution. My interpretation: reinvestment discipline, not neglect, though only a three-year window.

Price discipline without false precision

Below €740 you pay ~22x trailing earnings for ~17% growth5; below €593, the bear case's own break-even20. The finance-income sweep alone moves the central value €766–9832 — treat the zone as a band, not a line.
